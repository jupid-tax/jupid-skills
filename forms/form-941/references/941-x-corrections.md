# Form 941-X Corrections

How to correct errors on a previously-filed Form 941 using Form 941-X (Adjusted Employer's Quarterly Federal Tax Return or Claim for Refund), verified against Form 941-X (Rev. April 2026) and its instructions (Rev. April 2026). Re-check the next revision at https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-941-x.

Authority: IRC §6205 (interest-free adjustments), §6402 (refunds), §6511 (statute of limitations); IRS Reg. §31.6205-1, §31.6402(a)-1; Instructions for Form 941-X.

---

## When to file 941-X

File **one Form 941-X per quarter being corrected**. Don't combine multiple quarters on one 941-X.

Trigger | What happened | What to file
---|---|---
Under-reported tax | Filed 941 reported less than actual liability (e.g., missed wages, missed FICA, administrative withholding error) | 941-X with the additional tax (interest-free if filed and paid on time; see below)
Over-reported tax | Filed 941 reported more than actual liability (e.g., included contractor wages by mistake, double-counted) | 941-X claiming refund OR abatement
Misclassified worker | Treated as 1099 contractor but is actually a W-2 employee → wages need to be added | 941-X for each affected quarter
ERC claim withdrawal | Claimed Employee Retention Credit on prior 941 but no longer believe eligible | Use the IRS ERC withdrawal process or a 941-X (see Scenario 4). New ERC claims for Q3/Q4 2021 filed after January 31, 2024 can't be allowed or refunded (P.L. 119-21 §70605(d); IRS Fact Sheet 2025-07)
Section 3121(q) Notice | IRS issued tip-tax notice for prior periods | 941-X to add Line 5f tax

---

## When NOT to file 941-X

- **Original return wasn't filed** → file the original 941, not 941-X
- **Wrong EIN used** → don't file 941-X; instead, contact IRS to move the wages to the correct EIN, then file an original 941 under the right EIN
- **Math error or mistake the IRS already corrected via CP notice** → no need; the IRS already fixed it
- **Refund claim past the period of limitations** → 3 years from the date Form 941 was filed OR 2 years from the date the tax was paid, whichever is later (IRC §6511); too late, claim denied

---

## Statute of limitations

Two parallel rules:

1. **Overreported tax (refund/credit)**: 3 years from the **date the original 941 was filed** OR 2 years from the **date the tax was paid**, whichever is later (IRC §6511(a)).
   - Forms 941 for a calendar year filed before April 15 of the next year are treated as filed on April 15 of that next year (IRC §6513(c)(1) for refunds; §6501(b)(2) for assessment). Example: every 2026 Form 941 counts as filed April 15, 2027, so it can be corrected until April 15, 2030.
2. **Underreported tax**: file 941-X within 3 years of the date Form 941 was filed (IRC §6501(a); Instructions for Form 941-X, "Is There a Deadline for Filing Form 941-X?"). Fraud has no limit; a substantial omission extends assessment to 6 years.

For the employer self-correcting under-reported tax, file 941-X within that window or the IRS will eventually pick it up via W-2 / 1099 cross-matching.

For the employer claiming a refund, file before the §6511 window closes.

---

## Adjustment vs. claim — picking the path

941-X Part 1 has **two lines**; check exactly one:

- **Line 1 (Adjusted employment tax return)**: For under-reported tax (you owe more), OR over-reported tax you want applied as a credit to the Form 941 for the period in which you file the 941-X. Required if the form corrects both under- and over-reported amounts.
- **Line 2 (Claim)**: For over-reported tax only, when you want a refund OR abatement. Can't be used if the form corrects ANY under-reported amount.

### Filing deadlines

| Path | Deadline |
|------|----------|
| Line 1 — under-reported, paying with 941-X | By the due date of Form 941 for the quarter in which you discover the error (for interest-free treatment); within 3 years of the original filing at the latest |
| Line 1 — over-reported, applying overpayment | Soon after discovery, and more than 90 days before the period of limitations on credit or refund expires. In the last 90 days, only the claim process (Line 2) is allowed |
| Line 2 — refund or abatement | Any time before the §6511 period of limitations expires (3 years from filing or 2 years from payment) |

### Interest-free adjustment

Line 1 under-reported corrections qualify for interest-free treatment if the employer files the 941-X by the due date of Form 941 for the quarter in which the error was discovered, pays the Line 27 amount when filing, enters the discovery date, and explains the correction (IRC §6205, Reg. §31.6205-1). Not available if the issue was raised in an examination of a prior period, the underreporting was knowing, or the employer received a notice and demand or a §7436 determination notice (Instructions for Form 941-X, Rev. April 2026). A late 941-X needs an amended Schedule B if the employer is a semi-weekly depositor.

## Required certifications (Part 2)

Part 2 of Form 941-X (Rev. April 2026):

### Line 3 — Forms W-2 / W-2c

Always check: "I certify that I've filed or will file Forms W-2 or Forms W-2c, as required." If correcting under-reported amounts only, skip lines 4 and 5.

### Line 4 — Adjustment process (Part 1, line 1) for over-reported amounts — check all that apply

- **4a** — Repaid or reimbursed each affected employee for over-collected federal income tax or Additional Medicare Tax for the current year and over-collected Social Security and Medicare tax for current and prior years; for prior-year Social Security/Medicare, holds a written statement from each employee that they haven't claimed and won't claim a refund
- **4b** — The Social Security and Medicare adjustment is for the employer's share only (couldn't find the employees or didn't get the statements)
- **4c** — The adjustment is for tax that wasn't withheld from employee wages

### Line 5 — Claim process (Part 1, line 2) for over-reported amounts — check all that apply

- **5a** — Repaid or reimbursed each affected employee for over-collected Social Security and Medicare tax (plus written statements for prior years)
- **5b** — Holds written consent from each affected employee to claim the employee share (plus written statements for prior years)
- **5c** — Claim is for the employer's share only
- **5d** — Claim is for tax that wasn't withheld from employee wages

The certification matters because the IRS will not refund the employee FICA portion to the employer if the employees can still claim it themselves (avoiding double recovery).

**Federal income tax withholding**: generally correctable only if the error was discovered in the same calendar year the wages were paid (and, for an over-collection, repaid in that year). For prior years, only administrative errors (the 941 shows a different amount than was actually withheld) and §3509 corrections can be made (Instructions for Form 941-X, line 7). Once the calendar year closes, the employee recovers over-withheld income tax on their own Form 1040.

## Mechanics — filling the form

Form 941-X Part 3 uses its own line numbers (line 6 = Form 941 line 2, line 7 = line 3, line 8 = line 5a, line 11 = line 5b, line 12 = line 5c, line 13 = line 5d, line 14 = line 5f, line 15 = lines 7–9, line 16 = line 11, lines 19–22 = §3509 special additions to wages) with four columns:

| Column | What it is |
|--------|-----------|
| Column 1 | Total corrected amount (what should have been on the original 941) |
| Column 2 | Amount originally reported on the filed 941 |
| Column 3 | Difference (Column 1 − Column 2) |
| Column 4 | Tax adjustment (calculated from Column 3 using applicable rate) |

Net adjustment is the sum of all Column 4 amounts.

### Lines 23 and 27 — Subtotal and total

```
Line 23 = sum of Column 4 on lines 7–22
Line 27 = Lines 23 through 26c of Column 4 (positive = under-reported, owe more; negative = over-reported, credit or refund)
```

### Detailed explanation (Part 4, lines 41–43)

The IRS requires a written explanation of every correction:

- What was wrong on the original return
- How it was discovered
- The corrected facts
- How each line on 941-X was computed

Be specific. "Discovered $5,000 of bonus wages paid Dec 28, 2025 was inadvertently posted to January 2026 payroll, omitting from Q4 2025 941. Adding to Line 2 corrected amount, recomputing FICA and FIT withheld accordingly." Vague explanations get flagged for examination.

## Common correction scenarios

### Scenario 1 — Missed wages

User filed Q2 2025 941 in July 2025. In October 2025, user discovers a $4,000 commission paid to a salesperson on June 28 was not included.

- Part 1, line 1 (adjustment), no refund
- Line 6: wages +$4,000. Line 8 (SS wages) +$4,000 × 0.124 = $496; Line 12 (Medicare wages) +$4,000 × 0.029 = $116
- Line 7 (FIT): +$880 at the 22% supplemental rate; a federal income tax correction is allowed here because the error was discovered in the same calendar year the wages were paid (Instructions for Form 941-X, line 7)
- Line 27 total: $1,492 owed ($612 FICA + $880 FIT)
- Pay with the 941-X
- Interest-free if filed and paid within statute and before IRS examination

### Scenario 2 — Over-reported FICA (employer only)

User over-paid $1,200 of employer FICA in Q1 2025 due to including pretax §125 in error.

- Part 1, line 2 (claim), refund
- Line 5c if the user claims only the employer share (employees can't be found or haven't consented)
- Or 5a / 5b if the user repaid the employees or holds their written consents (then the employer can claim both shares)

### Scenario 3 — Misclassified contractor

User paid a "contractor" $30,000 across Q1 + Q2 2025 but in November 2025 IRS reclassifies them as an employee. User must file 941-X for Q1 and Q2 separately.

- File 2 separate 941-Xs (one per quarter)
- For each: correct line 6 (wages) and enter the reclassified wages on lines 19–21 (special additions to wages) with the §3509 rates, check line 42, and explain on line 43
- §3509 rates apply whether the IRS or the employer made the reclassification, unless the employer intentionally disregarded the withholding requirements or withheld income tax but not FICA. If the required information returns (Form 1099-NEC) were filed: 1.5% income tax, 7.44% Social Security (6.2% employer + 20% of 6.2%), 1.74% Medicare. If not filed: 3.0%, 8.68%, 2.03% (Instructions for Form 941-X, lines 19–22)
- File W-2s for the employee retroactively (W-2c if W-2 already issued)

### Scenario 4 — Withdrawing an ERC claim

User filed 941-X claiming Employee Retention Credit but now believes the claim was wrong (overly-aggressive ERC mill). IRS provides withdrawal procedures at https://www.irs.gov/coronavirus/withdraw-an-employee-retention-credit-erc-claim.

- Different procedure from regular 941-X
- Use the withdrawal program if eligible (claim not yet processed, or check not cashed)
- If past withdrawal window: file new 941-X reversing the prior 941-X
- Note: P.L. 119-21 §70605(d) bars the IRS from allowing or refunding ERC claims for Q3/Q4 2021 filed after January 31, 2024 (IRS Fact Sheet 2025-07)

## Filing 941-X

### E-file or paper

Form 941-X can be filed electronically through Modernized e-File (MeF), and the IRS encourages it (Instructions for Form 941-X, Rev. April 2026; IRS.gov/EmploymentEfile). Paper addresses from the same instructions ("Where Should You File Form 941-X?"):

- **CT, DE, DC, FL, GA, IL, IN, KY, ME, MD, MA, MI, NH, NJ, NY, NC, OH, PA, RI, SC, TN, VT, VA, WV, WI**: Department of the Treasury, Internal Revenue Service, Cincinnati, OH 45999-0005
- **AL, AK, AZ, AR, CA, CO, HI, ID, IA, KS, LA, MN, MS, MO, MT, NE, NV, NM, ND, OK, OR, SD, TX, UT, WA, WY**: Department of the Treasury, Internal Revenue Service, Ogden, UT 84201-0005
- **No legal residence or principal place of business in any state**: Internal Revenue Service, P.O. Box 409101, Ogden, UT 84409
- **Exempt organizations; federal, state, local, and Indian tribal governmental entities**: Department of the Treasury, Internal Revenue Service, Ogden, UT 84201-0005

Pay any balance electronically. Verify addresses in the current i941x before mailing — they shift between revisions.

### Required attachments

- **Form 8974** if the correction affects R&D payroll credit
- **Detailed explanation** in Part 4, line 43 (mandatory)
- **Amended Schedule B** if a semi-weekly depositor files the 941-X late

### After filing

- IRS processing: 6+ months for 941-X (longer than 941; especially backlogged for ERC corrections)
- IRS may send Letter 105C / 106C if claim is denied
- Refund is issued (claim process), or the credit is applied to the Form 941 for the quarter in which the 941-X was filed (adjustment process; reported on that return's Line 13)

## Common 941-X mistakes

1. **Combining multiple quarters on one 941-X.** Each 941-X covers exactly one quarter.
2. **Picking Box 1 (adjustment) when the user actually wants a refund check.** Box 2 is required for refund.
3. **Forgetting the certifications in Part 2.** Missing certs = automatic rejection.
4. **Vague Part 4 explanation.** "Math error" is not enough; explain the source of the error.
5. **Filing past the period of limitations.** §6511 cuts off refund claims at 3 years from filing (Forms 941 count as filed April 15 of the following year) or 2 years from payment.
6. **Not filing W-2c when changing employee wages.** If 941-X changes Line 2 for a specific employee, W-2c is also required (and the employee may need to file 1040-X).
7. **Using the adjustment process in the last 90 days of the period of limitations.** Over-reported corrections filed then must use the claim process (Part 1, line 2).
8. **Failing to repay employees before claiming over-withheld FICA.** Question 4 cert lies = perjury exposure.
