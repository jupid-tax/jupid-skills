# Qualification Tests for the Home Office Deduction

Who may claim Form 8829 at all, and for which square footage. Run these tests before any arithmetic. Every test depends on facts only the user knows; ask, do not assume. Sources: 2025 Instructions for Form 8829 ("Who Can Deduct Expenses for Business Use of a Home", "Principal Place of Business", "Storage of Inventory or Product Samples", "Daycare Facilities"); Pub. 587 (2025), "Qualifying for a Deduction" and "Daycare Facility"; IRC §280A(c).

## Gate 0 — Is the filer allowed to use Form 8829?

| Situation | Result | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietor or single-member LLC filing Schedule C | Form 8829 (or the simplified method) | Form 8829 header: "File only with Schedule C (Form 1040)" |
| W-2 employee working from home for an employer | No deduction on the personal return. Stop | i8829: "You have expenses for business use of your home as an employee. You can't deduct these expenses on your personal return." Unreimbursed employee expenses are miscellaneous itemized deductions, disallowed for tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2017 by IRC §67(h), made permanent by P.L. 119-21 §70110 (July 4, 2025). Pub. 587 Figure A: "Are you using the part of your home as an employee?" Yes → no deduction |
| Partner claiming actual home expenses | Not Form 8829. Pub. 587 worksheet; partner reporting goes through Schedule E | i8829 "Who cannot use Form 8829" |
| Schedule F farmer | Not Form 8829. Pub. 587 worksheet | i8829 |
| Both a W-2 job and a Schedule C business use the same room | Not settled by the Form 8829 instructions. Pub. 587 allows one office to serve more than one trade or business but tests each separately, and the employee use itself is never deductible. Do not compute a deduction; describe the facts in the draft and tell the user a CPA must decide whether the Schedule C use qualifies | Pub. 587, More Than One Trade or Business; i8829 "Who cannot use Form 8829" |
| All home expenses allocable to inventory | Schedule C, Part III, not Form 8829 | i8829 |
| Simplified method elected for this home for the year | No Form 8829 for this home | i8829; Pub. 587, Electing the Simplified Method |
| Investing in one's own portfolio from a home office | No deduction: not a trade or business | Pub. 587, Trade or Business Use, example |

Ask: "Do you work from home as an employee of someone else, as a self-employed person filing Schedule C, as a partner, or as a farmer?" If more than one, ask which activity uses the space.

## Gate 1 — Regular and exclusive use

**Exclusive**: a specific, separately identifiable area used only for the trade or business. A permanent partition is not required. Any personal use of the area fails the test (Pub. 587 example: a den used for legal work and family recreation does not qualify).

**Regular**: used for business on a regular basis. "Incidental or occasional business use is not regular use." Facts and circumstances.

Questions to ask (one at a time if the user is unsure):
1. "Is the space a separate room, or a defined area of a room?"
2. "Does anyone use that area for anything personal: guests, homework, TV, storage of personal items, exercise?"
3. "How often do you work there: every workday, a few days a week, occasionally?"

If the answer to question 2 is anything but "no," stop: the area does not qualify unless an exception below applies. Do not shrink the footage to a "business part" of a mixed-use area on your own; ask whether a defined sub-area is used only for business, and measure only that.

**Exceptions to exclusivity** (Pub. 587, Exceptions to Exclusive Use; the only two):
- Storage of inventory or product samples.
- Daycare facility.

## Gate 2 — One of the qualifying uses

The exclusively-and-regularly-used area must also be at least one of:

### A. Principal place of business

Two factors: relative importance of activities at each location, and time spent at each. A home office qualifies as the principal place of business if:
- It is used exclusively and regularly for **administrative or management activities** of the business (billing, books and records, ordering supplies, setting appointments, forwarding orders or writing reports), **and**
- There is **no other fixed location** where the filer conducts **substantial** administrative or management activities of that business.

These do not disqualify the home office (i8829 and Pub. 587):
- Others do the admin work elsewhere (an outside billing service).
- Admin work done at non-fixed locations (a car, a hotel room).
- Occasional minimal admin work at a fixed location outside the home.
- Substantial non-admin work (serving clients) at a fixed location outside the home.
- Suitable admin space exists elsewhere but the filer chooses the home office.

Pub. 587 examples to cite when the user's facts match: Sid the plumber (works at customer sites, books at home → qualifies); Alex the sales rep (orders written in hotel rooms occasionally → qualifies); Taylor the anesthesiologist (hospital offers a rarely used shared office → home office still qualifies).

### B. Place to meet patients, clients, or customers

The filer physically meets them on the premises, and their use is substantial and integral to the business. Occasional meetings and phone calls do not qualify. This area need not be the principal place of business (Pub. 587 example: attorney with a rented office three days and a home office used to meet clients two days → qualifies).

### C. Separate structure

A free-standing structure not attached to the home (studio, workshop, garage, barn, greenhouse) used exclusively and regularly in connection with the business. It need not be the principal place of business or a meeting place.

### D. Storage of inventory or product samples (no exclusivity required)

All must be true (Pub. 587):
- The business sells products at wholesale or retail.
- Inventory or samples are kept at home for use in the business.
- The home is the **only fixed location** of the business.
- The storage space is used on a regular basis.
- The space is separately identifiable and suitable for storage.

### E. Daycare facility (no exclusivity required)

- Trade or business of providing daycare for children, persons age 65 or older, or persons physically or mentally unable to care for themselves.
- Has applied for (not rejected), been granted (still in effect), or is exempt from a license, certification, registration, or approval as a daycare center or family or group daycare home under state law. Revoked or rejected → no exception.

Ask for the license or registration status in those words. Do not proceed on "I watch the neighbor's kids."

## Daycare time-use computation

When the daycare area is used regularly but not exclusively:

```
Line 3  = daycare area ÷ total home area                    (percentage)
Line 4  = total hours the area was used for daycare in the year
Line 5  = 8,760, or 24 × days the home was available for daycare
          if daycare started or stopped during the year
Line 6  = line 4 ÷ line 5                                   (decimal)
Line 7  = line 6 × line 3                                   (percentage)
```

Rules from Pub. 587:
- A room available for daycare throughout each business day and regularly used in the business counts as used for daycare throughout each business day; hour-by-hour records are not required for that room. Occasional personal use is allowed. A room used only occasionally for daycare does not qualify.
- Direct expenses for a daycare area not used exclusively: multiply by line 6 and enter in column (a) (Pub. 587 Example 1: $500 basement painting × 34.25% = $171).
- Indirect expenses: column (b), multiplied by line 7 on line 24.
- Food for daycare recipients is not a home expense. It goes on Schedule C (100% for recipients; generally 50% for food consumed by employees; never food for the provider's family). The standard meal and snack rates in Pub. 587 Table 3 are an optional method for eligible children.
- Rooms used exclusively plus rooms used partly: use the three-step special computation in the Form 8829 instructions (Part I) and attach a statement.

Ask: "Which rooms are used for daycare? Are any used only for daycare? What are your operating hours and days? Did you start or stop providing daycare during the year?"

## More than one business, more than one home, shared homes

- One office can be the principal place of business for two businesses; test each business separately. Any non-business activity in the space disqualifies it (Pub. 587, More Than One Trade or Business).
- One Form 8829 per home. Line 36 is allocated among businesses that use the same home by a reasonable method, and each Schedule C line 30 gets its share (i8829, Line 36).
- Moved during the year: each home gets its own computation for its period; the simplified method can be used for only one of the homes (i8829; Pub. 587, More than one home).
- Part-year use: only expenses for the part of the year the home was used for business count (Pub. 587, Part-year use).

## Rental use of part of the home

Renting part of the home (for example, a room rented to a tenant) is a rental activity reported on Schedule E, figured with actual expenses only; the simplified method does not apply to rental use (Pub. 587, Rental use). Do not include rented space on line 1. Route to [../../schedule-e/SKILL.md](../../schedule-e/SKILL.md).

## Recordkeeping the agent should ask about

Pub. 587, Recordkeeping: records must show the part of the home used, that the use is exclusive and regular for a qualifying purpose (or within an exception), and the depreciation and expenses for the business part. Keep them as long as they may matter, usually the later of 3 years from the return due date or filing date, or 2 years after the tax was paid. Keep basis records (purchase, improvements, depreciation) until the home is sold. Ask whether the user has a floor plan or measurements, photos of the space, and the prior-year Form 8829.
