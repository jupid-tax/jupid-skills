# Form 3520 vs Form 3520-A — Coordination

The two forms are siblings; many filers conflate them. This reference
clarifies who files which, when, and how the two forms must reference
each other.

## Form 3520 — filed by the US person

The Form 3520 covered by this skill is filed **by the US person**:

- US grantor / transferor (Part I)
- US owner of a foreign trust (Part II)
- US beneficiary receiving distribution (Part III)
- US recipient of foreign gifts (Part IV)

Due the 15th day of the 4th month after the US person's tax year ends
(April 15 for calendar-year individuals; June 15 for U.S. citizens and
residents living and working abroad, with a line 1j statement); if the
income tax return was extended, the 15th day of the 10th month (October
15), with line 1k completed (Instructions for Form 3520, Rev. December
2025).

Mailed to: Internal Revenue Service Center, P.O. Box 409101, Ogden, UT
84409.

## Form 3520-A — filed BY the foreign trust

Form 3520-A is the **annual information return of a foreign trust with at
least one US owner**. The trust files it (or its US agent does) — not the
US owner. It reports:

- Trust income, expenses, distributions for the year
- Trust assets and liabilities
- All US owners and their respective ownership percentages
- All US beneficiaries who received distributions

Due date: **March 15** (15th day of 3rd month after end of trust's tax
year). For calendar-year trusts, March 15.

Extension: **Form 7004**, filed using the foreign trust's EIN by the 15th
day of the 3rd month after the trust's year ends. An extension of the
owner's income tax return does NOT extend Form 3520-A (Instructions for
Form 3520-A, Rev. December 2025).

Mailed to: Internal Revenue Service Center, P.O. Box 409101, Ogden, UT
84409 (same address as Form 3520).

## Why this matters for the US owner

A US owner files Form 3520 Part II to report their ownership of the
foreign trust. **Part II requires** that Form 3520-A is also filed for
the same year. The trust must:

1. File its own 3520-A by March 15 (with Form 7004 extension if needed),
   AND
2. Provide the **Foreign Grantor Trust Owner Statement** (pages 3 and 4
   of Form 3520-A) to the US owner so they can attach it to their Form
   3520, and the Foreign Grantor Trust Beneficiary Statement (page 5) to
   U.S. beneficiaries

If the trust doesn't file its 3520-A — which is common, because foreign
trustees have no incentive to file with the IRS — the US owner must file
a **substitute Form 3520-A** as an attachment to their Form 3520 Part II.

## Substitute Form 3520-A

A "substitute 3520-A" is:

- A Form 3520-A completed to the best of the owner's ability with the
  data the trust would have reported, including the Owner Statement
  (pages 3–4) and the Beneficiary Statement (page 5)
- With the "**Substitute Form 3520-A**" checkbox at the top checked
- Signed by the **US owner** (not the trustee), with the owner's name and
  TIN on the "Title" line of the signature box
- Attached to the US owner's Form 3520 (Part II, line 22 "No")
- Copies of the Owner and Beneficiary Statements furnished to U.S. owners
  and U.S. beneficiaries by the Form 3520 due date

The substitute is due with Form 3520 (e.g., April 15 for individuals),
not by the trust's March 15 date; a substitute attached to a timely Form
3520 is considered timely and avoids the penalty for the trust's failure
to file (Instructions for Form 3520, Penalties; Instructions for Form
3520-A).

### Inputs needed for a substitute 3520-A

The US owner must obtain or estimate:

- Trust EIN (apply for one if the trust has none — Form SS-4)
- Beginning-of-year and end-of-year FMV of trust assets
- Trust income by category (ordinary, capital gains, foreign tax credit
  available, etc.)
- Trust expenses
- Distributions to all beneficiaries (not just the US owner)
- Identification of all beneficiaries who received distributions

If the user doesn't have this data because the trustee won't provide it,
the user has a real problem: filing the substitute requires the data, and
not filing exposes them to §6677 penalty.

Complete the substitute "to the best of your ability" (Form 3520, line
22); at a minimum, line 23 includes the value of all assets the owner
transferred. Document the requests to the trustee (emails, certified
letters). Note the instructions' limit on reasonable cause: reluctance of
a foreign fiduciary, or trust provisions that prevent disclosure, are not
reasonable cause (IRC §6677(d)). Form 8082 can notify the IRS that no
Owner Statement was received, but it does not relieve §6677 penalties.

## When neither 3520 nor 3520-A is filed

The penalty stack can be brutal:

- **§6677(a)** — the greater of $10,000 or 35% of the gross reportable
  amount for failure to report a transfer (Part I) or a distribution
  (Part III); if the failure continues more than 90 days after the IRS
  mails a notice, an additional $10,000 per 30-day period (or part); the
  total is capped at the gross reportable amount
- **§6677(b)** — the greater of $10,000 or 5% of the gross value of the
  portion of trust assets treated as owned by the US person, for the
  trust's failure to file Form 3520-A (charged to the US owner)
- These penalties stack — both can apply in the same year

Illustration: a US person who transferred $500,000 to a foreign trust and
reported neither the transfer (Part I) nor the trust (Form 3520-A or
substitute), with the trust still worth $500,000 at year-end, could face:

- $175,000 under §6677(a) (35% of $500,000)
- $25,000 under §6677(b) (5% of $500,000; more than $10,000)
- = $200,000 before any continuation penalties, vs. zero tax liability

The defense is reasonable cause under §6677(d), which requires an
affirmative showing; whether first-time abatement can apply is a question
for the practitioner (this skill does not rely on it).

## Joint filings — when one form serves both purposes

The IRS does NOT allow Form 3520 and Form 3520-A to be combined into one
filing. They are separate forms, separately due, separately mailed.

What CAN be combined:
- A single Form 3520 with multiple Parts (e.g., Part I + Part II)
- A single Form 3520 with substitute 3520-A attached
- A joint Form 3520 by spouses filing a joint income tax return when
  both are transferors, grantors, or beneficiaries of the same foreign
  trust (line 1i; Instructions, Joint Returns)

What CANNOT be combined:
- Form 3520 and Form 3520-A (always separate)
- Form 3520 reporting two different trusts (file one Form 3520 per trust)

## Practical workflow

For a US person who is a grantor/owner of a foreign trust (the most
complex case):

1. **March 1** — Send a request to the trustee for Form 3520-A data and
   a Foreign Grantor Trust Owner Statement
2. **March 15** — If trustee files 3520-A: receive the Owner Statement,
   keep for Form 3520 attachment. If trustee doesn't file or doesn't
   respond: the trust's Form 7004 (with the trust's EIN) is the trust's
   to file; plan to file a substitute 3520-A with the owner's Form 3520
3. **By April 15** (or extended deadline): file Form 3520 Part II with
   either:
   - Owner Statement attached (if trust filed 3520-A), or
   - Substitute 3520-A attached (if trust didn't file)
4. **Save proof of mailing** (USPS Certified Mail) and a complete copy of
   the package
