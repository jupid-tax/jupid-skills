# Personal Use, Vacation Homes, and IRC §280A

Load this file whenever a Schedule E rental property is a dwelling unit that anyone used for personal purposes during the year, or that was rented to family, or rented below market. Sources: 2025 Instructions for Schedule E, Line 2; Pub. 527 (2025), chapters 4 and 5 (including Worksheet 5-1); IRC §280A.

## 1. What counts as a dwelling unit

A house, apartment, condominium, mobile home, boat, vacation home, or similar property with basic living accommodations (sleeping space, toilet, cooking facilities), including structures belonging to it. A unit used solely as a hotel, motel, inn, or similar establishment (regularly available to paying customers and not used by an owner as a home during the year) is not a dwelling unit (Pub. 527 ch. 5).

## 2. Count the days: three buckets

Ask the user for a calendar of the year, unit by unit. Never estimate days.

**Fair rental days (Schedule E line 2, first column).** Days the unit was actually rented at a fair rental price. A fair rental price is generally what an unrelated person would pay; it is not fair if substantially below rents for similar properties in the area (same purpose, size, condition, furnishings, location) (Pub. 527 ch. 5).

**Personal use days (line 2, second column).** Any day, or part of a day, the unit was used by (Instructions, Line 2; Pub. 527 ch. 5):

1. You, or any other person who owns an interest in it, for personal purposes (unless rented to a co-owner as a main home under a shared equity financing agreement);
2. A member of your family, or of the family of another owner, unless that person rents it at a fair rental price as a main home. Family = spouse, siblings, half siblings, ancestors, lineal descendants;
3. Anyone under an arrangement that lets you use some other dwelling unit (a swap), even at a fair rent;
4. Anyone paying less than a fair rental price.

Donated use: if you donate use of the unit to a charity that sells it at a fundraiser, the purchaser's days are personal use days (Pub. 527 ch. 5).

**Days that are neither.**

- Days available for rent but not rented are not rental days (Pub. 527 ch. 5).
- A day you spent working substantially full time repairing and maintaining (not improving) the unit is not a personal use day, even if family members used it for recreation the same day (Instructions, Line 2; Pub. 527).
- Days you used the unit as your main home before or after renting it are not personal use days for the "used as a home" test if you rented or tried to rent it for 12 or more consecutive months (or for less than 12 months ending in a sale or exchange). This special rule does not apply when dividing expenses (Pub. 527 ch. 5).

**Overlap rule.** A day rented at a fair price on which you also used the unit counts as a rental day when dividing expenses, but as a personal use day (not a rental day) when applying the "used as a home" test (Pub. 527 ch. 5, beach cottage example).

## 3. Divide expenses (always, when there is any personal use)

Rental share = days rented at fair rental price ÷ total days of rental use plus personal use. Only the rental share goes on Schedule E. Example in the Instructions (Line 2 caution): 7 personal days and 63 rented days → 10% (7 ÷ 70) of expenses are not rental expenses. Pub. 527 Worksheet 5-1 lines A–F do the same calculation.

The personal share of mortgage interest, real estate taxes, and qualifying casualty losses may go on Schedule A if the user itemizes (route to `../../schedule-a/SKILL.md`). The personal share of insurance, utilities, repairs and depreciation is not deductible anywhere.

## 4. The "used as a home" test

You used a dwelling unit as a home during the year if personal use days exceeded the greater of (Instructions, Line 2; Pub. 527 ch. 5):

- 14 days, or
- 10% of the total days it was rented to others at a fair rental price.

Write the test out in the draft: `personal days = N; threshold = max(14, 0.10 × fair rental days) = T; used as home = N > T`.

## 5. Four outcomes

| Situation | Treatment | Where |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| No personal use | Ordinary rental. All income and expenses; passive and at-risk rules apply | Schedule E; see `passive-loss-and-at-risk.md` |
| Personal use, not used as a home | Report all rent; divide expenses by days; loss allowed subject to passive and at-risk rules | Schedule E |
| Used as a home, rented fewer than 15 days | Do not report the rent; deduct no rental expenses; mortgage interest, taxes, casualty losses on Schedule A if itemizing | Nothing on Schedule E (Instructions, Line 2; Pub. 527) |
| Used as a home, rented 15 days or more | Report all rent; divide expenses; if rental expenses (including depreciation) exceed rent, Worksheet 5-1 limits them and carries the excess forward. Not a passive activity, so no Form 8582 for this unit | Schedule E (Pub. 527 ch. 5; Instructions, Other activities) |

## 6. Worksheet 5-1 (Pub. 527): order of deduction when used as a home and at a loss

Use only if all three are "Yes": used as a home; rented at a fair price 15 days or more; rental expenses plus depreciation exceed rental income (Pub. 527).

Part I: A days available at fair price; B days available but not rented; C = A − B rental days; D personal days (including days rented below fair price); E = C + D; F = C ÷ E.

Part II, in this order:

1. Line 1: rents received.
2. Lines 2a–2d, fully deductible even if they create a loss: rental share of deductible home mortgage interest (2a), rental share of deductible real estate taxes (2b), rental share of deductible casualty losses (2c), and direct rental expenses (2d: interest on loans for the rental other than to buy, build or improve the unit, rental agency fees, advertising, office supplies, depreciation on office equipment). Line 2e = total.
   - Standard-deduction filers enter nothing on 2a and 2b; the rental share of mortgage interest moves to 4b and of real estate taxes to 4c.
   - Itemizers whose state and local taxes exceed $40,000 ($20,000 MFS) use the Line 2b Worksheet; the excess goes to 4c (Pub. 527, 2025).
3. Line 3 = line 1 − line 2e (not below zero).
4. Lines 4a–4d: rental share of operating expenses (repairs, insurance, utilities), excess mortgage interest, excess real estate taxes, and the operating-expense carryover from the prior year's worksheet. Line 4f = smaller of line 3 or 4e.
5. Line 5 = line 3 − line 4f.
6. Lines 6a–6c: rental share of excess casualty losses, rental share of depreciation, and the prior year's casualty/depreciation carryover. Line 6e = smaller of line 5 or 6d.
7. Part III carryovers: 7a = 4e − 4f (operating expenses); 7b = 6d − 6e (excess casualty losses and depreciation).

When 4e or 6d cannot be deducted in full, the allowable amount can be allocated among those expenses in any way; enter the allocated amounts on the matching Schedule E lines (Pub. 527, Worksheet 5-1 footnote). The carryover stays tied to the same property and applies even in a later year when the unit is not used as a home (Pub. 527 ch. 5, Limit on deductions).

Show the whole worksheet in the draft. The reviewer needs lines A–F and 1–7b to accept a Schedule E column that nets exactly to zero.

## 7. Related situations

- **Renting part of your home** (a room, a basement unit): treat the rented part as a separate property; divide shared expenses by a reasonable method; expenses only for the rented part are not divided; no part of the first phone line is deductible (Pub. 527 ch. 4, Renting Part of Property). Pub. 527's duplex example splits mortgage interest and taxes 50/50 between Schedule E and Schedule A.
- **Property changed to rental use** during the year: only expenses for the rental period are rental expenses; depreciation basis is the smaller of adjusted basis or FMV on the conversion date (Pub. 527 ch. 4).
- **Not rented for profit**: report the income on Schedule 1, line 8j; no rental expenses deductible; mortgage interest (main or second home), taxes and casualty losses on Schedule A if itemizing. Presumption of profit: rental income exceeds rental expenses in at least 3 of 5 consecutive years; Form 5213 can postpone the determination (file within 3 years after the due date of the first year's return, or within 60 days after a written IRS notice, if earlier) (Pub. 527 ch. 4).
- **Hotel-like services** (regular cleaning, changing linen, maid service, primarily for the tenant's convenience): Schedule C, possibly with self-employment tax (Pub. 527 ch. 3; Instructions, Line 3). Route to `../../schedule-c/SKILL.md`.

## 8. Questions to ask (stop until answered)

1. "For each property, list every date range in 2025 when it was rented, who rented it, and what they paid."
2. "Did you, a co-owner, or any family member (spouse, sibling, parent, grandparent, child, grandchild) use the property, even for one night? Did they pay full market rent?"
3. "Did you swap use with another property owner?"
4. "Were any of your days at the property spent working substantially full time on repairs or maintenance? Which days?"
5. "Was the property your main home before or after the rental period? Did you rent or try to rent it for 12 consecutive months, or sell it at the end?"
6. "Do you itemize deductions on Schedule A? Are your total state and local taxes above $40,000 ($20,000 if married filing separately)?"
7. "Do you have a Worksheet 5-1 carryover (line 7a or 7b) from last year for this property?"
