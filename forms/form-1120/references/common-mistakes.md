# Form 1120 Common Mistakes

Errors to catch before a Form 1120 draft goes out, each with the rule and the fix. Sources are the 2025 Form 1120, the 2025 Instructions for Form 1120, Publication 542 (Rev. January 2024), and the IRC sections cited.

---

## 1. Deducting federal income tax on line 17

**Pattern:** the books show "income tax expense" and the whole account lands on line 17.

**Rule:** line 17 excludes federal income taxes (Instr., Line 17). Federal income tax per books is added back on Schedule M-1 line 2.

**Fix:** move the federal portion off line 17. Keep state and local income or franchise taxes on line 17 if deductible.

## 2. Distributions treated as a deduction

**Pattern:** dividends paid to shareholders, or owner draws, appear on line 26 or line 12.

**Rule:** distributions are not deductions. They belong on Schedule M-2 line 5 (or the question 13 line when the schedules are not required) and are reported to shareholders on Form 1099-DIV.

**Fix:** remove from page 1; ask whether any "draw" was actually payroll compensation run through Form 941. Do not reclassify without payroll records.

## 3. Officer compensation chosen by the preparer

**Pattern:** the agent or user plugs a "typical" salary into line 12, or splits a lump sum between line 12 and line 13 without records.

**Rule:** line 12 is the compensation actually paid or incurred and deductible (Instr., Line 12). Unreasonable compensation to a shareholder-officer can be recharacterized as a nondeductible dividend (IRC §162(a)(1) allows only a reasonable allowance for salaries).

**Fix:** use only W-2 and payroll figures supplied by the user; list each officer on Form 1125-E when total receipts are $500,000 or more. Refer reasonableness questions to a CPA.

## 4. Skipping Form 1125-E

**Pattern:** total receipts are, say, $480,000 of sales plus $30,000 of interest and other income, and no Form 1125-E is attached.

**Rule:** Form 1125-E is required when total receipts (line 1a plus lines 4 through 10) are $500,000 or more (Instr., Line 12). Interest and other income count.

**Fix:** recompute total receipts with lines 4 through 10 and attach Form 1125-E; carry its line 4 to line 12.

## 5. Taking the full NOL carryforward

**Pattern:** a $150,000 post-2017 carryforward against $120,000 of line 28 income produces a $120,000 deduction and zero taxable income.

**Rule:** post-2017 NOLs offset at most 80% of taxable income figured without the NOL, section 199A, and section 250 deductions (Instr., Line 30). With no special deductions, the cap here is $96,000.

**Fix:** limit line 29a, carry the remaining $54,000 forward, and enter the full $150,000 available on Schedule K item 12.

## 6. Ignoring the section 246(b) limit on the dividends-received deduction

**Pattern:** a year with weak operating results and large dividends takes the full 50% or 65% deduction.

**Rule:** the deduction is limited by taxable income through the Worksheet for Schedule C, Lines 9 and 22, unless the full deduction creates or increases an NOL (Instr., Line 9, Column (c)).

**Fix:** run the worksheet; apply the NOL-year exception test first.

## 7. Claiming the deduction on dividends that fail the holding period

**Pattern:** a corporation buys stock shortly before the ex-dividend date, sells soon after, and puts the dividend on Schedule C line 1.

**Rule:** no deduction for stock held less than 46 days during the 91-day period beginning 45 days before the ex-dividend date (Instr., Line 20; section 246(c)).

**Fix:** move those dividends to line 20 with no deduction.

## 8. Charitable contributions deducted in full

**Pattern:** a $12,000 gift deducted on line 19 by a corporation with $60,000 of taxable income.

**Rule:** the deduction is limited to 10% of taxable income computed without the contribution deduction, special deductions, and carrybacks; with an NOL carryover, the base is taxable income after the NOL deduction; excess carries forward 5 years (Instr., Line 19). For tax years beginning after December 31, 2025, only the amount above 1% of taxable income is deductible, up to 10% (IRC §170(b)(2)(A) as amended by P.L. 119-21 §70426).

**Fix:** compute the limit, deduct the allowed amount, record the carryover, and add any book-versus-return difference to Schedule M-1.

## 9. Netting dividends on line 4

**Pattern:** line 4 shows dividends after the dividends-received deduction.

**Rule:** line 4 is Schedule C line 23, total dividends and inclusions in column (a). The deduction is on line 29b from Schedule C line 24.

**Fix:** gross up line 4 and put the deduction on line 29b.

## 10. Wrong answer to question 13

**Pattern:** the corporation compares only gross sales to $250,000, or answers "Yes" because receipts are small while assets are $310,000.

**Rule:** total receipts are line 1a plus lines 4 through 10, and both total receipts and year-end total assets must be under $250,000 (Form 1120, Schedule K question 13).

**Fix:** recompute; if either test fails, complete Schedules L, M-1, and M-2.

## 11. Assuming Schedule M-2 is optional when question 13 is "No"

**Pattern:** a draft completes Schedules L and M-1 but leaves M-2 blank, reasoning from the Form 1120-S rule.

**Rule:** on Form 1120, question 13 "Yes" excuses L, M-1, and M-2 together; a "No" requires all three.

**Fix:** complete M-2 and tie line 8 to Schedule L line 25 column (d).

## 12. M-1 that does not tie to line 28

**Pattern:** Schedule M-1 line 10 is reconciled to line 30 (after the NOL) instead of line 28.

**Rule:** M-1 line 10 is "Income (page 1, line 28)".

**Fix:** keep the NOL deduction and special deductions out of M-1.

## 13. Estimated tax based on last year's zero

**Pattern:** last year's return showed no tax (a loss year), so the corporation paid no 2025 estimates, relying on the prior-year rule.

**Rule:** the prior-year method is available only if the prior return covered 12 months and showed a positive tax; a large corporation may use it only for the first installment (Pub. 542; Instructions for Form 2220).

**Fix:** flag Form 2220 exposure; for next year, base installments on 25% of the current-year tax when the prior-year method is unavailable.

## 14. Relying on Form 1120-W

**Pattern:** the user asks for "the 2026 Form 1120-W".

**Rule:** Form 1120-W and its instructions are historical; the 2022 revisions were the last (Pub. 542, Rev. January 2024).

**Fix:** compute installments with Pub. 542 Methods 1 and 2 and document them in the draft's estimated-tax section.

## 15. Missing Schedule G or Form 5472

**Pattern:** a founder owns 60% and a foreign fund owns 30%, but questions 4b and 7 are answered "No".

**Rule:** question 4b "Yes" requires Schedule G Part II; question 7 "Yes" (one foreign person 25% or more by vote or value) may require Form 5472 for each reportable related party (Instr., Questions 4 and 7).

**Fix:** answer both "Yes"; attach Schedule G; route Form 5472 work to [`../../form-5472/SKILL.md`](../../form-5472/SKILL.md).

## 16. Capitalizing 2025 domestic research costs out of habit

**Pattern:** the 2025 draft amortizes current-year domestic research costs over 5 years as in 2022–2024.

**Rule:** for tax years beginning after December 31, 2024, section 174A(a) allows a current deduction for domestic research and experimental expenditures, or an election to amortize over at least 60 months; transition options for unamortized 2022–2024 amounts are in Rev. Proc. 2025-28 (Instr., What's New).

**Fix:** ask which treatment and which transition option the corporation adopted; take amounts from the user's or CPA's computation.

## 17. Paper filing when e-file is mandatory

**Pattern:** a calendar-year corporation that was required to file, during calendar 2025, its 2024 Form 1120, 4 Forms 941, 1 Form 940, 3 Forms W-2, and 3 Forms 1099-NEC mails a paper 2025 Form 1120.

**Rule:** a corporation must e-file Form 1120 if it is required to file at least 10 returns of any type (income tax, employment tax, excise tax, information returns) during the calendar year ending with or within its tax year; controlled group members count their returns together (Instr., Electronic Filing; Reg. §301.6011-5(a)(1)–(2), (d)(5)). The count here is 12.

**Fix:** count returns for the right calendar year before choosing a channel; route to an authorized e-file provider or request a waiver (filing.md).

## 18. Treating an extension as more time to pay

**Pattern:** Form 7004 filed, tax paid with the return in October.

**Rule:** Form 7004 extends the time to file, not to pay; interest and the late-payment penalty run from the original due date (Instr., Tax Payments, Interest and Penalties).

**Fix:** pay the estimated balance by the original due date through EFTPS or the IRS business tax account.
