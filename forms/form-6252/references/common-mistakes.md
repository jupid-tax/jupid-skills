# Common Form 6252 Mistakes

Errors that misstate installment sale income, with the rule that catches each one. Sources: 2025 Form 6252 and its instructions (https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f6252.pdf), Pub. 537 (2025), 2025 Form 4797, 2025 Instructions for Schedule D.

## 1. Spreading depreciation recapture over the payments

**Mistake:** §1245 or §1250 recapture left inside gross profit and taxed only as payments arrive.

**Rule:** Recapture "is fully taxable in the year of sale even if no payments were received" (line 12 instructions). Form 4797 Part III → line 31 → Form 6252 line 12 and Form 4797 line 13.

**Fix:** Run Form 4797 Part III in the year of sale for every depreciable asset before Part I. If recapture equals the whole gain, line 14 is zero and no Form 6252 is filed.

## 2. Putting §1252/§1254/§1255 recapture on line 12, or all of it in year one

**Mistake:** Farmland, mining, or cost-sharing recapture treated like §1245 recapture.

**Rule:** Those go on line 25 (and Form 4797 line 15), never more than line 24 each year; the excess carries to later years (line 25 instructions). §179 ordinary income never goes on line 25.

## 3. Including interest in payments or selling price

**Mistake:** The full monthly payment entered on line 21, or the total of principal plus interest entered on line 5.

**Rule:** Lines 5, 21, 23, and 33 exclude stated and unstated interest; interest is reported elsewhere (Form 6252 instructions, "Interest").

**Fix:** Use the amortization schedule; principal only on Form 6252, interest on Schedule B.

## 4. Entering the buyer's new loan on line 6

**Mistake:** The buyer gets a bank mortgage, the seller's loan is paid off at closing, and the bank loan is entered on line 6.

**Rule:** Line 6 is only the seller's debt the buyer assumed or took the property subject to, "Don't include new mortgages the buyer gets from a bank, the seller, or other sources" (line 6 instructions). Cash from the buyer's new loan is a payment; amounts withheld at closing to pay off the seller's mortgage are payments on line 21.

## 5. Missing the deemed payment when assumed debt exceeds basis

**Mistake:** Line 17 left blank when the assumed mortgage is larger than the installment sale basis.

**Rule:** Line 17 = line 6 − line 13 when positive; it is added to the contract price (line 18) and treated as a year-of-sale payment on line 20. The percentage becomes 100% (Pub. 537, "Mortgage more than basis").

## 6. Recomputing the gross profit percentage every year

**Mistake:** Line 19 recalculated from current-year amounts.

**Rule:** In later years, enter the year-of-sale percentage "even if you didn't file Form 6252 for that year" (line 19 instructions). Recompute only when the selling price is reduced (Pub. 537 Worksheet B).

## 7. Rounding the percentage to two places

**Mistake:** 58.78% entered as 0.59.

**Rule:** Decimal rounded to at least 4 digits (0.5878) (line 19 instructions). Two-place rounding misstates gain on every payment over the life of the note.

## 8. Stopping Form 6252 in a year with no payment

**Mistake:** No Form 6252 filed for a year in which the buyer skipped a payment.

**Rule:** File every year until and including the year of final payment, "regardless of whether a payment is received in such year" (Form 6252 instructions, "Purpose of Form").

## 9. Using the installment method for a loss or for publicly traded securities

**Rule:** No loss on the installment method (line 24 instructions; Rev. Rul. 70-430). Stock or securities traded on an established market: report all on Form 8949 / Schedule D in the year of sale. Inventory and dealer property are excluded too (Pub. 537).

## 10. Ignoring the related-party resale

**Mistake:** A child or controlled entity buys on installments and resells within 2 years; the seller keeps reporting only actual payments.

**Rule:** §453(e); Part III lines 30–37 accelerate the gain at the second disposition unless a line 29 exception applies. Part III is required for the year of sale and the 2 years after.

**Fix:** Ask about any resale, gift, or other disposition by the related buyer every year Part III is required.

## 11. Using the installment method for depreciable property sold to a controlled entity

**Rule:** §453(g): all payments are treated as received in the year of sale unless the seller shows tax avoidance was not a principal purpose (Form 6252 instructions, "Sale of Depreciable Property to Related Person"). Pub. 537 lists the related persons for this rule; they differ from the Part III list.

## 12. Treating the post-resale payments as new income

**Mistake:** After a Part III acceleration, the seller enters the related buyer's later payments on line 21 again.

**Rule:** Amounts reported on the equivalent of line 34 in a prior year go on line 23, not line 21 (line 21 instructions), so they produce no further gain.

## 13. Forgetting unrecaptured §1250 gain on later payments

**Mistake:** The 25% maximum-rate gain on a rental building is applied only in the year of sale, or prorated across payments.

**Rule:** Schedule D instructions, Unrecaptured Section 1250 Gain Worksheet, line 4, Step 3: gain on each payment is unrecaptured §1250 gain until the total from Step 2 is used up. Track the remaining balance year by year.

## 14. Below-market interest left unadjusted

**Mistake:** A 0% or 2% note to a relative, with the full stated price on line 5.

**Rule:** If interest is below the test rate, §483 or §1274 recharacterize part of the principal as interest, reducing the selling price (Pub. 537). See [`interest-and-afr.md`](./interest-and-afr.md).

## 15. Testing the rate against the wrong month's AFR

**Mistake:** Comparing the note's rate to the current month's AFR at the time the return is prepared.

**Rule:** The test rate is the lower of the lowest AFR in the 3 months ending with the binding-contract month and the lowest AFR in the 3 months ending with the sale month, for the note's term and compounding period (Pub. 537, "Test rate of interest").

## 16. Missing the election-out deadline

**Mistake:** The user decides in the next year to "elect out."

**Rule:** The election is made on a timely filed return including extensions; a timely filer who did not elect can still elect on an amended return within 6 months of the due date, excluding extensions, marked "Filed pursuant to section 301.9100-2." Revocation requires IRS approval (Form 6252 instructions; Pub. 537).

## 17. Borrowing against the note without counting a payment

**Rule:** Pledge rule (§453A(d)): for sales over $150,000 (except personal-use, farm, timeshare and residential lot property), net proceeds of a loan secured by the installment obligation are a payment, capped at contract price minus prior payments.

## 18. Misreading the §453A threshold

**Mistake:** Assuming interest on deferred tax applies to any sale over $5 million, or never to a $3 million sale.

**Rule:** It applies when the sales price exceeds $150,000 and the aggregate face amount of nondealer installment obligations arising during and outstanding at the end of the year exceeds $5 million (Form 6252 instructions; IRC §453A(b)). Report on Schedule 2 line 15.

## 19. Cancelling or gifting the note without reporting

**Rule:** §453B: a gift, cancellation, or sale of the obligation is a disposition; gain or loss is FMV (or amount realized) minus basis in the obligation; for related parties a cancelled obligation's FMV is at least face value (Pub. 537, "Disposition of an Installment Obligation").

## 20. One Form 6252 for a whole business with no allocation

**Rule:** A business sale is a sale of separate assets: allocate price and payments, exclude inventory and recapture-only assets, file Form 8594, and either use a separate Form 6252 per sale or one form with an attached per-asset schedule (Pub. 537, "Sale of a Business" and "Reporting an Installment Sale").
