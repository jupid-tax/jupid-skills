# Federal Poverty Line (FPL) Tables for Form 8962

PTC eligibility and the applicable figure depend on household income as a percentage of the federal poverty line. Form 8962 Line 4 is the FPL value for the filer's tax family size and state group.

## Critical year rule

**Form 8962 uses prior-year HHS poverty guidelines.** For tax year **2025** (returns filed 2026), use the **2024** HHS poverty guidelines. For tax year **2026**, use the **2025** HHS guidelines (published January 2025).

Source citation: IRC §36B(d)(3)(B) — the poverty line used is the most recently published poverty line as of **the 1st day of the regular enrollment period** for coverage during the calendar year. For the 2025 plan year, that's November 2024 → use the 2024 HHS table (2025 Form 8962 instructions, Line 4: "For 2025, the 2024 federal poverty lines are used").

## State groups

HHS publishes three FPL tables:

- **48 contiguous states + DC** — base table
- **Alaska** — higher (cost of living adjustment)
- **Hawaii** — higher (cost of living adjustment)

Check the box for the state of residence during the tax year (Form 8962 Line 4: a Alaska, b Hawaii, c other 48 states and DC). If the filer moved during the year and lived in Alaska and/or Hawaii, or joint filers lived in different states, use the table with the higher dollar amounts (2025 Form 8962 instructions, Line 4).

## How to compute FPL for tax family size > 8

For each additional person beyond size 8, add the per-person increment from the relevant table. Example for 48+DC, 2024: family size 10 = $52,720 + 2 × $5,380 = $63,480.

## How the FPL value lands on Form 8962

Line 4 = exact FPL value (not 100%, 200%, 400% — just the 100% baseline for the family size).

Line 5 = (Line 3 / Line 4) × 100, rounded down to whole percent.

Example: Single filer, household income $30,000, 2024 FPL for family size 1 = $15,060.

- Line 4 = $15,060
- Line 5 = ($30,000 / $15,060) × 100 = 199.20% → rounded down to **199%**

## Common mistakes

1. **Using current-year HHS instead of prior-year**. Most-flunked rule. Always prior-year.
2. **Using the wrong state group**. Alaska and Hawaii filers easily forget to use the right table.
3. **Counting non-dependents in family size**. Tax family size = filer + spouse + dependents *claimed on the return*, not "people in the household".
4. **Counting a dependent who's claimed by someone else**. A child who lives with the filer but is claimed as a dependent by the other parent (custody arrangement) is in the *other* parent's tax family.
5. **Rounding Line 5 up instead of down**. 199.99% becomes 199%, not 200%. The applicable figure for 199% is in the 150-200% band; for 200% it's in the 200-250% band — different sliding rates.
