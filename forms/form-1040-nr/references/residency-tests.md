# Residency Tests for Form 1040-NR (2025 returns)

Load this before drafting anything. Form 1040-NR is only for an individual who is a nonresident alien for the whole year. Sources: Instructions for Form 1040-NR (2025), "Resident Alien or Nonresident Alien"; Pub. 519 (2025), chapter 1; Form 8843 (2025) and Form 8840 (2025) instructions; IRC §7701(b).

Run the tests in this order and stop at the first one that decides the case.

---

## 1. Green card test

A person who was a lawful permanent resident (Form I-551) **at any time** in 2025 is a resident for 2025. Status continues until it is taken away or administratively or judicially determined to have been abandoned. When the person initiates abandonment, it is effective when Form I-407 or a letter of intent is filed with the green card; mailed filings must go certified, return receipt requested. An old card (over 10 years) or a long absence does not end resident status by itself.

- Green card at any time in the year → not this skill. If the card was granted or surrendered during the year, it is a dual-status year: stop (see section 6).

## 2. Substantial presence test (IRC §7701(b)(3))

Resident for 2025 if physically present in the United States on at least:

1. **31 days during 2025**, and
2. **183 days** during 2025, 2024, and 2023 counted as: all 2025 days + **1/3** of 2024 days + **1/6** of 2023 days.

The 1040-NR instructions print the multipliers as 1.000, 0.333, and 0.167; the statute uses the fractions 1/3 and 1/6. Compute with the exact fractions and show the arithmetic. Any part of a day counts as a day (Schedule OI item H instructions: any part of the 24-hour period beginning at 12:01 a.m.). "United States" means the 50 states, D.C., and U.S. territorial waters; it does not include U.S. territories or U.S. airspace (Pub. 519).

Worked check from Pub. 519: 120 days in each of 2025, 2024, and 2023 → 120 + 40 + 20 = 180 → not met.

**Ask for evidence when the total is within about 10 days of 183**: passport stamps or the CBP I-94 travel history (i94.cbp.dhs.gov, cited in the Form 8843 instructions). Don't accept a rounded estimate near the line.

### Days that do not count

| Category | Rule | Paperwork |
|---|---|---|
| Regular commuters from Canada or Mexico | Commute on more than 75% of workdays in the working period | Schedule OI item G box |
| In transit | In the U.S. less than 24 hours between two places outside the U.S. | None |
| Foreign vessel crew | Regular crew member of a foreign vessel in transport between the U.S. and a foreign country or U.S. territory, unless otherwise engaged in U.S. business that day | None |
| Medical condition | Intended to leave but couldn't because of a condition that arose in the U.S. (not a preexisting known condition, not travel for treatment) | Form 8843 Part V with physician statement |
| NATO visa | Member of a force or civilian component; family members still count every day | None |
| Exempt individual | See next table | Form 8843 (except A/G foreign government-related individuals) |

### Exempt individuals

| Category | Visa | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Foreign government-related individual | A or G, other than A-3 or G-5 | None stated; no Form 8843 needed for other A/G classes |
| Teacher or trainee | J or Q (not as a student), substantially complying | Not exempt in 2025 if exempt as teacher, trainee, or student for any part of **2 of the 6** preceding calendar years (2019–2024). Exception: exempt for any part of 3 or fewer of the 6 prior years AND a foreign employer paid all 2025 compensation AND the person was present as a teacher or trainee in a prior-6 year AND a foreign employer paid all compensation in each of those years |
| Student | F, J, M, or Q, substantially complying | Not exempt in 2025 if exempt as teacher, trainee, or student for any part of **more than 5** calendar years, unless they establish no intent to reside permanently (closer connection abroad; no affirmative steps toward permanent residence); Form 8843 line 12 statement |
| Professional athlete | Temporarily present to compete in a charitable sports event | Form 8843 Part IV with proof that net proceeds went to charity |

Questions to ask an F/J/M/Q holder, one at a time:

1. "Which visa did you hold in each calendar year from 2019 through 2025, and in which of those years were you in the U.S. at all?"
2. "Were you ever a teacher or trainee on a J or Q visa (not as a student)?"
3. "Did you apply for, or take steps toward, a green card during 2025?" (Form 8843 line 13)

### Form 8843 filing rule

- With a 2025 Form 1040-NR: attach Form 8843; lines 1a–4a can be satisfied by writing "Information provided on Form 1040-NR" on line 1a; complete line 4b and the relevant Part.
- No return required: mail Form 8843 alone to Department of the Treasury, Internal Revenue Service Center, Austin, TX 73301-0215 by the Form 1040-NR due date (including extensions).
- Not filed on time: the student/teacher/trainee/athlete/medical days can't be excluded unless the person shows by clear and convincing evidence that they took reasonable actions to learn the requirement and significant steps to comply.

## 3. Closer connection exception (Form 8840)

A person who meets the substantial presence test can still be a nonresident if all are true:

- present fewer than **183 days** in 2025;
- had a tax home in a foreign country for all of 2025;
- had a closer connection to that one foreign country (or, under stated conditions, two) than to the U.S.;
- has no pending application for adjustment to permanent resident status and has not applied or taken steps to apply.

Each spouse files a separate, fully completed Form 8840, attached to Form 1040-NR or, with no return, mailed to Austin, TX 73301-0215 by the 1040-NR due date. Not filed on time → the exception is lost (same clear-and-convincing-evidence relief as Form 8843). Treas. Reg. §301.7701(b)-2.

## 4. Treaty tie-breaker (dual-resident taxpayer)

A person who is a resident under the Code and also a resident of a treaty country under its law can, if the treaty's tie-breaker article assigns residence to the other country, figure U.S. tax as a nonresident by filing Form 1040-NR with **Form 8833** attached (Treas. Reg. §301.7701(b)-7). Competent authority help: Rev. Proc. 2015-40. Special Form 8938 rules apply to dual-resident taxpayers.

Boundary: identify the position, list the consequences (Form 8833 required; $1,000 penalty for a missing Form 8833 under IRC §6712), and ask the user to have a CPA confirm the tie-breaker analysis. For a green card holder, also repeat Pub. 519 ch. 1: a lawful permanent resident who is required to file a tax return as a resident and fails to do so may be regarded as having abandoned that status; immigration advice is outside this skill.

## 5. Elections that make a nonresident a resident (route away)

- **Nonresident spouse treated as resident**: married, spouse a U.S. citizen or resident on the last day of the year, joint Form 1040 for the election year with the Pub. 519 statement. One-time; may forfeit treaty benefits. → form-1040, not this skill.
- **First-year choice** (Pub. 519 ch. 1): someone who does not meet either test this year but meets substantial presence next year may choose part-year residence. This creates a dual-status year → stop.

## 6. Dual-status year: stop

A dual-status year is a year with both resident and nonresident periods (usually the arrival or departure year). Signals: a green card granted or surrendered in the year; meeting the substantial presence test in a year the person arrived (residency starts on the first day present, subject to Pub. 519 exceptions); leaving the U.S. for good during the year.

Consequences to tell the user (Instructions for Form 1040-NR, Dual-Status Taxpayers; Pub. 519 ch. 6):

- Resident on December 31 → Form 1040 marked "Dual-Status Return" with a Form 1040-NR statement; nonresident on December 31 → Form 1040-NR marked "Dual-Status Return" with a Form 1040 statement.
- No standard deduction, no head of household, no joint return (unless the resident election is made), no EIC, credit for the elderly or disabled, or education credits.
- Married → MFS rates.
- Pub. 519 (2025): dual-status taxpayers cannot e-file their 2025 return.
- Paper addresses: Austin, TX 73301-0215 without payment; P.O. Box 1303, Charlotte, NC 28201-1303 with payment.

Then ask: "This is a dual-status year, which this skill doesn't draft. Do you want me to list the documents a CPA will need?"
