# Doing Business in California (R&TC §23101)

An LLC not taxed as a corporation must file Form 568 (and pay the $800 + any LLC fee) if it is **organized in California**, **registered with the California Secretary of State**, or **doing business in California** under R&TC §23101 (2025 Form 568 Booklet, General Information D). The doing-business prong catches out-of-state LLCs that have California nexus without registering.

This is the most-litigated question in California LLC tax. The agent should walk through the tests carefully — getting it wrong creates either unnecessary $800 payments or unfiled-return penalties.

---

## The tests (R&TC §23101(a) and (b))

An LLC is "doing business in California" if **any** of these is true (R&TC §23101; FTB "Doing business in California" page):

### Test 1 — Active engagement in California (§23101(a))
The LLC is **actively engaging in any transaction** for the purpose of financial or pecuniary gain or profit in California. This is fact-specific; even one transaction can qualify. An LLC is also doing business in California if any of its members, managers, or other agents conduct business in California on its behalf (2025 booklet, General Information D).

### Test 2 — Organized or commercially domiciled in California (§23101(b)(1))
The LLC is organized under California law **or** has its commercial domicile (principal place of business) in California.

### Test 3 — California sales exceed threshold (§23101(b)(2))
California sales, including sales by agents and independent contractors, **exceed** the lesser of **$757,070** (2025) or 25% of total sales. Sales are assigned using R&TC §§25135–25136.

### Test 4 — California real or tangible property exceeds threshold (§23101(b)(3))
California real and tangible personal property **exceeds** the lesser of **$75,707** (2025) or 25% of total real and tangible personal property.

### Test 5 — California compensation exceeds threshold (§23101(b)(4))
California compensation paid **exceeds** the lesser of **$75,707** (2025) or 25% of total compensation paid.

> **Threshold values**: the dollar thresholds in tests 3–5 are revised every year (R&TC §23101(c)). Year by year: 2025 $757,070 / $75,707 / $75,707; 2024 $735,019 / $73,502 / $73,502; 2023 $711,538 / $71,154 / $71,154 (FTB, https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/doing-business-in-california.html, updated 11/07/2025; 2025 values also in the 2025 Form 568 Booklet). The 2026 values were not posted as of 2026-10-06; re-check that page before relying on a number near the line. The LLC's pro rata share of sales, property, and payroll of partnerships and S corporations counts toward its own totals (§23101(d)).

If **any one** test is met, the LLC is "doing business in California" and must file Form 568.

## Common gotchas

### Gotcha 1 — Out-of-state LLC with a California member

If a California-resident member or manager runs or works in the LLC's business from California, the LLC meets Test 1: an LLC is doing business in California if any member, manager, or agent conducts business in California on its behalf (2025 booklet, General Information D).

The reverse direction is narrower. Members of an LLC doing business in California are themselves doing business in California only if they have any ability or authority to influence or participate in management (2025 booklet, General Information A). *Swart Enterprises v. Franchise Tax Board* (2017, Cal. Ct. App.) held that a passive 0.2% member of a manager-managed LLC was not doing business in California.

### Gotcha 2 — Online business with California customers

An LLC with no physical California presence but enough California customers can meet Test 3 (sales threshold). An LLC operating from another state with California sales above $757,070 (2025), or above 25% of its total sales, is "doing business in California" under §23101(b)(2).

### Gotcha 3 — Single-member LLC owned by a California-resident individual

The owner's California residency does not by itself put the LLC in California — but if the owner conducts the business from California (laptop in San Francisco, customers anywhere), Test 1 (a member conducting business in California for the LLC) and Test 2 (commercial domicile) are likely met. The LLC owes $800 and Form 568.

### Gotcha 4 — Real estate LLC holding California property

Even a passive holding LLC owning California real estate generally meets Test 1 and Test 4 — both income from CA real estate (rents) and the property itself. Single-member LLCs holding California rental properties are an extremely common Form 568 filer.

### Gotcha 5 — LLC dissolved mid-year but had California activity earlier

The LLC is "doing business in California" for the part of the year it had nexus, even if it dissolved before year-end. Form 568 is still required (check "Final Return", Item H(2)), and the $800 is still owed for that taxable year. Cancellation with the SOS (Form LLC-4/7, plus Form LLC-3 for a domestic LLC) is required to stop the $800 for **future** years (2025 booklet, General Information Q).

## When the LLC does NOT need to file Form 568

The LLC has **no California nexus** if all of these are true:

- Not organized under California law
- Not registered with California SOS as a foreign LLC doing business in California
- No member, manager, or agent conducting the LLC's business in California
- No California property (real or tangible)
- No California sales above the threshold
- No California compensation above the threshold
- No California-source business activity

If every test fails, no Form 568 is required and the $800 is not owed. Exception: a nonregistered foreign LLC (other than a disregarded single-member LLC) that is not doing business but has California-source income files Form 565 instead (2025 booklet, General Information D).

---

## Voluntary registration

An LLC that has **registered with California SOS as a foreign LLC** (Form LLC-5) is required to file Form 568 and pay $800 — even if its actual nexus is borderline. The act of registration creates the obligation (2025 booklet, General Information D and F). A registered LLC with no California activity and no California-source income still files, writing "SB 1106 Filing" at the top of Side 1.

If the user registered "just in case" but never actually did business in California, tell them the $800 continues until the registration is cancelled with the SOS. Which SOS form applies to a foreign LLC is an SOS question; point the user to sos.ca.gov or bizfile Online.

---

## "Final Return" mechanics

When the LLC ceases doing business in California:

1. **Check "Final Return"** (Item H(2)) on Form 568 Side 1 and attach a statement explaining the termination
2. **File the final Form 568** timely (including extension) and pay the $800 for that final taxable year
3. **Stop doing business in California** after the final taxable year
4. **File Form LLC-4/7** (Certificate of Cancellation) with the California SOS, plus **Form LLC-3** (Certificate of Dissolution) for a domestic LLC, within 12 months of the timely final return

If all four happen, no further annual tax is assessed. If the cancellation is filed after the taxable year ends, a return and another $800 for the next year may be required (2025 booklet, General Information Q).

## Citation summary

- R&TC §23101(a) — definition of "doing business"
- R&TC §23101(b)(1)-(4) — domicile, sales, property, and compensation tests
- R&TC §23101(c) — annual revision of the thresholds
- R&TC §23101(d) — pass-through entity shares count
- 2025 Form 568 Booklet, General Information D (who must file) and Q (cancelling an LLC)
- *Swart Enterprises v. Franchise Tax Board* (2017, Cal. Ct. App.) — limit on FTB's reach for passive minority members
- FTB Pub 3556 — Limited Liability Company Filing Information

For complex multi-state nexus questions (especially involving holding-company structures, passive minority interests under *Swart*, or borderline economic nexus), the agent should redirect to a California-licensed CPA. This skill cannot replace legal/tax advice on nexus disputes.
