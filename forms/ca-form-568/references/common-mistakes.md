# Common Form 568 Mistakes

The audit-trip and notice-generating mistakes most commonly seen on California Form 568. Each entry includes the trigger, the consequence, and the citation.

---

## 1. Not filing Form 568 because the LLC had $0 revenue

**Trigger**: First-year LLC owner thinks "no revenue = no tax = no return".

**Reality**: Every California LLC owes $800 (R&TC §17941) and must file Form 568 every year, regardless of revenue or profit, until it is cancelled with the Secretary of State (Form LLC-4/7, plus Form LLC-3 for a domestic LLC).

**Consequence**: Penalty for failure to comply with filing requirements under R&TC §19172 — $18 per member per month or part of a month, max 12 months ($216 per member per year). For a 2-member LLC this is $432/year on top of the $800 owed. If tax or fee is unpaid, the late-filing penalty under R&TC §19131 (5% per month, max 25%) can apply too (2025 booklet, General Information G).

**Citation**: R&TC §17941, §19172, §19131.

---

## 2. Relying on the AB 85 first-year exemption after 2023

**Trigger**: User read online that the $800 first-year tax is waived; user formed LLC in 2024 or later.

**Reality**: AB 85 (Stats. 2020, ch. 8) waived the first-year $800 tax only for first taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2021, and before January 1, 2024. LLCs whose first taxable year begins in 2024, 2025, or 2026 owe the full $800, due by the 15th day of the 4th month after filing with the SOS (formed June 18 → due September 15). For first taxable years beginning in 2027 through 2029, SB 122 reduces the first-year tax to $400 — it does not eliminate it.

**Consequence**: Late-payment penalty + interest on the $800 from the original due date.

**Citation**: AB 85 (Stats. 2020, ch. 8); FTB Limited Liability Company page <https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/limited-liability-company/index.html>; FTB "What's new with tax forms" (SB 122) <https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/whats-new.html>.

## 3. Using gross profit (or net profit) as Schedule IW total income

**Trigger**: User computes Schedule IW by copying gross profit (federal Form 1065 line 3) or net profit and skips the cost-of-goods-sold lines.

**Reality**: Schedule IW total income is **gross income plus cost of goods sold** — i.e., gross receipts, not gross profit. Line 1a (Schedule B gross profit) is paired with line 1b (cost of goods sold). For a reseller with $480K revenue and $300K COGS, gross profit is $180K but Schedule IW line 17 is $480K. This determines the LLC fee tier.

**Consequence**: Understated line 1 → underpaid LLC fee → the fee balance plus late-payment penalty and interest, and possibly the 10% estimated-fee penalty.

**Citation**: R&TC §17942(b)(1)(A); 2025 Form 568, Schedule IW.

---

## 4. Underestimating Form 3536 in a growth year

**Trigger**: LLC paid less in June than both its prior-year fee and this year's fee — e.g., prior-year fee $2,500, June payment $900, final fee $6,000.

**Reality**: R&TC §17942(d)(2) adds 10% of the underpayment (fee for the year − amount paid by the 6th-month date). No penalty applies if the June payment is at least the preceding year's total fee. An LLC that paid $900 in June after a $900 prior-year fee owes no penalty even if the final fee is $2,500.

**Consequence**: In the trigger case, 10% × ($6,000 − $900) = $510. Separately, the unpaid balance is due by the return's original due date; paying it late adds the late-payment penalty and interest.

**Mitigation**: Pay at least the prior-year fee with FTB 3536 by the 15th day of the 6th month; pay the remaining fee with FTB 3536 by the original due date. All of it goes on Form 568, Side 1, line 8.

**Citation**: R&TC §17942(d); 2025 FTB 3536 instructions.

---

## 5. Forgetting to dissolve with SOS after "Final Return"

**Trigger**: LLC owner files final Form 568 marking "Final Return", then assumes the LLC is closed.

**Reality**: Marking "Final Return" on Form 568 tells the FTB. It does **not** tell the SOS. The FTB stops assessing the $800 only if the LLC files a timely final return, does no business in California after the final taxable year, and files the SOS cancellation (Form LLC-4/7, plus Form LLC-3 for a domestic LLC) within 12 months of the timely final return.

**Consequence**: Continuing $800 + filing requirement until the cancellation is filed.

**Citation**: 2025 Form 568 Booklet, General Information Q; California Corporations Code §17707.01 et seq. (LLC dissolution and cancellation).

---

## 6. Not collecting Form 3832 for a nonresident member

**Trigger**: Multi-member LLC has one member who lives in Nevada; LLC files Form 568 without FTB 3832, without Schedule T, and without withholding on her distributions.

**Reality**: Two separate rules. (1) The member signs FTB 3832, or the LLC pays tax on her distributive share on Schedule T (12.3% for an individual). (2) Regardless of consent, distributions of California-source income to a domestic nonresident member above $1,500 in the calendar year require 7% withholding, remitted with Form 592-Q and reported on Form 592-PTE, with Form 592-B to the member.

**Consequence**: Schedule T tax + penalties and interest; liability for the tax that should have been withheld plus interest and penalties.

**Citation**: 2025 Form 568 Booklet, General Information F, P, R; R&TC §18662; 2025 Form 592-PTE instructions.

---

## 7. Claiming federal §168(k) bonus depreciation on California

**Trigger**: LLC bought $50K equipment, took 100% federal bonus depreciation, copied the same $50K deduction to California Schedule K.

**Reality**: California does not conform to §168(k) (2025 FTB 3885L). Recompute depreciation on FTB 3885L; California depreciates under MACRS (e.g., 20% Year 1 on a 5-year asset under the half-year convention = $10K).

**Consequence**: Understated California income on Schedule K (568) and the members' returns; assessment with interest. The California statute of limitations is generally four years from the due date or the filing date, whichever is later, and is extended for federal examinations (2025 booklet, General Information A).

**Citation**: 2025 FTB 3885L; R&TC §17250 (depreciation).

---

## 8. Claiming federal §179 over $25K on California

**Trigger**: LLC took $100K federal §179; assumes California allows the same.

**Reality**: California §179 limit is $25,000, reduced dollar for dollar once §179 property placed in service exceeds $200,000 (2025 FTB 3885L, lines 1 and 3); the remaining basis is depreciated under MACRS. Federal 2025 limit: $2,500,000, threshold $4,000,000 (2025 Instructions for Form 4562).

**Consequence**: Same as §168(k) — California adjustment, understated income, assessment.

**Citation**: R&TC §17255, §24356; 2025 FTB 3885L.

## 9. Filing on wrong due date

**Trigger**: Multi-member LLC files Form 568 by April 15 thinking it follows the SMLLC deadline.

**Reality**: Partnership-classified Form 568, and a single-member LLC owned by a pass-through entity, is due the 15th day of the 3rd month after year-end (March 16, 2026 for 2025, because March 15 fell on a Sunday). Other single-member LLCs are due the 15th day of the 4th month after the close of the owner's taxable year (April 15, 2026).

**Consequence**: If filed after the extended due date, the late-filing penalty runs from the original due date; plus the R&TC §19172 $18 per member per month penalty and late-payment penalty on any balance.

**Mitigation**: California grants an automatic extension to FILE without any form: 7 months for partnership-classified LLCs and SMLLCs owned by a partnership, 6 months for other SMLLCs. The LLC fee balance (FTB 3536) and nonconsenting nonresident members' tax (FTB 3537) are still due by the original date.

**Citation**: R&TC §18633.5, §18567; 2025 Form 568 Booklet, General Information E; 2025 FTB 3537.

---

## 10. Filing Form 568 when LLC elected S-corp federally

**Trigger**: LLC filed federal Form 2553 to elect S-corp status; then files Form 568 by habit.

**Reality**: California automatically follows a federal S election (R&TC §23801(a)); LLCs classified as S corporations file **Form 100S**, not Form 568 (2025 booklet, General Information A). The §17942 LLC fee does NOT apply; the §23802 1.5% S-corporation tax (with the $800 minimum) does.

**Consequence**: Wrong return; the Form 100S filing obligation is unmet. Tell the user to have a CPA file the correct Form 100S and resolve the Form 568 already filed with the FTB.

**Citation**: R&TC §23801(a), §23802.

---

## 11. Treating an out-of-state LLC with California members as having no nexus

**Trigger**: Wyoming LLC with one California-resident member and online customers nationwide.

**Reality**: Test 1 (a member conducting business in California on the LLC's behalf) and Test 2 (commercial domicile) are likely met if the California member operates the LLC from California. *Swart Enterprises* (2017) concerned the reverse question — a passive 0.2% member of a manager-managed LLC was not itself doing business in California.

**Consequence**: Unfiled Form 568 + unpaid $800/year + late penalties on every year of nexus.

**Mitigation**: When in doubt, consult a CPA or treat the LLC as having California nexus and file. The cost of compliance ($800 + maybe a small fee) is much less than back-taxes after a notice.

**Citation**: R&TC §23101; 2025 Form 568 Booklet, General Information D; *Swart Enterprises v. Franchise Tax Board* (2017).

---

## 12. Forgetting Form LLC-3 / LLC-4/7 in dissolution

(Same as #5; included again because it's the most common multi-year mistake — the owner thinks they closed the LLC and later gets an $800 bill because the SOS still has it active.)

**Mitigation**: After filing the final Form 568, check bizfile Online to confirm the cancellation (and, for a domestic LLC, dissolution) is recorded within 12 months of the final return. A domestic LLC that never did business and cancels within 12 months of organizing can use the short form, LLC-4/8, and avoid the first-year tax.

**Citation**: 2025 Form 568 Booklet, General Information Q; California Corporations Code §17707.01 et seq.

## Quick reminder list for the agent

Before declaring a Form 568 draft ready, walk through this list:

1. ☐ Has the user paid FTB 3522 ($800) for this year?
2. ☐ If expected fee > $0, was FTB 3536 paid by the 6th month, and is it at least the prior-year fee?
3. ☐ Schedule IW computed using gross income plus COGS, not gross profit?
4. ☐ Federal §168(k) and §179-over-$25K recomputed on FTB 3885L?
5. ☐ Every nonresident member has FTB 3832 or a Schedule T entry, **and** distributions over $1,500 were withheld on (592-Q / 592-PTE / 592-B)?
6. ☐ If "Final Return", will the SOS cancellation (LLC-4/7, plus LLC-3 for a domestic LLC) be filed within 12 months?
7. ☐ Right deadline (15th day of 3rd month for partnership-classified and pass-through-owned SMLLCs; 4th month for other SMLLCs)?
8. ☐ Correct return (Form 568 vs. Form 100S vs. Form 100)?
9. ☐ Multi-state? Schedule IW assigned item by item and Schedule R attached?
10. ☐ Schedules L, M-1, M-2 required (federal Form 1065 Schedule B Questions 4a–4c not all "Yes", or more than 10 members)?

If any answer is "no" or "I don't know", surface the issue to the user before finalizing.
