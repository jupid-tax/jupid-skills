# Form W-4 — Step-by-Step Reference

Complete walkthrough of the **2026 Form W-4** (Cat. No. 10220Q, created 12/8/25), verified on 2026-10-06 against the form text, including its page 2 instructions, the Step 2(b) Multiple Jobs Worksheet (page 3), the Step 4(b) Deductions Worksheet (page 4) and the Multiple Jobs tables (page 5). Re-check the 2027 form before using this file for 2027 wages: https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-4. The form has 5 steps plus an "Exempt from withholding" box. Most filers complete Steps 1 and 5 only.

## Step 1 — Personal Information (REQUIRED for everyone)

### Step 1(a) — Name and Address

Enter your full legal name, current mailing address (street, city, state, ZIP).

**Common gotchas:**
- Use legal name as on the Social Security card. If the name doesn't match the card, the form tells the employee to contact SSA (800-772-1213 or www.ssa.gov) so they get credit for their earnings. Pub. 15 (2026), section 4: the employer reports the name and SSN as shown on the card.
- If recently moved, use the new address — your W-2 mails to whatever address is on file when payroll prints W-2s.

### Step 1(b) — Social Security Number

Enter your 9-digit SSN. Must match the Social Security card.

If you don't have an SSN yet, apply for one with SSA (Form SS-5). An ITIN is not accepted in place of an SSN for employment (Pub. 15 (2026), section 4: "Don't accept an ITIN in place of an SSN for employee identification or for work"). An employee who has applied but not received the SSN gives it to the employer when it arrives; the employer handles the W-2 ("Applied For") under Pub. 15 section 4.

### Step 1(c) — Filing Status

Check exactly one. The status sets the standard deduction and tax rates payroll uses (2026 Form W-4, page 2):

- **Single or Married Filing Separately**: Use this if unmarried, divorced, or married but filing separately
- **Married Filing Jointly or Qualifying Surviving Spouse**: Use if married and filing one return; or if a widow(er) with a dependent child within 2 years of spouse's death
- **Head of Household**: Use ONLY if all three are true (form text: "unmarried and pay more than half the costs of keeping up a home for yourself and a qualifying individual"):
  1. Unmarried (or "considered unmarried" — lived apart from spouse last 6 months of year)
  2. Paid more than half the cost of keeping up a home
  3. A qualifying person (usually child or dependent parent) lived with you more than half the year (for parents, can live elsewhere)

Don't claim HoH if uncertain — see [Pub 501](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p501).

**SSN caution (new on the 2026 form):** "To claim certain credits or deductions on your tax return, you (and/or your spouse if married filing jointly) are required to have a social security number valid for employment." Ask before entering Step 3 or the Schedule 1-A items in the Deductions Worksheet.

## Step 2 — Multiple Jobs or Spouse Works (CONDITIONAL)

Complete Step 2 if **either** is true (2026 Form W-4, Step 2):
- You hold more than one job at a time
- You are married filing jointly and your spouse also works

**Why it's needed:** Each employer's withholding assumes its wages are the household's only wages, so it applies the full standard deduction and the lowest brackets to each job. Example from the 2026 Single table (page 5, row $40,000–59,999, column $50,000–59,999): two jobs of about $50,000 each call for $7,080 of extra annual withholding.

### Three options (do only ONE)

**Option (a) — IRS Tax Withholding Estimator (most accurate)**

Go to www.irs.gov/W4App (landing page https://www.irs.gov/individuals/tax-withholding-estimator). The form says to use this option if you or your spouse have self-employment income. Have the most recent pay stubs for all jobs. The estimator asks for:
- Pay and year-to-date withholding from all jobs
- Filing status, dependents
- Other income, deductions, credits

Output: the entries for the W-4, including an extra amount for Step 4(c). Most accurate option, especially for mid-year changes (the form's TIP recommends it when completing the W-4 after the start of the year).

**Option (b) — Multiple Jobs Worksheet (page 3; tables on page 5)**

Page 5 has three tables, in this order: Married Filing Jointly or Qualifying Surviving Spouse; Single or Married Filing Separately; Head of Household. Rows = "Higher Paying Job Annual Taxable Wage & Salary"; columns = "Lower Paying Job Annual Taxable Wage & Salary" in $10,000 bands up to $120,000.

Worksheet lines:
- **Line 1 — Two jobs.** Find the value at the intersection of the higher-paying job's row and the lower-paying job's column. Then skip to line 3.
- **Line 2 — Three jobs.** 2a: table value for the highest-paying job (row) and next-highest job (column). 2b: add the wages of the two highest jobs and use the total as the row, with the third job's wages as the column. 2c: 2a + 2b.
- **Line 3** — Number of pay periods per year for the highest-paying job (52 weekly, 26 biweekly, 12 monthly, etc.).
- **Line 4** — Line 1 (or 2c) ÷ line 3. Enter in Step 4(c) of the highest-paying job's W-4, plus any other extra amount.

If more than one job has annual wages over $120,000 or there are more than three jobs, the form sends you to Pub. 505 for additional tables or to the Estimator.

Example (MFJ):
- Higher job $95,000, lower job $68,000
- MFJ table: row "$80,000 - 99,999", column "$60,000 - 69,999" → $7,610
- $7,610 / 26 biweekly = $292.69 → $293/check on Step 4(c)

**Option (c) — Step 2(c) Checkbox**

Only if there are exactly two jobs in total. Check the box on BOTH W-4s. When the box is checked, "the standard deduction and tax brackets will be cut in half for each job" (2026 Form W-4, page 2); payroll uses the Pub. 15-T "Form W-4, Step 2, Checkbox, Withholding Rate Schedules".

**Accuracy rule from the form:** option (c) "is generally more accurate than Step 2(b) if pay at the lower paying job is more than half of the pay at the higher paying job. Otherwise, Step 2(b) is more accurate." With unequal pay, the checkbox withholds more than necessary, and the excess grows with the pay difference.

### Coordination rule

Complete **Steps 3 through 4(b)** on only ONE W-4; leave them blank on the others. Withholding is most accurate if you do this on the W-4 for the highest-paying job (2026 Form W-4, Step 2 note). Otherwise dependent credits and adjustments are double-counted across employers.

## Step 3 — Claim Dependent and Other Credits (CONDITIONAL)

**Complete only if total income will be:**
- $200,000 or less (Single, HoH, MFS)
- $400,000 or less (MFJ)

Above those amounts the Child Tax Credit and Credit for Other Dependents are reduced by $50 for each $1,000 (or fraction) of modified AGI over the threshold (IRC §24(b)(1) with the §24(h)(3) thresholds); the form gives no Step 3 instructions above the line, so use the Estimator.

### The math

```
(a) Qualifying children under 17 × $2,200  = $___
(b) Other dependents             × $500    = $___
    Other expected credits (ask)           = $___
                                            ━━━━━━
Total — Step 3                             = $___
```

$2,200 per child is printed on the 2026 form (P.L. 119-21; Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.05).

### Qualifying child (CTC at $2,200)

Per the form's page 2 instructions: the child must be under age 17 as of December 31, must be your dependent who generally lives with you for more than half the year, and must have the required SSN. In full (Instructions for Schedule 8812 (2025); IRC §152(c), §24(c), §24(h)(7)):
1. **Relationship**: Son, daughter, stepchild, eligible foster child, sibling, half-sibling, step-sibling, or descendant of any (grandchild, niece, nephew)
2. **Age**: Under 17 at end of tax year
3. **Residency**: Lived with you more than half the year (with exceptions for temporary absences such as school, illness, military service)
4. **Support**: Did not provide more than half of own support
5. **Dependent / joint return / citizenship**: Claimed as your dependent, not filing a joint return (other than only to claim a refund), and a U.S. citizen, national, or resident alien
6. **Child's SSN**: Valid for employment and issued before the return's due date, including extensions (not an ITIN, not an ATIN)
7. **Your SSN**: You, or at least one spouse on a joint return, must have an SSN valid for employment issued before the due date; the other spouse needs an SSN or ITIN (new for 2025 and later; Instructions for Schedule 8812 (2025), What's New)

### Other dependents (Credit for Other Dependents at $500)

Includes:
- Children 17 or older (lost CTC eligibility but still qualify as dependents)
- Children who are dependents but lack an SSN valid for employment, if they have an SSN, ITIN, or ATIN issued by the due date
- Parents you support (don't need to live with you)
- Qualifying relatives (must meet income, support, and relationship tests; 2026 gross income limit $5,300, Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.23)

The dependent must be a U.S. citizen, national, or resident alien.

### Other credits

The form lets you add an estimate of other credits for the year, "such as the foreign tax credit and the education tax credits." Ask the user; do not invent an amount.

### Coordination

In MFJ, only ONE W-4 carries Step 3 — the highest-paying job's for best accuracy. The other spouse leaves Step 3 blank.

In divorced/separated households, only the parent claiming the child as a dependent on the tax return enters them on Step 3. Custodial parent typically; non-custodial parent only with signed Form 8332.

## Step 4 — Other Adjustments (OPTIONAL)

Three independent fields. Complete any subset.

### Step 4(a) — Other Income (not from jobs)

Enter the **annual** expected amount of other income that won't have withholding. The form lists "interest, dividends, and retirement income"; other non-job, non-business income (capital gains, rental, gambling, cancellation of debt) fits here too.

**Do NOT include income from any jobs or self-employment** (2026 Form W-4, page 2, Step 4(a)). For self-employment income the form says to use the Estimator (Step 2(a)) and enter its result in Step 4(c), or to pay estimated tax with Form 1040-ES.

**What this does:** Payroll adds the amount to wages before computing withholding (Pub. 15-T (2026), Worksheet 1A line 1d). If you complete Step 4(a), "you likely won't have to make estimated tax payments for that income."

### Step 4(b) — Deductions (Deductions Worksheet, page 4)

Enter line 15 of the Deductions Worksheet. Payroll subtracts it from annual wages (Pub. 15-T (2026), Worksheet 1A line 1f). If skipped, withholding is based on the standard deduction. Every line, from the 2026 form:

| Line | Entry |
|---|---|
| 1a | Qualified tips: if total income is less than $150,000 ($300,000 MFJ), estimated qualified tips up to $25,000 |
| 1b | Qualified overtime: if total income is less than $150,000 ($300,000 MFJ), estimated qualified overtime up to $12,500 ($25,000 MFJ) of the "and-a-half" portion of time-and-a-half pay |
| 1c | Qualified passenger vehicle loan interest: if total income is less than $100,000 ($200,000 MFJ), estimate up to $10,000 |
| 2 | 1a + 1b + 1c |
| 3a | Seniors: if total income is less than $75,000 ($150,000 MFJ), $6,000 if you are 65 or older before the end of the year |
| 3b | Same test, $6,000 if your spouse is 65 or older before the end of the year and has an SSN valid for employment |
| 4 | 3a + 3b |
| 5 | Estimate of student loan interest, deductible IRA contributions, educator expenses, alimony paid, and certain other Schedule 1 Part II adjustments (Pub. 505) |
| 6a | Medical and dental expenses in excess of 7.5% of total income |
| 6b | State and local taxes: if total income is less than $505,000 ($252,500 MFS), taxes paid up to $40,400 ($20,200 MFS) |
| 6c | Home mortgage interest (including mortgage insurance premiums) if home acquisition debt is less than $750,000 ($375,000 MFS) |
| 6d | Gifts to charities in excess of 0.5% of total income |
| 6e | Other itemized deductions |
| 7 | 6a + 6b + 6c + 6d + 6e |
| 8a | Total income |
| 8b | 8a − line 4. If line 4 is greater, enter -0- here and on line 10 and skip line 9 |
| 9 | $768,700 MFJ/QSS; $640,600 Single or HoH; $384,350 MFS |
| 10 | If line 9 > line 8b, line 7; otherwise line 7 × 94% |
| 11 | Standard deduction: $32,200 MFJ/QSS; $24,150 HoH; $16,100 Single or MFS |
| 12 | If taking the standard deduction: cash gifts to charities up to $1,000 ($2,000 MFJ) |
| 13 | 11 + 12 |
| 14 | If line 10 > line 13: line 10 − line 11. If line 13 > line 10: line 12 |
| 15 | 2 + 4 + 5 + 14 → Step 4(b) |

The thresholds above are the form's simplified tests ("total income"); the return itself uses modified AGI and the Schedule 1-A and Schedule A rules. The Schedule 1-A instructions govern who qualifies for lines 1a, 1b, 1c, 3a and 3b (form note above line 1). Line 11 standard deductions match Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.14; line 9 amounts match the 37% bracket starts in Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.01.

Show every line in the draft, including zeros.

### Step 4(c) — Extra Withholding

Enter a **per-pay-period** flat dollar amount added to every paycheck's withholding, "including any amounts from the Multiple Jobs Worksheet, line 4."

Use cases:
- Result from Step 2 option (a) or (b)
- Income tax and SE tax on self-employment income, if the user prefers withholding to Form 1040-ES (Estimator result, or a Pub. 505 Worksheet 1-3/1-5 projection)
- Owed taxes last year and want to bump withholding
- Privacy alternative: the form lets an employee who doesn't want to report other income in Step 4(a) enter an extra amount here instead

**Example:** Want $2,600 of additional annual withholding, paid biweekly (26 periods) → $100 per check on Step 4(c).

## Exempt from withholding (CONDITIONAL)

Check the box only if BOTH are true (2026 Form W-4, page 2):
1. No federal income tax liability in 2025: total tax on 2025 Form 1040 line 24 is zero (or less than the sum of lines 27a, 28, 29, and 30), or no return was required because income was below the filing threshold
2. You expect no federal income tax liability in 2026

Then complete Steps 1(a), 1(b), and 5 only. No federal income tax is withheld; social security and Medicare tax still is (Pub. 15 (2026), section 9). The exemption lasts for 2026 only: submit a new W-4 by **February 16, 2027** to continue it. Nonresident aliens may not claim exemption (Pub. 15 (2026), section 9). The employer may treat a form as invalid if "exempt" is claimed together with Step 2(c) or numbers in Steps 3 and 4.

---

## Step 5 — Sign and Date (REQUIRED)

Sign under penalties of perjury and date the form. "This form is not valid unless you sign it."

If the employer has no valid W-4 for the employee, it withholds as if the employee checked Single or Married filing separately in Step 1(c) and made no entries in Steps 2, 3, or 4; if an earlier valid W-4 exists, it keeps using that one (Pub. 15 (2026), section 9, "Invalid Forms W-4"; form Privacy Act notice).

Submit to your employer's HR or payroll department. Don't send to the IRS (the employer may be directed to send copies on request).

## Filing Status Decision Tree

```
Are you legally married on December 31?
├─ YES
│   ├─ Filing one return with spouse? → MFJ
│   ├─ Lived apart from spouse for the last 6 months, paid > half the home costs,
│   │  and a qualifying child lived with you > half the year? → may be HoH (check Pub 501)
│   └─ Otherwise filing separate returns? → MFS
│
└─ NO (single, divorced, legally separated)
    ├─ Did your spouse die in the last 2 years AND do you have a dependent child?
    │   └─ YES → QSS (Qualifying Surviving Spouse)
    │
    ├─ Are all THREE true: unmarried + paid >half home costs + qualifying person lived with you >half year?
    │   └─ YES → HoH
    │
    └─ Otherwise → Single
```

## Step Coordination for MFJ Two-Earner Households

| Spouse | Step 1 | Step 2 | Step 3 | Step 4 | Step 5 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Higher-paying job | ✅ Required | ✅ Option (a) or (b) result in 4(c), or check 2(c) | ✅ Claim all dependents here | ✅ 4(a), 4(b), 4(c) | ✅ Sign |
| Lower-paying job | ✅ Required | ☐ Blank, or ✅ check 2(c) if the other W-4 checks it | ☐ Leave blank | ☐ 4(a)/4(b) blank; 4(c) optional | ✅ Sign |

The rule: only ONE W-4 in the household carries Steps 3 through 4(b). With option (c), both W-4s check the Step 2(c) box.

## Effective Date Rules (Pub. 15 (2026), section 9; Pub. 505 (2026), chapter 1)

- New hire W-4 → effective with the first wage payment
- Replacement W-4 → employer must put it into effect no later than the start of the first payroll period ending on or after the 30th day after receiving it
- A W-4 that makes a change for the next calendar year won't take effect in the current year
- Changes that reduce the withholding you are entitled to (e.g., MFJ to Single, losing an expected Child Tax Credit, credits down more than $500, deductions down more than $2,300) → new W-4 within 10 days
