# Top 1099-MISC Mistakes (with citations and fixes)

The most common errors agents and payers make on Form 1099-MISC, with the underlying authority and the fix.

## 1. Skipping 1099-MISC for a corporate medical provider

**Mistake**: Payer pays $4,000 to a hospital (corporation) for an executive's annual physical and skips 1099-MISC because "we don't 1099 corporations."

**Why wrong**: The standard corporate exemption (Reg. §1.6041-3(p)(1)) does **NOT** apply to Box 6 medical payments. Hospitals, doctor offices organized as corporations, lab corps — all reportable on Box 6 if the year's total meets the threshold ($600 for 2025 payments, $2,000 for 2026). Exception: 501(c)(3) tax-exempt and government-owned hospitals.

**Fix**: Issue 1099-MISC with Box 6 = $4,000 (unless the hospital is tax-exempt or government-owned).

**Citation**: Reg. §1.6041-3(p)(1); Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, Box 6 ("The exemption from issuing Form 1099-MISC to a corporation does not apply to payments for medical or health care services provided by corporations, including professional corporations.").

## 2. Skipping 1099-MISC Box 10 for a corporate law firm

**Mistake**: Payer settles a lawsuit, sends the $50,000 settlement to the plaintiff's law firm (a professional corporation), and skips 1099-MISC because the firm is incorporated.

**Why wrong**: The corporate exemption does not apply to payments for legal services (Instructions, "Payments to corporations for legal services"; Reg. §1.6041-3(p)(1)). Box 10 (gross proceeds paid to attorney) is reportable regardless of attorney's entity type.

**Fix**: Issue 1099-MISC with Box 10 = $50,000 (the gross — full settlement amount). Don't issue a 1099-NEC for the claimant's attorney's fee: the payer does not report it, whatever the firm's entity type. If the claim is taxable, also issue the plaintiff a 1099-MISC with Box 3 = $50,000.

**Citation**: IRC §6045(f); Reg. §1.6045-5; Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, "Gross proceeds paid to attorneys".

## 3. Putting attorney's fee on Box 10 instead of gross proceeds

**Mistake**: For a $60,000 settlement with $20,000 attorney fee + $40,000 to plaintiff, payer puts $20,000 in Box 10.

**Why wrong**: Box 10 is **gross proceeds** — the entire $60,000 paid to the attorney. The payer does not report the attorney's $20,000 fee at all.

**Fix**: 
- 1099-MISC to attorney, Box 10 = $60,000
- No 1099-NEC to the attorney
- 1099-MISC to plaintiff, Box 3 = $60,000 if the damages are taxable (none if they are non-punitive personal physical injury damages)

**Citation**: Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, "Gross proceeds paid to attorneys" ("Generally, you are not required to report the claimant's attorney's fees. For example, an insurance company pays a claimant's attorney $100,000 to settle a claim. The insurance company reports the payment as gross proceeds of $100,000 in box 10."); Reg. §1.6045-5(f), Examples 2–3.

## 4. Forgetting Box 3 for plaintiff's settlement portion

**Mistake**: Payer issues Box 10 to attorney ($60,000) but forgets to issue Box 3 to the plaintiff, or issues Box 3 for only the plaintiff's $40,000 net.

**Why wrong**: For taxable damages, the payer must report the full amount to the claimant under §6041, even though part of it went to the attorney (Instructions example: "pays $2,000 of taxable damages to a claimant by paying that amount to a claimant's attorney" → Box 3 to the claimant and Box 10 to the attorney).

**Fix**: Issue 1099-MISC to plaintiff with Box 3 = $60,000 (the full taxable damages; threshold $600 for 2025 payments, $2,000 for 2026).

**Citation**: IRC §6041; Reg. §1.6041-1(f); Reg. §1.6045-5(f), Example 3; Instructions, "Gross proceeds paid to attorneys" and Box 3.

## 5. Treating residential rent paid by tenant as 1099-MISC Box 1

**Mistake**: An individual tenant pays rent to a landlord for their personal apartment. They wonder if they need to issue 1099-MISC.

**Why wrong**: 1099-MISC Box 1 reporting only applies to rent paid **in the course of a trade or business**. Personal residential rent paid by a tenant to a landlord is NOT 1099-MISC reportable.

**Fix**: No 1099-MISC for personal rent. The landlord reports the rent on their own Schedule E using their own records.

If the tenant uses part of the apartment as a home office and deducts the business-portion rent on Schedule C, ask whether that portion is paid in the course of the trade or business; if so, and the landlord is non-corporate and the business portion meets the threshold ($600 for 2025, $2,000 for 2026), it may be reportable in Box 1. Treat this as a judgment call for a CPA.

**Citation**: Reg. §1.6041-1(a)(1)(i) (definition of "in the course of trade or business"); Instructions, "Trade or business reporting only".

## 6. Applying the wrong year's threshold

**Mistake**: Payer applies $2,000 to 2025 payments (filed in early 2026), or $600 to 2026 payments (filed in early 2027), for Box 1 rents, Box 3, Box 6, Box 9, Box 12, or Box 15.

**Why wrong**: P.L. 119-21 §70433 replaced the $600 in IRC §6041(a) with $2,000 for payments made after December 31, 2025, indexed for inflation from 2027 (§6041(h)). 2025 payments keep $600. The boxes with their own sections did not change: royalties and substitute payments $10, attorney gross proceeds $600, fish purchases $600, fishing boat proceeds any amount.

**Fix**: Ask which calendar year the payments were made in, then apply that year's threshold per box (Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, Rev. April 2025 vs. Rev. December 2026).

**Citation**: IRC §6041(a), (h); P.L. 119-21 §70433; Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC (Rev. December 2026), What's New and "Specific Instructions for Form 1099-MISC".

## 7. Filing 1099-MISC with January 31 IRS deadline (confusing with 1099-NEC)

**Mistake**: Payer files Copy A of 1099-MISC by January 31, treating it the same as 1099-NEC.

**Why wrong**: 1099-MISC has a **later** IRS deadline than 1099-NEC. For 1099-MISC:
- Recipient Copy B: January 31 (February 15 if Box 8 or Box 10 has an amount)
- IRS Copy A paper: February 28
- IRS Copy A electronic (IRIS): March 31

Each date moves to the next business day if it falls on a weekend or legal holiday (for 2026 forms: Feb 1, Feb 16, Mar 1, Mar 31, 2027). For 1099-NEC, both IRS and recipient copies are due January 31. Filing 1099-MISC early is not an error; the error is treating the NEC date as the only date and missing the Box 8 / 10 recipient date or vice versa.

**Fix**: Don't conflate the two schedules. Use the correct calendar.

**Citation**: IRC §6071(c) (acceleration of due date for nonemployee compensation only); Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, "Filing dates" and "Statements to Recipients"; Pub. 1099 (2026), Reminders (February 15 for Boxes 8 and 10).

## 8. Putting Box 6 medical payment in Box 3 (other income)

**Mistake**: Payer pays a doctor $1,500 and reports it in Box 3 instead of Box 6.

**Why wrong**: Box 6 is the dedicated box for medical / health-care services. Box 3 is a catch-all for other income. The boxes have different downstream tax treatments and audit risk profiles. Recipients may miscategorize on their return based on Box.

**Fix**: Use Box 6 for medical / health-care payments. If already filed, this is an incorrect money amount (Error Type 1): file one corrected 1099-MISC with the CORRECTED box checked, Box 3 = $0 and Box 6 = $1,500, and furnish a corrected Copy B.

**Citation**: Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, Box 6; Pub. 1099 (2026), part H, Error Type 1 ("Incorrect money amount(s), code, or checkbox").

## 9. Issuing 1099-MISC to corporate landlord

**Mistake**: Payer issues 1099-MISC Box 1 to a property management LLC taxed as S-corp.

**Why wrong**: Standard corporate exemption applies to Box 1 rent. Corporations and S-corps are exempt for rent payments.

**Fix**: Verify entity type from W-9 line 3a. If C-corp / S-corp (or LLC taxed as either): do not issue. If individual / partnership / SMLLC: issue if the year's rent meets the threshold ($600 for 2025, $2,000 for 2026).

Special case: a property management company that collects rent on behalf of a landlord. Rent paid to a real estate agent or property manager is not reportable by the tenant, whatever the manager's entity type; the agent or manager reports the rent paid over to the owner on 1099-MISC (Reg. §1.6041-3(d); Reg. §1.6041-1(e)(5), Example 5).

**Citation**: Reg. §1.6041-3(p)(1) (corporations), §1.6041-3(d) (rental agents).

## 10. Issuing 1099-MISC Box 3 for §104-excluded settlement

**Mistake**: Payer issues 1099-MISC Box 3 = $30,000 to a plaintiff for a personal-injury settlement that's fully excluded under IRC §104(a)(2).

**Why wrong**: The instructions say not to report damages (other than punitive damages) received on account of personal physical injuries or physical sickness (Box 3 item 6a). The payer still reports the gross proceeds paid to the attorney in Box 10 (Reg. §1.6045-5(f), Example 2: "P does not file an information return with respect to tax-free damages paid to C").

**Fix**: No Box 3 for non-punitive physical-injury damages. Report punitive damages and any nonphysical-injury portion in Box 3. For mixed claims, follow the settlement agreement's allocation; if there isn't one, ask the user to get it from counsel before filing.

**Citation**: IRC §104(a)(2); Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, Box 3 item 6; Reg. §1.6045-5(f); IRS Pub. 4345 (Settlements — Taxability).

## 11. Assuming CF/SF covers every state

**Mistake**: Payer files federal 1099-MISC via IRIS with the CF/SF option and assumes every state is covered.

**Why wrong**: Not every state participates in the Combined Federal/State Filing Program, and some participating states still require direct filing. Example: Massachusetts participates in CF/SF but requires Forms 1099-MISC, 1099-NEC, 1099-K and others to be filed directly with its DOR (mass.gov, "Form 1099 Filing Requirements").

**Fix**: Ask which states the payees are in, then check each state's revenue department rule. See Pub. 1099 (2026), CF/SF Program section, for the IRS side.

**Citation**: Pub. 1099 (2026); state DOR websites.

## 12. Treating Stripe-collected rent as 1099-MISC Box 1

**Mistake**: Payer pays rent through Stripe to landlord; tries to issue 1099-MISC Box 1.

**Why wrong**: Payments made with a payment card or through a third party network are reported on Form 1099-K by the payment settlement entity and "are not subject to reporting on Form 1099-MISC or Form 1099-NEC." The payer should NOT issue 1099-MISC Box 1 for the same payments.

**Fix**: Exclude card- and platform-paid amounts from 1099-MISC issuance. Whether the platform actually issues a 1099-K depends on its own thresholds (third party network: over $20,000 and over 200 transactions; card payments: no minimum); that doesn't change the payer's answer.

**Citation**: IRC §6050W; Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, "Form 1099-K".
