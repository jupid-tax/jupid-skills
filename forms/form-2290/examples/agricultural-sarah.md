# Form 2290 — Worked Example: Sarah, Ranch Owner with Cattle Hauler (Agricultural Suspension)

End-to-end Form 2290 filing for a single-vehicle agricultural operation under the suspension category at the 7,500-mile threshold, 2026-2027 period (Form 2290 Rev. July 2026).

---

## Persona

**Sarah** owns a 600-acre cattle ranch in Montana. She has a single hauling truck — a 2018 Kenworth T800 — that she uses about 4 times a year to take cattle to auction in Billings (~120 miles each way).

- Truck: 2018 Kenworth T800
- Taxable gross weight: 56,000 lbs (Category B)
- Use: **Agricultural** — transporting her own cattle (livestock, a farm commodity) and feed to and from the ranch; all of its highway miles are farming purposes
- Estimated annual mileage on public highways: ~4,000 miles (well under the 7,500 agricultural threshold)
- Truck is registered with Montana DMV as a highway motor vehicle used for farming purposes, for the whole period
- EIN: 45-1234567 (registered to her ranch operation, Big Sky Ranch LLC, taxed as sole proprietorship via SMLLC)
- Files Schedule F (farming income) on her personal Form 1040

## Why Agricultural Suspension Applies

Sarah's situation meets all three conditions under IRC §4483(d)(5) and the Form 2290 instructions ("Agricultural vehicles"):

1. **Used primarily for farming purposes** — more than half of its highway mileage (here, all of it) is transporting a farm commodity (livestock, feed) to or from a farm
2. **Registered as a farm vehicle** under Montana law for the entire period
3. **Expected highway miles ≤ 7,500** during the period — Sarah projects about 4,000 miles, well under the limit (miles driven on the ranch itself don't count)

She files Form 2290 with the truck in **Category W** and pays $0 tax.

## Filing Workflow

1. **August 10, 2026** — Sarah receives an annual reminder to file Form 2290
2. **August 12, 2026** — Sarah logs into a 2290 e-file provider she picked from the IRS list
3. **Provider workflow:**
   - Confirms business name (Big Sky Ranch LLC), EIN, Montana address
   - Selects tax period 2026-2027
   - Adds vehicle: VIN, Category W (suspended), agricultural flag = Yes, first-use July 2026
   - Provider fills Part II line 7 with the 7,500-mile agricultural box
   - Sarah reviews and digitally signs
   - Tax due: $0
   - No payment method needed (since tax is $0)
   - Submits filing
4. **Within 5 minutes** — IRS accepts; watermarked Schedule 1 PDF returned (listing the VIN under Category W)
5. **August 12, 2026** — Sarah saves stamped Schedule 1:
   - Cloud storage backup
   - Print copy for ranch office
6. **September 2026** — Sarah renews Montana plates with the agricultural designation; uploads stamped Schedule 1 to MT DMV portal; renewal approved

## Mileage Tracking Plan

Because Sarah is filing under suspension, she must track miles to ensure she stays under 7,500. Her plan:

- **Notebook in glove compartment** — date, route, miles for each trip
- **Monthly summary** transferred to a spreadsheet
- **Threshold alert at 6,500 miles** — gives 1,000 miles of buffer to file an Amended Return if needed

## What If Mileage Exceeds 7,500?

If Sarah unexpectedly crosses 7,500 miles (say, a year with extra hauling for neighbors or expanded operations):

1. **File Form 2290 with the Amended Return box checked**, writing the month the limit was exceeded next to the box, by the last day of the following month
2. **Report and pay the tax on line 2**, figured from the month the truck was first used in the period. First use was July 2026, so it is the full annual tax for Category B (56,000 lbs): $122.00 (page 2, column (1)(a))
3. **Receive updated stamped Schedule 1** showing Category B (no longer suspended)
4. **Forward updated Schedule 1** to Montana DMV

If she exceeded the limit in May 2027, she'd file by June 30, 2027, and pay $122.00. If she exceeded it in February 2027, she'd file by March 31, 2027.

The tax is based on the first-use month, not the month the limit was exceeded (instructions, "Suspended vehicles exceeding the mileage use limit").

## At Tax Time (Schedule F for Tax Year 2026)

Sarah files Schedule F (Profit or Loss From Farming), not Schedule C, because she's a farmer. Her HVUT is $0, so there's nothing to deduct this year.

If she had paid tax (e.g., if she exceeds the limit), the HVUT would go on **Schedule F, Line 29 (Taxes)** (2025 Schedule F line numbering).

The truck's other expenses (fuel, repairs, insurance, depreciation) go elsewhere on Schedule F per the standard farm expense lines.

## Lessons This Example Illustrates

1. **Suspension is filed, not skipped** — Sarah files Form 2290 even though she owes $0
2. **7,500-mile threshold for agricultural vehicles** is higher than the 5,000-mile general rule
3. **State agricultural registration matters** — the truck must be registered under Montana law as a farming-purposes vehicle for the entire period
4. **Stamped Schedule 1 still required** — the DMV needs proof of HVUT compliance even at $0
5. **Mileage tracking is non-optional** — without it, she can't defend the suspension if questioned, and can't catch an overage in time to amend
6. **Schedule F, not Schedule C** — farmers file Schedule F; ranchers, dairy operators, and crop growers use this form
