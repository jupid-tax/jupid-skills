# Sport Fishing and Archery Manufacturers Excise Tax (§4161)

IRC §4161 imposes a manufacturers excise tax on sport fishing equipment and archery equipment. The tax is paid by the manufacturer, producer, or importer on its sale of the taxable article (for arrow shafts, the first sale). Retail sellers (sporting goods stores) generally don't pay; they buy at a price that already reflects the tax. A person who assembles a taxable article from parts can be a manufacturer (Pub. 510, ch. 5, "Manufacturer").

**Verified against:** IRC §§4161, 4162, 4216 (law.cornell.edu), Form 720 (Rev. June 2026) Part II, Instructions for Form 720 (Rev. June 2026), Pub. 510 (Rev. Dec. 2025) ch. 5, Rev. Proc. 2024-40 §2.44 and Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.43, on 2026-10-06.

**Where it goes on Form 720:** all of these are **Part II** lines. They are paid with the return: "Pay this tax with Form 720. No tax deposits are required." (Pub. 510, ch. 5). Part II taxes are not reported on Schedule A (Form 720 Schedule A note).

## Sport fishing equipment (§4161(a))

### Tax base and rate

| Item | IRS No. | Rate | Source |
|------|---------|------|--------|
| Sport fishing equipment other than rods and poles (reels, lines, lures, terminal tackle, nets, etc.) | 41 | 10% of sale price | §4161(a)(1)(A) |
| Fishing rods and fishing poles (and component parts) | 110 | 10% of sale price, not more than $10 per article | §4161(a)(1)(B) |
| Electric outboard motors | 42 | 3% of sale price | §4161(a)(2) |
| Fishing tackle boxes | 114 | 3% of sale price | §4161(a)(3) |

Parts or accessories sold on or in connection with a taxable article are treated as part of it (§4161(a)(4)).

### What's taxable (§4162(a))

- Fishing rods and poles (and component parts), fishing reels
- Fly fishing lines and other fishing lines not over 130 pounds test
- Fishing spears, spear guns, spear tips
- Terminal tackle: leaders, artificial lures, artificial baits, artificial flies, hooks, bobbers, sinkers, snaps, drayles, swivels
- Fishing supplies and accessories: fish stringers, creels, tackle boxes, bags, baskets, and other containers designed to hold fish, portable bait containers, fishing vests, landing nets, gaff hooks, hook disgorgers, dressing for fishing lines and artificial flies
- Fishing tip-ups and tilts; fishing rod belts, rodholders, harnesses, fish fighting chairs, outriggers, downriggers
- Electric outboard boat motors (3%)

### What's NOT taxable

- Natural bait (worms, minnows, etc.), and terminal tackle designed for lines over 130 pounds test
- Boats themselves (even fishing boats)
- Gas-powered outboard motors (only electric outboard motors are listed in §4162(a)(9))
- Sonar devices / fish finders: not in the §4162(a) list
- Generic outdoor equipment not on the §4162(a) list
- Used or second-hand equipment, and resale of tax-paid equipment by unrelated retailers. Exception: a person related to the manufacturer (§465(b)(3)(C)) who resells an article owes a second tax unless the constructive sale price rules applied to the manufacturer's sale; a credit for the tax previously paid is available with documentation (§4162(b); Pub. 510 "Certain equipment resale")

### Sale price computation

Sale price = total consideration for the article (Pub. 510 ch. 5; §4216):
- **Include** charges for coverings or containers and for packing the article ready for shipment.
- **Exclude** the manufacturers excise tax itself; transportation charges pursuant to the sale; delivery, insurance, installation, and dealer preparation charges; discounts, rebates, and allowances actually granted; qualifying local advertising charges; and warranty charges paid at the purchaser's option (a mandatory warranty charge is included).

For sales to related parties, a **constructive sale price** applies (§4216(b)). Manufacturers transferring to controlled affiliates should not use a discounted transfer price as the tax base.

### Reporting and payment

Report the quarter's total tax on the IRS No. 41, 110, 42, or 114 line in Part II. No Schedule A, no semimonthly deposits; pay any balance with the return. Keep sale-by-sale records (date, buyer, article, price, tax) so the quarterly totals can be reconstructed.

### Common sport fishing scenarios

1. **Domestic manufacturer sells to a retailer**: 10% of sale price on IRS No. 41 (rods and poles on IRS No. 110 with the $10 cap; tackle boxes 3% on IRS No. 114). File Form 720 for the quarter; no Schedule A.

2. **Importer brings finished rods from overseas**: the importer is the manufacturer for §4161 purposes. Tax is 10% of its sale price, capped at $10 per rod (IRS No. 110).

3. **E-commerce store buys components, assembles rods, sells direct-to-consumer**: the assembler can be the manufacturer. Tax is on its sale price, capped at $10 per rod (IRS No. 110).

4. **Retail store buys rods from a manufacturer**: the retail store does NOT file; the manufacturer paid the §4161 tax. (State sales tax on resale is not Form 720.)

5. **Used / second-hand rods**: not taxable.

## Archery equipment (§4161(b))

### Tax base and rate

| Item | IRS No. | Rate | Source |
|------|---------|------|--------|
| Bows with a peak draw weight of 30 pounds or more | 44 | 11% of sale price | §4161(b)(1)(A) |
| Parts or accessories suitable for inclusion in or attachment to a taxable bow | 44 | 11% of sale price | §4161(b)(1)(B)(i) |
| Quivers, broadheads, and points suitable for use with taxable arrows | 44 | 11% of sale price | §4161(b)(1)(B)(ii) |
| Arrow shafts (sold separately or incorporated in a finished or unfinished product) | 106 | $0.63 per shaft for 2025 (Rev. Proc. 2024-40 §2.44); $0.65 per shaft for 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.43) | §4161(b)(2) |

The arrow shaft amount is 39 cents adjusted for inflation each calendar year after 2005 (§4161(b)(2)(C)). Use the amount for the calendar year of the sale and re-check the Rev. Proc. each year.

### What's taxable

- Bows with a peak draw weight of 30 pounds or more (compound, recurve, longbow, takedown)
- Parts and accessories suitable for inclusion in or attachment to a taxable bow, whether or not sold with the bow (§4161(b)(1)(B)(i))
- Quivers, broadheads, and points (11%)
- Arrow shafts of a type used for an arrow that, once assembled, is 18 inches or longer, or shorter than 18 inches but suitable for use with a taxable bow (per shaft)

### What's NOT taxable

- Bows with a peak draw weight under 30 pounds (children's and many beginner bows), and parts and accessories suitable only for them
- All-natural wood shafts with no laminations or spine enhancement, for arrows 5/16 inch or less in diameter and not suitable for a taxable bow (§4161(b)(2)(B))
- Targets, target stands, archery clothing not described in §4161(b)
- Native Indian handicraft bows and arrow shafts made by Indians on reservations, in Indian schools, or under U.S. jurisdiction in Alaska (Pub. 510 "Exemptions")
- Crossbows are not named in §4161(b) or Pub. 510. Don't decide their status; ASK the user to confirm with a CPA or IRS guidance before reporting or omitting them.

No tax is imposed under §4161(b) on an article taxable under §4161(a) (§4161(b)(3)).

### Per-shaft arrow tax (§4161(b)(2))

The tax is per shaft, not a percentage of price. A $5 shaft and a $50 shaft both owe $0.65 in 2026. It applies to shafts of any material (other than the exempt all-natural wood shafts) sold by themselves or as part of an assembled arrow. Points and broadheads on an assembled arrow are separately taxable at 11% under IRS No. 44 when their manufacturer, producer, or importer sells them.

### Reporting and payment

Archery taxes are Part II lines (IRS Nos. 44 and 106). Pub. 510: "Pay this tax with Form 720. No tax deposits are required." No Schedule A.

### Common archery scenarios

1. **Bow manufacturer sells a compound bow with a 60 lb peak draw weight**: 11% of sale price on IRS No. 44.

2. **Bow manufacturer sells a youth bow with a 25 lb peak draw weight**: not taxable (under the 30 lb threshold).

3. **Arrow shaft producer sells 1,000 carbon shafts at $4 each in 2026**: tax = 1,000 × $0.65 = $650 on IRS No. 106. NOT 11% of $4,000.

4. **Importer sells assembled arrows at $100 per dozen in 2026**: shaft tax = 12 × $0.65 = $7.80 per dozen. If the importer also imported the points or broadheads, they are taxable at 11% of the price allocable to them on IRS No. 44. ASK how the invoice allocates price between shafts and points before computing.

5. **Custom bow builder sells 1 bow (peak draw weight 50 lb) at $1,500**: 11% × $1,500 = $165 on IRS No. 44.

## Joint scenarios (sport fishing + archery)

An importer of both fishing rods and bows files ONE Form 720 with both IRS Nos. (110 and 44). Each line is computed separately; both feed Part II line 2 and Part III line 3.

## Common §4161 mistakes

1. **Treating §4161 taxes as Part I**: they are Part II lines (41, 110, 42, 114, 44, 106). They don't go on Schedule A and no deposits are required (Pub. 510 ch. 5).

2. **Putting fishing rods on IRS No. 41**: rods and poles are IRS No. 110, capped at $10 per article. A $180 rod owes $10, not $18.

3. **Wrong rate or line for tackle boxes**: tackle boxes are 3% on IRS No. 114 (§4161(a)(3)), not 10% and not IRS No. 42.

4. **Wrong rate or line for electric outboard motors**: 3% on IRS No. 42 (§4161(a)(2)). Gas outboards aren't taxed at all.

5. **Counting bows under 30 lb peak draw weight as taxable**.

6. **Charging 11% on the assembled arrow price instead of the per-shaft amount** ($0.65 in 2026).

7. **Constructive sale price for related-party transfers**: a manufacturer that sells to its own affiliate at a discounted transfer price understates the base; use the constructive sale price (§4216(b)).

8. **Used/second-hand confusion**: §4161 applies to the manufacturer's, producer's, or importer's sale, not to resale by unrelated persons (related-person resale of sport fishing equipment is the exception, §4162(b)).

9. **State sales tax confusion**: the federal manufacturers tax is separate from state sales tax. Both may apply.

## Audit defense for §4161 filers

The IRS examines manufacturers excise tax periodically. Common documentation needed:

1. Sales records showing sale price, date, buyer, and article for each sale
2. Customer list with related-party flags
3. Sale price adjustments (excluded transportation, discounts, optional warranties) with support
4. Form 720 quarterly filings and payment confirmations
5. Inventory records distinguishing taxable vs non-taxable items, and IRS No. 41 vs 110 vs 42 vs 114
6. For arrows: shaft count records by calendar year
7. For bows: peak draw weight specifications confirming the 30 lb threshold
8. For tackle boxes: classification support (3% vs 10%)

Keep records supporting the tax, claims, and exemptions for at least 4 years from the latest of the date the tax became due, the date it was paid, or the date a claim was filed (i720 "Recordkeeping").
