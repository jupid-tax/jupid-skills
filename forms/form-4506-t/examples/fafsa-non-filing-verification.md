# Example: College Student Needs Verification of Non-filing Letter for FAFSA

A complete walkthrough for a student whose financial-aid office asks for an IRS Verification of Non-filing Letter (VNF). The student's parent earned below the filing threshold and did not file a federal tax return for the year. Form 4506-T (Rev. April 2025) Line 7 produces the VNF. Note the federal rule first: for IRS non-filers, the 2025–26 FSA Handbook (Application and Verification Guide, Ch. 4) accepts a signed non-filing statement plus W-2s, so an IRS letter is only needed when the school insists on one.

## The filer

- **Name**: Jasmine Carter (the parent — she is the one who didn't file and whose VNF the school needs)
- **Filing status**: She would have filed as Head of Household if required, but her gross income was below the HoH filing threshold for the year, so she was not required to file
- **Tax year requested**: 2023 (the FAFSA year for academic year 2025–2026 uses 2023 income data — the "prior-prior year" rule)
- **State of residence**: Georgia
- **Purpose**: Her daughter Aliyah's college financial-aid office at Georgia State University asked for a Verification of Non-filing Letter from the IRS confirming Jasmine did not file a 2023 federal return. Aliyah's FAFSA was selected for verification. The agent first asked Jasmine to confirm with the school that a signed non-filing statement plus her W-2 (the federal minimum for IRS non-filers) would not do; the school said its process still asks for the IRS letter.

## The situation

Aliyah filled out the 2025–2026 FAFSA and was selected for verification. The school's verification process asks Jasmine, as the contributor parent, for a Verification of Non-filing Letter because she did not file.

Jasmine did not file a 2023 return because her 2023 gross income was below the HoH filing threshold for someone under 65 ($20,800 for 2023, Pub. 501 (2023) Table 1). She earned about $19,400 from a part-time job; her employer withheld federal income tax, but the threshold for filing was higher than her income, so she had no filing obligation. (She could have filed to claim the withholding back as a refund, but she didn't.)

## Step 1 — Try the Individual Online Account

Jasmine first tries her IRS online account, which offers VNFs (current tax year after June 15; prior three tax years anytime). She cannot complete ID.me verification: her only photo ID expired last month, and ID.me requires a valid government-issued photo ID.

Get Transcript by Mail and 800-908-9946 do not offer VNFs, so she falls back to Form 4506-T sent by mail.

## Step 2 — Confirm transcript type

The school requires a **Verification of Non-filing Letter**. This maps to **Line 7** on Form 4506-T.

The VNF states that the IRS has no record of a processed Form 1040-series return for the requested year as of the date of the request; it does not say whether she was required to file (irs.gov transcript types page).

## Step 3 — Confirm tax year

For the 2025–2026 academic-year FAFSA, the income data referenced is **tax year 2023** (FAFSA uses prior-prior year income under the FAFSA Simplification Act).

Format on Line 9: **12/31/2023** (calendar-year).

**Year-availability note**: Form 4506-T line 7 says current-year requests are available only after June 15 and "there are no availability restrictions on prior year requests." Online, VNFs cover the prior three tax years; irs.gov says to use Form 4506-T for older years. Tax year 2023 is a prior year, so Line 7 works directly.

## Step 4 — Confirm form number filed

Line 6 form number: **1040** (the type of return she would have filed if required).

## Step 5 — Determine routing

Per the "Chart for individual transcripts" on page 2 of Form 4506-T (Rev. April 2025), a filer who lived in **Georgia** goes to the **Austin RAIVS Team**: Internal Revenue Service, RAIVS Team, Stop 6716 AUSC, Austin, TX 73301 (fax 855-587-9604). (Georgia routes to Kansas City only in the "all other transcripts" chart, which covers returns other than the Form 1040 series, W-2, and 1099.)

Jasmine chose mail because she has time before the school's verification deadline (approximately 6 weeks).

## Step 6 — Customer file number and delivery

Line 5 is only an optional customer file number (up to 10 digits printed on the letter); the IRS cannot mail the VNF to the school. Jasmine leaves **Line 5 blank** and will upload the letter to the school's verification portal herself.

The IRS mails only to the address of record. Her last return (2022) shows the Cascade Road address, and she has not told the IRS about her move, so she files Form 8822 first, as the form instructs when lines 3 and 4 differ.

## Year-availability note for older VNFs

The online account offers VNFs for the current tax year (after June 15) and the prior three tax years. For older years, irs.gov says to use Form 4506-T, and the form's line 7 says there are no availability restrictions on prior-year requests. If the IRS cannot process an old-year request, call 800-908-9946 or visit a Taxpayer Assistance Center.

## Why each non-obvious choice

**Why Line 7 and not Line 6a?** Line 6a (Return Transcript) is for *filed* returns. Jasmine did not file a 2023 return, so a Return Transcript would return "no record" — which is technically equivalent to the VNF, but schools specifically request the VNF document because it is an explicit IRS letter, not a "no record" empty transcript response. Line 7 produces the explicit VNF.

**Why do Line 4 and Form 8822 matter here?** Jasmine moved after her 2022 return. Line 4 must show the address on that last return. Because the IRS mails transcripts and letters only to the address of record, she files Form 8822 so the letter reaches her current address.

**Why use prior-prior year (2023 for 2025–2026 academic year)?** The FAFSA Simplification Act and prior FAFSA rules use **prior-prior year (PPY)** income data. A FAFSA submitted in fall 2024 for the 2025–2026 academic year asks about tax year 2023 income (the PPY). This lets families file FAFSA earlier in the cycle without waiting for the most recent year's tax return. The VNF must therefore be for tax year 2023, not 2024.

**Why didn't Jasmine just file a $0 return to avoid this hassle?** She could have. Filing a "zero return" (a return showing income below the threshold) would have produced a Tax Return Transcript via Line 6a, which the school accepts. But she didn't, so she now needs the VNF. (The agent might suggest filing a zero return for future years to avoid repeated VNF requests if Aliyah is a multi-year student.)

**Is the VNF limited to 4 years?** Only online (prior three tax years). On Form 4506-T there is no restriction on prior-year requests. The FAFSA prior-prior year rule means schools rarely need anything older than two years before the award year.

**Could Jasmine use the online account instead?** Yes, once she has a valid photo ID for ID.me: the online account offers the VNF for the prior three tax years and could save weeks. Verify with the financial-aid office which format it accepts.

**Why doesn't the agent suggest Get Transcript by Mail for VNFs?** Get Transcript by Mail (and 800-908-9946) delivers only Tax Return Transcripts and Tax Account Transcripts to the address on file. VNFs require the online account or Form 4506-T.

## What if the IRS returns "no record found" but the school still rejects it?

The VNF *is* the school's expected document. If the school's verification worksheet specifically asks for "IRS Verification of Non-filing Letter," then the VNF Jasmine receives — even if it says "we have no record of a tax return filed" — is the correct document. She uploads it as-is.

If the school's intake clerk rejects it because they don't recognize the format, Jasmine can:
1. Point to the IRS letterhead and the explicit "no return filed" language
2. Ask the financial-aid office to escalate to a verifier who knows the FAFSA verification rules
3. Reference the FSA Handbook, Application and Verification Guide, Ch. 4, which for IRS non-filers requires only a signed non-filing statement plus W-2s — so the IRS letter more than meets the federal requirement

The VNF is the correct document; rejection is usually a clerical issue, not a problem with the IRS response.
