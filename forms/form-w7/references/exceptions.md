# W-7 Exceptions — When the W-7 Can Be Filed Alone

The default rule is that Form W-7 must be filed **with** a federal tax return that requires the ITIN. This makes sense in most cases — the ITIN exists to enable tax filing.

But the IRS recognizes several situations where a person needs an ITIN before filing a return, or where no return is needed at all. These are the **Exceptions** (1-5) listed in Form W-7 instructions. When applying under an exception, the W-7 is filed **alone** with supporting evidence, not with a 1040 / 1040-NR.

The agent's job: identify which exception applies, gather the right supporting evidence, and skip the tax return attachment. Every Exceptions Table carries the same condition: the federal tax withholding and/or information reporting must take place within the current tax year. The exception is written on the dotted line next to box h by number, letter, and category (e.g., "Exception 1d-Pension Income").

---

## Exception 1 — Passive income: third-party withholding or tax treaty benefits

**Who** (Instructions for Form W-7, Exceptions Tables, Exception #1):
- 1(a) Partners of a U.S. or foreign partnership that invests in the U.S. and owns assets generating income subject to IRS information reporting and federal tax withholding
- 1(b) Nonresident aliens who opened an interest-bearing bank deposit account generating income effectively connected with their U.S. trade or business, subject to information reporting and/or withholding
- 1(c) Resident aliens for tax purposes who opened an interest-bearing bank deposit account subject to information reporting and/or withholding
- 1(d) Individuals receiving distributions during the current tax year (pensions, annuities, rental income, royalties, dividends, etc.) who must give an ITIN to the withholding agent (investment company, insurance company, financial institution, etc.)

Information returns involved may include Form 1042-S, 1099-INT, 1099-MISC, Form 8805, and Schedule K-1 (Form 1065).

**Required evidence** (by sub-category):
- 1(a): a copy of the portion of the partnership or LLC agreement showing the partnership's EIN and that the applicant is a partner
- 1(b), 1(c): an original signed letter from the bank on its letterhead, showing the applicant's name and stating the account is subject to information reporting and/or withholding on interest in the current tax year
- 1(d): an original document or signed letter from the withholding agent on letterhead, showing the applicant's name and verifying that an ITIN is required for current-year distributions subject to reporting and/or withholding (self-generated income statements only with a copy of the contract or a letter with a postmarked envelope from the withholding agent)

**Limits**: Not available if the applicant is required to file a federal tax return. Applicants paid for personal services performed in the U.S., or holding a U.S. visa valid for employment, must first apply for an SSN and attach the SSA denial letter.

**Reason code**: Box (a) + box (h) if a treaty benefit is claimed (enter "Exception 1x-<category>", treaty country, and article); otherwise box (h) with the designation (e.g., "Exception 1a-Partnership Income").

## Exception 2 — Other income

**Who** (Exceptions Tables, Exception #2):
- 2(a) Wages, salary, compensation, and honoraria: individuals claiming treaty exemption or a reduced rate who will submit Form 8233 to the payer
- 2(b) Scholarships, fellowships, and grants, claiming treaty benefits: individuals who will submit Form W-8BEN to the withholding agent
- 2(c) Scholarships, fellowships, and grants, not claiming treaty benefits: noncompensatory income subject to information reporting and/or withholding in the current year
- 2(d) Gambling income: nonresident visitors claiming a treaty rate who use a gaming official serving as an IRS ITIN Acceptance Agent

**Required evidence** (summary; follow the table for the sub-category):
- 2(a): letter of employment from the payer, the employment contract, or a letter requesting the speaking engagement; treaty information on the W-7; a copy of the withholding agent's portion of Form 8233
- 2(b): letter or official notification from the college or university awarding the grant (or a contract with it); passport showing a valid U.S. visa; treaty information on the W-7; a copy of the Form W-8BEN given to the withholding agent; SSA denial letter, or a DSO/RO letter for F-1, J-1, or M-1 students who won't work
- 2(c): award letter or contract; passport with a valid U.S. visa (not needed if the foreign address is in Canada or Mexico); original DSO/RO letter stating the income is subject to reporting and/or withholding; SSA denial letter or DSO/RO no-employment letter
- 2(d): the W-7 must be submitted through the gaming official acting as Acceptance Agent. Without one, file Form 1040-NR at year end with the W-7 and a copy of Form 1042-S
- SEVP-approved institutions may submit a certification letter for F, J, or M students and their spouses and dependents instead of original ID documents (2(b) and 2(c))

**Reason code**: Box (a) or (f) + box (h) with the designation (e.g., "Exception 2b-Scholarship Income and claiming tax treaty benefits", "Exception 2c-Scholarship Income", "Exception 2d-Gambling Winnings").

## Exception 3 — Mortgage interest: third-party reporting

**Who**: Individuals with a home mortgage loan on real property located in the United States that is subject to third-party reporting of mortgage interest (Form 1098, IRC §6050H).

Examples:
- Foreign buyer of a U.S. home financed with a U.S. mortgage whose lender will issue Form 1098
- Foreign property owner who refinances a U.S. home mortgage

**Required evidence**: documentation showing evidence of a home mortgage loan on U.S. real property, such as a copy of the contract of sale or similar documentation.

**Reason code**: Box (h), "Exception 3-Mortgage Interest".

---

## Exception 4 — Disposition by a foreign person of a U.S. real property interest: third-party withholding

**Who**: An individual who is a party to the disposition of a U.S. real property interest by a foreign person (the foreign seller, or the buyer or other transferee acting as withholding agent), or who has a notice of non-recognition from the transferor under Regulations section 1.1445-2(d)(2). Under IRC §1445 (FIRPTA), the buyer generally withholds 15% of the amount realized and reports it on Forms 8288 / 8288-A.

Examples:
- Foreign individual selling a U.S. vacation home
- U.S. buyer acquiring property from a foreign seller who needs an ITIN to complete Form 8288
- Foreign seller applying for a withholding certificate on Form 8288-B

**Required evidence**:
- A completed Form 8288, Form 8288-A, or Form 8288-B; and either
- A copy of the real estate sales contract, Settlement Statement (HUD-1), or Closing Disclosure, or, for a notice of non-recognition, documents evidencing the transaction (date, parties, type of transfer)
- Sellers attach copies of the sales contract (or HUD-1 / Closing Disclosure) and the Forms 8288 and 8288-A submitted by the buyer

**Reason code**: Box (h), "Exception 4".

**Note**: FIRPTA situations are tax-strategically important because the §1445 withholding (15% of the amount realized) often exceeds the tax on the actual gain. The seller files Form 1040-NR after closing to claim a refund. The ITIN application happens before that filing — that's why Exception 4 lets the W-7 go alone.

## Exception 5 — Reporting obligations under T.D. 9363

**Who**: A non-U.S. representative of a foreign corporation who needs an ITIN to meet the corporation's e-filing requirements under Treasury Decision 9363.

Less common; mostly for officers of foreign corporations that must e-file.

**Required evidence**: an original signed letter from the employer on corporate letterhead stating that an ITIN is needed for T.D. 9363 and that the applicant has been designated as the person responsible for ensuring compliance with IRS information-reporting requirements.

**Reason code**: Box (h), "Exception 5, T.D. 9363".

## Exception decision tree

```
Is the applicant filing their own 1040 / 1040-NR?
  YES → No exception; default rule applies (W-7 with return)
  NO  ↓
    Does the applicant have current-year passive income subject to third-party
    withholding/reporting or covered by a treaty (partnership, interest, pension, rent, royalty, dividend)?
      YES → Exception 1
      NO  ↓
        Does the applicant have treaty-covered wages/honoraria (Form 8233), scholarship /
        fellowship / grant income, or treaty-covered gambling winnings?
          YES → Exception 2
          NO  ↓
            Does the applicant have a home mortgage loan on U.S. real property reported on Form 1098?
              YES → Exception 3
              NO  ↓
                Is the applicant a party to a disposition of a U.S. real property interest by a foreign person (FIRPTA)?
                  YES → Exception 4
                  NO  ↓
                    Is the applicant a non-U.S. representative of a foreign corporation needing an ITIN for T.D. 9363 e-filing?
                      YES → Exception 5
                      NO  → No exception applies; W-7 cannot be filed alone
```

## When NOT under an exception — the default rule

If no exception applies, the W-7 must be filed with a tax return that requires the ITIN. Common cases:

- Spouse on MFJ return → W-7 attached to the primary filer's 1040 → reason code (e)
- Dependent claimed on someone's 1040 → W-7 attached to the primary filer's 1040 → reason code (d)
- Nonresident alien with U.S. business income → W-7 attached to 1040-NR → reason code (b)
- Resident alien (substantial presence) with U.S. income → W-7 attached to 1040 → reason code (c)

The W-7 is on top of the return packet. The return cannot be e-filed when an ITIN is being applied for; mail to the **IRS Austin ITIN Operation** (different from regular 1040 mailing addresses).

## Common mistakes with exceptions

1. **Claiming Exception 4 (FIRPTA) without the required documents** → needs Form 8288, 8288-A, or 8288-B plus the sales contract, HUD-1, or Closing Disclosure
2. **Filing W-7 alone hoping to "pre-issue" an ITIN** before having a tax filing obligation → IRS rejects; ITIN is issued only when there's a documented need (filing or exception)
3. **Confusing Exception 2 (treaty-exempt income) with reason code (a) on the form** → Box (a) is the reason code for "claiming treaty benefit"; Exception 2 is the basis for filing without a tax return. Both apply together: check (a) and (h), and enter the Exception 2 designation on the dotted line next to box h.
4. **Treating Exception 3 (mortgage interest) as automatic for any foreign property buyer** → Exception 3 is specifically for a home mortgage loan on U.S. real property whose interest the lender reports on Form 1098 (§6050H). If the foreign buyer is also receiving rental income, they likely need a 1040-NR (no exception).
