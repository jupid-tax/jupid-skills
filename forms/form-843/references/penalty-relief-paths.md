# Penalty Relief Paths: AEP, First Time Abate, Reasonable Cause, Written Advice

How the IRS decides whether to remove a penalty, in the order it considers the grounds, with the evidence each ground needs. Load this file before choosing line 7 on Form 843 or drafting line 8.

## 1. Order of consideration

IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1: "penalty relief under Administrative Waivers, including FTA, is to be considered and applied before reasonable cause." If FTA criteria are met, the IRS applies FTA and tells the taxpayer the penalty was removed for compliance history, not for the reasonable cause statement.

Work through the grounds in this order and stop at the first one that fits:

1. **The account is wrong.** The return was filed on time or the tax was paid on time. Send proof (certified mail receipt, e-file acceptance, cancelled check front and back). The penalty appeal page lists this as its own eligibility path.
2. **Statutory exception or erroneous written advice** (IRC §6404(f), section 5 below).
3. **Automatic Exemption from Penalty (AEP)** for 2025 tax year and 2026 quarterly returns onward (section 2).
4. **First Time Abate** for earlier periods and periods not considered for AEP (section 3).
5. **Reasonable cause** (section 4).

A user may hold strong reasonable-cause facts and still be granted FTA instead; the IRS chooses the order, not the taxpayer.

## 2. Automatic Exemption from Penalty (AEP), starting summer 2026

From the IRS FTA page (14-Jul-2026): "FTA is transitioning to a new relief called Automatic Exemption from Penalty (AEP), starting Summer 2026."

- Applies to 2025 tax year returns and later, and 2026 quarterly returns and later.
- Eligible return series: Forms 1040, 1065, 1120; Forms 940, 941, 943, 944, 945; Form CT-1.
- Applied when the original return completes processing. If IRS records show timely compliance for the prior three years (or 12 consecutive quarters), the IRS does not assess failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, or failure-to-deposit penalties and sends a letter saying so.
- No taxpayer action is needed. The taxpayer still owes unpaid tax, interest, and any penalty not covered.
- Under AEP the failure-to-pay penalty "does not accrue and is not assessed on unpaid tax"; under FTA it "may continue to accrue until the tax is fully paid" (comparison table on the same page).
- "If you receive a notice showing an assessed penalty but believe you should have qualified for this relief, contact us."

**Agent rule:** for a 2025-or-later annual return (or a 2026-or-later quarterly return) of an eligible series, ask whether the user received an AEP letter or a penalty notice. If a penalty was assessed and the user believes AEP should have applied, route to the phone number on the notice first. Form 843 is still available if the call does not resolve it.

The comparison table says FTA "Applies to eligible 2025 tax year and 2026 quarterly returns (not considered for AEP), plus all prior years/periods." So a 2025 return that AEP did not cover can still be considered for FTA.

## 3. First Time Abate (FTA)

### Penalties covered

IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1(1) and the IRS FTA page:

| Penalty | IRC section |
|---|---|
| Failure to file: tax returns | 6651(a)(1) |
| Failure to file: partnership returns | 6698(a)(1) |
| Failure to file: S corporation returns | 6699(a)(1) |
| Failure to pay tax shown on the return | 6651(a)(2) |
| Failure to pay tax required to be shown, after notice and demand | 6651(a)(3) |
| Failure to deposit | 6656 |

FTA does not cover the accuracy-related penalty (§6662), the estimated tax penalty (§6654/§6655), information return penalties, or any penalty not in this table.

### Criteria (current IRS wording)

From the IRS FTA page, "How to qualify for the relief":

1. The same return type, as the original return, was timely filed for the prior three years (or 12 consecutive quarters), and
2. Either no penalty (except the estimated tax penalty) was assessed, or a penalty was assessed but later abated for reasonable cause or IRS error, and
3. Business taxpayers also meet: the failure-to-deposit penalty was not waived four or more times in the prior three years (or 12 quarters), and was not charged for EFTPS avoidance.

From IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1(4), stated in IRS terms:
- The taxpayer filed the same return for the 3 years preceding the penalized period, unless there was no filing requirement.
- Those returns were processed and their modules have no unreversed penalties (except an estimated tax penalty) for which a notice was issued.
- Those modules have no penalties removed under FTA or tolerance (Penalty Reason Codes 018, 020, 021). **A prior FTA inside the lookback disqualifies.**
- For Form 1040 penalties, the history of both spouses' SSNs is checked when filing status or the spouse changed in the lookback years.

### Limits

- One tax period only. If four quarters of Form 941 were penalized after a clean history, FTA applies to the first quarter; the other three need reasonable cause (IRM example).
- Not penalty-specific: any unreversed penalty (other than the estimated tax penalty) on a lookback module blocks FTA for every penalty on the period under consideration.
- Not a "first request" rule: "the FTA waiver is not applicable based solely on a taxpayer's first request for penalty relief."
- Not available for event-based or infrequent returns, the daily delinquency penalty, or information reporting attached to another filing. IRM examples: Forms 706, 709, 990, 1099 series, 8300, 3520, 3520-A, 5471, 5472.
- If the tax is not paid in full, the IRS abates the failure-to-pay penalty assessed so far, but it keeps accruing on the unpaid tax; after full payment the additional failure-to-pay penalty can be removed under FTA (IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1, paragraphs on RC 062).

The companion blog post lists "all currently required returns filed" and "tax paid or arranged to pay" as FTA conditions. Neither the current IRS FTA page nor IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1 lists them. Do not tell a user they fail FTA on those grounds alone. Do tell them that unpaid tax keeps accruing failure-to-pay penalty and interest.

### How to request

From the IRS FTA page: "Call the IRS at the toll-free number found in the top right corner of your notice or letter, or send a written statement or Form 843 ... to the address specified in the Form 843 instructions." The taxpayer does not need to name FTA or provide documents; the IRS checks its records. IRM: "Taxpayers are not required to specifically request penalty relief under the FTA waiver to be eligible."

## 4. Reasonable cause

### The standard

"You may qualify for penalty relief if you demonstrate that you exercised ordinary care and prudence and were nevertheless unable to file your return or pay your taxes on time" (IRS reasonable cause page). The decision is case by case on all facts.

"Reasonable cause doesn't apply to certain penalties such as the estimated tax penalty" (same page).

### Grounds the IRS lists as possibly valid (failure to file or pay)

- Fires, natural disasters, or civil disturbances
- Inability to get records
- Death, serious illness, or unavoidable absence of the taxpayer or immediate family
- System issues that delayed a timely electronic filing or payment

IRM sections the penalty appeal page points to: 20.1.1.3.2.2.1 (death, serious illness, unavoidable absence), 20.1.1.3.2.2.2 (fire, casualty, natural disaster), 20.1.1.3.2.2.3 (unable to obtain records), 20.1.1.3.2.2.4 (mistake was made), 20.1.1.3.2.2.5 (erroneous advice or reliance).

### Grounds that generally do not qualify (IRS reasonable cause page)

- Reliance on a tax professional (the taxpayer remains responsible)
- Lack of knowledge
- Mistakes and oversights (unless other facts show an effort to comply)
- Lack of funds, by itself (other facts showing reasonable care may still help)

When a user's reason is on this list, say so plainly before drafting, and ask whether other facts exist.

### Accuracy-related and information return penalties

- Accuracy-related (§6662): reasonable cause and good faith under Treas. Reg. §1.6664-4. Factors: efforts to report correctly, complexity, the taxpayer's experience, steps taken to get advice; for reliance on an advisor, whether full information was given and the advisor was competent.
- Information returns: Treas. Reg. §301.6724-1. The filer must show responsible conduct before and after the failure plus significant mitigating factors or events beyond its control.

### What the explanation must contain

From the IRS reasonable cause page:
1. What happened and when it happened.
2. How the situation prevented filing or paying on time.
3. What attempts were made to file or pay.
4. When the circumstance ended and how fast the taxpayer complied afterward (the IRS asks for start and end dates for illness).

Evidence the page lists: hospital or court records or a doctor's letter with start and end dates; documentation of disasters; copies of relevant letters; receipts and forms.

Address each penalty separately. IRM 20.1.1 says each penalty is a different type of failure; if a request names only one penalty, only that penalty is considered. Facts that explain a late filing do not automatically explain a late payment.

### Phone first, then paper

"We may reduce or remove some penalties over the phone. Call the toll-free number at the top right corner of your notice ... If you don't qualify for either or we can't approve your relief over the phone, you can request relief in writing with Form 843" (IRS reasonable cause page).

## 5. Erroneous written advice (IRC §6404(f))

The IRS abates a penalty or addition to tax caused by erroneous written advice from an IRS employee only if (i843; §6404(f)(2)):
1. The taxpayer reasonably relied on the written advice,
2. The advice responded to a specific written request by the taxpayer or an authorized representative, and
3. The penalty did not result from the taxpayer's failure to provide adequate or accurate information.

Form 843: complete lines 1–4; line 3 if paid; line 6 the penalty's Code section; line 7 box b. Attach: the written request, the IRS written advice, and any report of tax adjustments identifying the penalty. Timing: within the period allowed for collecting the penalty, or, if paid, within the refund claim period. Regulation: Treas. Reg. §301.6404-3.

## 6. Estimated tax penalty (§6654) is a different track

- Not covered by FTA, AEP, or reasonable cause.
- Waivers exist only for casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstance, or retirement after age 62 or disability with reasonable cause (Instructions for Form 2210, "Waiver of Penalty").
- On the current return: Form 2210 Part II box A or B with a statement. See `../../form-2210/SKILL.md`.
- Already billed: the IRS page for the individual estimated tax penalty says to send a written explanation, signed under penalties of perjury, to the address at the top of the notice (https://www.irs.gov/payments/underpayment-of-estimated-tax-by-individuals-penalty).

## 7. If the request is denied

- Penalty abatement denials are usually sent on Letter 854C, "Penalty Waiver or Abatement Disallowed/Appeals Procedure Explained" (IRM 20.1.1.3.5.3).
- "You generally have 30 days from the date of the rejection letter to file your request for an appeal. Refer to your rejection letter for the specific deadline" (IRS penalty appeal page).
- Publication 5 (Rev. 4-2021): if the total tax and penalties for each period in the letter is $25,000 or less, a small case request (the form in the letter, Form 12203, or a brief written statement) is allowed; above $25,000 for any period, a formal written protest is required for all periods, with facts, law, and a penalties-of-perjury statement.
- Publication 4576 describes the penalty appeals process.
