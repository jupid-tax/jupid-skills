# Credit Reduction States and Schedule A (Form 940)

When Schedule A (Form 940) is required, how to fill it, the credit reduction rates for 2025, and the status of 2026. Built from the 2025 Schedule A (Form 940) and its page 2 instructions (Created 11/12/25), the 2026 draft Schedule A (Created 3/18/26), the 2025 Instructions for Form 940 (I940), the IRS FUTA credit reduction page, and the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) FUTA credit reduction tables.

---

## 1. What a credit reduction state is

A state (including DC, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands) that borrowed from the federal unemployment trust fund to pay benefits and has an outstanding balance on January 1 of two consecutive years, and does not repay in full by November 10 of the second year. Employers paying wages subject to that state's unemployment law lose part of the 5.4% credit (IRS, "FUTA credit reduction" page; IRC §3302(c)(2); DOL FUTA Credit Reductions page).

Schedule of reductions (IRS FUTA credit reduction page): 0.3% for the first year, another 0.3% for the second year, and another 0.3% for each later year the loan is not repaid. Additional "add-on" reductions may apply beginning with the third and fifth years (IRC §3302(c)(2)). DOL announces the final list after the November 10 deadline each year.

Effect: an employer in a state with a 0.3% reduction takes a 5.1% credit instead of 5.4%, for an effective rate of 0.9% (IRS FUTA credit reduction page).

## 3. Who completes Schedule A

- Every multi-state employer (Form 940 line 1b checked), even if no state has a credit reduction.
- Every employer that paid wages subject to a credit reduction state's unemployment law (line 2 checked), even a single-state employer.

(I940, lines 1b and 2; IRS FUTA credit reduction page.)

If Form 940 line 9 applies (all FUTA wages excluded from state unemployment tax), line 11 is skipped, but a multi-state employer still completes Schedule A (I940, line 9).

## 4. Filling Schedule A (Schedule A page 2 instructions)

Header: EIN and name (not trade name).

**Step 1.** Put an "X" in the box of every state (including DC, PR, VI) in which you had to pay state unemployment tax this year, even if that state's credit reduction rate is zero. Make sure you hold a state reporting number for each.

**Step 2.** For each state with a reduction rate greater than zero:

- Enter in the FUTA Taxable Wages box the total FUTA taxable wages paid in that state. The FUTA wage base for all states is $7,000 per employee, in total across states.
- Exclude wages that were excluded from that state's unemployment tax. Example from the instructions: $5,000 FUTA taxable wages in a credit reduction state of which $1,000 was excluded from state unemployment tax → enter $4,000.
- Do not enter state unemployment wages (the state base) in this box.
- Enter the reduction rate and multiply; enter the result in the Credit Reduction box.

**Step 3.** Add all Credit Reduction boxes; enter the total in Total Credit Reduction and on Form 940, line 11.

## 5. The official examples, recomputed

**Example 1 (single credit reduction state).** Three employees each paid $20,000 in State A (rate 0.012), no exempt payments.
Total payments $60,000; excess over $7,000 = 3 × ($20,000 − $7,000) = $39,000; FUTA taxable wages in State A = $60,000 − $0 − $39,000 = $21,000; credit reduction = $21,000 × 0.012 = $252.00 → Form 940 line 11. (Form 940 line 8 would be $21,000 × 0.006 = $126.00, so line 12 = $378.00 if nothing else applies.)

**Example 2 (employee moves between states).** Mary Smith is paid $48,000 ($4,000 a month), no exempt payments. She works in State B (no reduction) in January and in State C (credit reduction) from February 1. State B FUTA wages = $4,000. State C payments are $44,000, but only $7,000 − $4,000 = $3,000 of FUTA wage base remains, so State C FUTA taxable wages = $3,000. Multiply $3,000 by State C's rate.

## 6. Timing: the credit reduction is a fourth-quarter liability

Credit reduction liability is treated as incurred in the fourth quarter. It is due with the fourth-quarter deposit (by the Form 940 due date: February 2, 2026 for 2025; February 1, 2027 for 2026). Employers may deposit the expected amount earlier, but it is not due until then, and in Part 5 it is recorded on line 16d (I940, "Fourth quarter liabilities" and line 16 Tip; IRS FUTA credit reduction page; Pub. 15 (2026), section 14).

Consequence: an employer whose regular 0.6% liability never exceeded $500 can cross $500 in the fourth quarter because of the reduction and then must deposit the whole cumulative amount by the Form 940 due date.

## 7. Agent procedure

1. Ask: "In which states did you have to pay state unemployment tax this year?" List them.
2. If more than one state, check line 1b. If any state's reduction rate for the year is above zero, check line 2.
3. For each credit reduction state, compute FUTA taxable wages employee by employee, honoring the $7,000 combined base across states and removing wages the state excluded.
4. Multiply by that year's final rate. If the year's final rates are not yet published, stop and say so; do not file with potential rates.
5. Put the total on line 11 and on Part 5 line 16d.
6. Re-run the deposit test for the fourth quarter including the credit reduction.

## 8. Validation

- [ ] Every state with a state unemployment account is checked on Schedule A (multi-state employers)
- [ ] FUTA Taxable Wages box ≤ $7,000 × employees who worked in that state
- [ ] Sum across states of FUTA taxable wages ≤ Form 940 line 7
- [ ] Credit reduction = FUTA taxable wages × rate, to the cent
- [ ] Total Credit Reduction = Form 940 line 11
- [ ] Line 16d includes the credit reduction
