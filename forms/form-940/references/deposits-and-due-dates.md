# FUTA Deposits, Quarterly Liability, and Due Dates

The $500 deposit rule, how to compute quarterly FUTA liability for Part 5, deposit and filing dates for the 2025 and 2026 returns, payment methods, and penalties. Built from the 2025 Instructions for Form 940 (I940) "When Must You Deposit Your FUTA Tax?", "How Do You Figure Your FUTA Tax Liability for Each Quarter?", "How Must You Deposit Your FUTA Tax?", "When Must You File Form 940?", line 14, and line 16; the 2026 draft instructions (I940-26D, dated Sep 22, 2026); Publication 15 (2026) sections 11, 12, and 14; Treas. Reg. §31.6302(c)-3; IRC §6656.

---

## 1. The $500 rule

- Figure FUTA liability each quarter.
- If cumulative undeposited liability at the end of a quarter is **$500 or less**, carry it to the next quarter. No deposit is due.
- If cumulative undeposited liability is **more than $500**, deposit all of it by the **last day of the month after the quarter**.
- After a deposit, the running total restarts. A later quarter at $500 or less is carried again.
- **Fourth quarter:** if fourth-quarter liability plus undeposited amounts from earlier quarters is more than $500, deposit the entire amount by the Form 940 due date. If it is $500 or less, either deposit it or pay it with Form 940 by the due date.
- **Credit reduction** liability is incurred in the fourth quarter and must be included with the fourth-quarter deposit.
- If total FUTA for the year is more than $500, at least one deposit is required.

(I940, "When Must You Deposit Your FUTA Tax?"; Treas. Reg. §31.6302(c)-3(a)(2): no deposit is required for a period unless the tax for the period plus undeposited tax for prior periods exceeds $500; Pub. 15 (2026), section 14.)

Household employers who report on Schedule H do not deposit FUTA; they pay it with Form 1040 (Pub. 15 (2026), section 14, "Household employees"). If they move household wages onto Form 941/944 and Form 940, the deposit rules above apply.

## 2. Computing quarterly liability

For each employee, take the first $7,000 of FUTA wages (after removing exempt payments) in the order paid, and assign each dollar to the quarter it was paid. Quarterly liability = quarter's FUTA wages × 0.006, assuming the full credit (I940, "Figure Your Tax Liability").

If some FUTA wages are not subject to state unemployment tax, those wages carry the full 6.0% rate for deposit purposes (I940 example below). Stop counting an employee once FUTA wages reach $7,000 for the year (Pub. 15 (2026), section 14).

**IRS example 1.** Q1 payments: Mary $11,000, George $2,000, Jane $4,000; none exempt. FUTA wages = $7,000 + $2,000 + $4,000 = $13,000; × 0.006 = $78. No deposit; carry $78 to Q2.

**IRS example 2.** Same payments, but Mary and George are corporate officers whose wages the state excludes. Mary and George: $9,000 × 0.060 = $540. Jane: $4,000 × 0.006 = $24. Q1 liability $564 → more than $500 → deposit $564 by April 30.

## 3. Deposit due dates

| Undeposited FUTA more than $500 on... | Deposit by | 2025 calendar | 2026 calendar |
|---|---|---|---|
| March 31 | April 30 | Wed Apr 30, 2025 | Thu Apr 30, 2026 |
| June 30 | July 31 | Thu Jul 31, 2025 | Fri Jul 31, 2026 |
| September 30 | October 31 | Fri Oct 31, 2025 | Sat Oct 31, 2026 → **Mon Nov 2, 2026** |
| December 31 | January 31 (the Form 940 due date) | Sat Jan 31, 2026 → **Mon Feb 2, 2026** | Sun Jan 31, 2027 → **Mon Feb 1, 2027** |

If a due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the deposit is timely if made by the next business day. Legal holidays for deposits are only those in the District of Columbia (I940, "Timeliness of federal tax deposits"; Pub. 15 (2026), section 11). The fourth-quarter dates match the Form 940 due dates printed in I940 (February 2, 2026) and I940-26D (February 1, 2027). The weekday shifts in the table were computed from the calendar; re-check DC holidays in Pub. 15 section 11 for the year.

## 4. How to deposit

All federal tax deposits must be made by electronic funds transfer (EFT): EFTPS, IRS Direct Pay, or the IRS business tax account, or a same-day wire arranged with a bank through the Federal Tax Collection Service. A tax professional, bank, or payroll service may deposit on the employer's behalf. EFTPS, Direct Pay, and the business tax account are free. EFTPS accepts same-day payments of $1 million or less submitted before 3:00 p.m. Eastern on a business day. New businesses are pre-enrolled in EFTPS when they apply for an EIN (I940, "How Must You Deposit Your FUTA Tax?").

Do not use Form 940-V, a credit or debit card, or electronic funds withdrawal (EFW) for amounts that were required to be deposited (I940, Reminders and line 14; Form 940-V instructions).

## 5. Part 5 (lines 16a–17)

Complete only if Form 940 line 12 is more than $500. Enter each quarter's liability incurred, not deposits. Leave a zero quarter blank. Figure 16d as line 17 (copied from line 12) minus 16a, 16b, and 16c, so the credit reduction and any line 9/10 adjustment not already assigned land in the fourth quarter (I940, line 16). Line 17 must equal line 12.

IRS example: FUTA on wages paid March 28 was $200 (no deposit, ≤ $500); FUTA on wages paid June 28 was $400; cumulative $600 → deposit $600 by July 31. Part 5: 16a $200, 16b $400.

## 6. Filing due dates

| Return | Due date | Later date if every FUTA deposit was made when due |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 Form 940 | February 2, 2026 | February 10, 2026 |
| 2026 Form 940 | February 1, 2027 | February 10, 2027 |

Sources: I940, "When Must You File Form 940?"; I940-26D, same heading. A paper return is timely if properly addressed, with sufficient postage, and postmarked by USPS (or sent by an IRS-designated private delivery service) on or before the due date. A due date on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday moves to the next business day.

The later date is granted to employers who "deposited all your FUTA tax when it was due". When no deposit was ever required (line 12 of $500 or less, paid with the return), plan to file by the regular due date; if the user wants to rely on the later date in that case, flag it for a CPA rather than assuming.

State unemployment tax is "on time" for the FUTA credit if paid by the Form 940 due date (February 10 only if that is your due date) (I940, "Credit for State Unemployment Tax Paid to a State Unemployment Fund").

## 7. Penalties

Failure to deposit (IRC §6656; Pub. 15 (2026), section 11, "Deposit Penalties"):

| Deposit | Penalty |
|---|---|
| 1 to 5 days late | 2% |
| 6 to 15 days late | 5% |
| 16 or more days late, but before 10 days from the first IRS notice | 10% |
| Required deposits paid directly to the IRS or with the return instead of by EFT deposit | 10% |
| Still unpaid more than 10 days after the first IRS notice, or the day notice and demand for immediate payment is received, whichever is earlier | 15% |

Failure to file and failure to pay (Pub. 15 (2026), section 12, "Penalties"; IRC §6651): 5% of unpaid tax per month or part of a month the return is late, generally capped at 25%; 0.5% per month for late payment, capped at 25%; when both apply in a month, the failure-to-file penalty is reduced by the failure-to-pay penalty. Reasonable cause can avoid penalties.

Relief: request abatement of assessed penalties on Form 843, not on Form 940; answer a penalty notice with an explanation rather than attaching one to the return (I940, "How Can You Avoid Penalties and Interest?"). The 2026 draft instructions announce an Automatic Exemption from Penalty (AEP) program beginning July 2026 for the 2025 tax year and later: a filer who timely filed Form 940 and timely paid for the prior 3 years is not assessed failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, or failure-to-deposit penalties (except failure to deposit by EFT); AEP replaces First Time Abate (I940-26D, What's New). Confirm in the final 2026 instructions.

Installment agreement: if line 14 cannot be paid, an online installment agreement is available when the amount owed is $25,000 or less and can be paid within 24 months (I940, line 14).
