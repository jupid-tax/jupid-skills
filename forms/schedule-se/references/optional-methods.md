# Schedule SE Optional Methods (Part II)

The farm optional method (Part II line 15) and nonfarm optional method (Part II lines 16–17) let a low-income self-employed filer voluntarily pay SE tax on a higher base than their actual net earnings would dictate. The point: **earn Social Security credits** in a year that would otherwise generate few or none.

They usually **increase** SE tax owed. The Schedule SE instructions list three reasons to consider them: Social Security credit accrual; a possibly larger earned income credit, additional child tax credit, or child and dependent care credit (when net SE earnings figured without the optional methods are less than $7,240 for 2025); and the self-employed health insurance deduction. Figure the return with and without the election.

---

## Why optional methods exist

To qualify for Social Security retirement, disability, survivor, or Medicare benefits, a worker needs to earn **40 credits over their lifetime** (max 4 per year). One credit in 2025 = $1,810 in covered earnings (verify yearly at https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/QC.html). If a self-employed filer has a bad year and net SE earnings are below $1,810 × 4 = $7,240, they earn fewer than 4 credits.

The optional methods let them voluntarily report up to $7,240 (2025, Schedule SE line 14) in elective SE earnings to keep the credit accrual going. For 2026 the quarter-of-coverage amount is $1,890 (4 credits = $7,560); read the 2026 optional-method figures from the 2026 Schedule SE when it is released.

**Lifetime maximum**: nonfarm optional method may be used in only **5 tax years total**, not necessarily consecutive (IRC §1402(a), flush language; Schedule SE instructions). Farm optional method has no lifetime limit.

---

## Farm Optional Method (Part II line 15)

### Eligibility (one-of)

- **Gross farm income of $10,860 or less** (2025; verify each year), OR
- **Net farm profits less than $7,840** (2025; verify each year)

There is no requirement of prior-year self-employment for the farm method.

### How much you elect

```
Line 15 = min($7,240, 2/3 × gross farm income)   (Line 14 prints $7,240 for 2025)
```

The $7,240 cap is the elective amount, not a multiplier on it. Flows to Line 4b without the 0.9235 factor. Skip Lines 1a and 1b when using this method.

### When it makes sense

A farmer whose primary income is a "bad crop year" — gross farm income low, net even lower or negative. Reporting $7,240 in SE earnings (2025) generates 4 SS credits for the year.

**Tax cost**: $7,240 × 0.153 = **~$1,108 in SE tax** (Line 4b is not multiplied by 0.9235). The filer is paying ~$1,108 to keep their 40-credit clock ticking.

### Eligibility math example

| Farmer | Gross farm income | Net farm profit | Eligible? |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| A | $8,000 | $4,000 | Yes (gross ≤ $10,860) |
| B | $12,000 | $5,000 | Yes (net < $7,840) |
| C | $50,000 | $40,000 | No (both above thresholds) |
| D | $0 | −$2,000 (loss) | Yes (gross ≤ $10,860) |

## Nonfarm Optional Method (Part II lines 16–17)

### Eligibility (all-of)

- **Net nonfarm profits less than $7,840** (2025; verify yearly). Net nonfarm profits = Schedule C line 31 + nonfarm K-1 (Form 1065) Box 14 Code A
- **Net nonfarm profits less than 72.189% of gross nonfarm income** (gross = Schedule C line 7 + K-1 Box 14 Code C)
- **2-of-3 prior years** had actual net SE earnings ≥ $400 (regularity test, IRC §1402(h))
- **Lifetime cap**: not used in 5 prior tax years

The 72.189% rule: net nonfarm profits ÷ gross nonfarm income must be **below** 72.189%. The statute frames it as net earnings less than 66⅔% of gross income (IRC §1402(a), flush language); the form's 72.189% applies that test before the 0.9235 factor (66.667% ÷ 0.9235 ≈ 72.189%).

### How much you elect

```
Line 16 = Line 14 − Line 15
Line 17 = min(Line 16, 2/3 × gross nonfarm income)
```

With no farm election, the cap is $7,240 (2025). Line 17 flows to Line 4b without the 0.9235 factor. Skip Line 2 when using this method. The user can't report less than actual net nonfarm earnings.

### When it makes sense

A regular self-employed worker — bookkeeper, freelance writer, consultant — who has a slow year (net nonfarm profits below $7,840 in 2025). The optional method keeps them at 4 SS credits for the year.

**Tax cost**: same ~$1,108 in SE tax for the full $7,240 (2025).

### Lifetime cap mechanics

The 5-time lifetime cap is per filer. Track it: ask the user "Have you elected the non-farm optional method in any prior tax year? In how many?" If they don't know, ask them to check prior-year Schedule SE copies (Part II line 17 filled in) or request account transcripts; the SSA earnings statement (https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/) shows credited SE earnings by year but does not label the method used.

If the filer is on their 6th attempt, the election is invalid; remove it and revert to actual net earnings.

### 2-of-3 prior years test

Prior 3 tax years before the current one must include 2 with net SE earnings ≥ $400. Examples for filing tax year 2025 (prior 3 = 2022, 2023, 2024):

| Filer | 2022 SE | 2023 SE | 2024 SE | Eligible? |
|-------|---------|---------|---------|-----------|
| A | $5,000 | $8,000 | $0 | Yes (2022 + 2023) |
| B | $0 | $0 | $5,000 | No (only 1 of 3) |
| C | $401 | $402 | $403 | Yes (all 3 ≥ $400) |
| D | $399 | $0 | $399 | No (none ≥ $400) |

## Combined elections (rare)

A filer with **both** farm and non-farm income may elect both methods in the same year. Line 4b = Line 15 + Line 17, and the combined amount cannot exceed $7,240 in 2025 (Line 16 = Line 14 − Line 15 enforces this). The user can report less than total actual net farm + nonfarm earnings, but not less than actual net nonfarm earnings alone.

---

## Tax cost vs. SS credit benefit

The cost-benefit of an optional method election:

- **Cost**: SE tax on the elected amount. For 2025, electing the full $7,240 base produces ~$1,108 SE tax (subject to wage-base interaction).
- **Benefit**: 4 SS credits for the year, accelerating eligibility for SS retirement / disability / Medicare. Each credit is worth nothing on its own — only the 40-credit lifetime threshold matters.

Decision rules:
- **Filer is < 40 credits and < age 62**: usually worth electing if the year would otherwise generate 0 credits
- **Filer already has 40+ credits**: rarely worth it (extra credits don't increase SS benefit; the benefit calculation uses the highest 35 years of indexed earnings)
- **Filer is in their last few credits before 40**: worth it
- **Filer is on disability**: maintaining recent credits matters for disability eligibility (DIB requires 20 credits in the 40 quarters before disability) — see SSA disability rules

The agent should **not** make this decision. Surface the trade-off to the user and let them decide.

## Verify before filing

- Gross farm income threshold (2025: $10,860)
- Net farm profit threshold (2025: $7,840)
- Net nonfarm profits threshold (2025: $7,840, and less than 72.189% of gross nonfarm income)
- Optional method cap (2025: $7,240, Schedule SE line 14)
- One SS credit dollar amount (2025: $1,810; 2026: $1,890)

The IRS prints these on Schedule SE Part II and in the Schedule SE instructions each tax year; they derive from the SSA quarter-of-coverage amount (IRC §1402(l)). Read the 2026 figures from the 2026 Schedule SE when it is released.
