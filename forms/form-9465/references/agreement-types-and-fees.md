# Agreement Types, Thresholds, and User Fees

Which kind of payment plan a Form 9465 request becomes, what each one requires, and what it costs. Use this file to decide whether Form 9465 is the right tool at all and which lines must be completed.

Sources (re-check each before use; fees and thresholds are year-dependent):
- Instructions for Form 9465 (Rev. July 2024): https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i9465.pdf

- Form 13844 (Rev. 2-2026), Application for Reduced User Fee for Installment Agreements: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f13844.pdf

## 1. Options before a long-term agreement

| Option | Who | Cost to set up | Source |
|--------|-----|----------------|--------|
| Pay in full now | Anyone | $0 | irs.gov payment plans page |
| Short-term payment plan (pay in 180 days or less) | Individuals online; anyone by phone, mail, or in person | $0 | irs.gov payment plans page |
| Long-term installment agreement (monthly) | See tiers below | $29 to $178 (table in section 4) | irs.gov payment plans page |

The instructions tell the user not to file Form 9465 if they can pay in full within 180 days; call 800-829-1040 instead and avoid the user fee (i9465, "Can you pay in full within 180 days?").

**Short-term threshold wording differs between sources. Flag it, do not resolve it:**
- i9465 (Rev. 7-2024): "If you owe **$100,000 or less**, you can apply for a short-term payment plan ... by using the OPA application."
- irs.gov payment plans page and OPA page: "You owe **less than $100,000** in combined tax, penalties and interest."

If the user's balance is exactly $100,000, tell them the sources disagree and have them try OPA or call 800-829-1040.

## 2. Long-term agreement tiers

### Guaranteed installment agreement — IRC §6159(c)

The IRS **must** accept the agreement if, on the date the individual offers it (statute text):
1. The liability is income tax (subtitle A) of an individual, and the aggregate liability **determined without regard to interest, penalties, additions to tax, and additional amounts** does not exceed **$10,000**;
2. During any of the preceding **5 taxable years**, the taxpayer (and spouse, if the liability is on a joint return) has not failed to file an income tax return, failed to pay tax shown on such a return, or entered into an installment agreement for income tax;
3. The IRS determines the taxpayer is financially unable to pay in full when due (and the taxpayer submits the information the IRS requires);
4. The agreement requires full payment within **3 years**; and
5. The taxpayer agrees to comply with the tax law while the agreement is in effect.

i9465 paraphrases the same test ("the tax you owe isn't more than $10,000"). The $10,000 test excludes penalties and interest; the agent must ask for the tax-only portion of the balance (an account transcript separates tax, penalty, and interest).

### Streamlined installment agreement — i9465 "Streamlined installment agreement"

Generally eligible if:
- Assessed tax liability is **$25,000 or less** (individual, in-business with income tax only, or out-of-business taxpayer); or
- Assessed tax liability is **$25,001 to $50,000** (individual or out-of-business sole proprietorship) **and** the taxpayer agrees to pay by **direct debit or payroll deduction**.

The proposed payment must pay the assessed liability in full **within 72 months or by the CSED, whichever is less**. The CSED is normally 10 years from the date of assessment but can be suspended or extended (i9465; IRS.gov/filing/time-irs-can-collect-tax). A streamlined agreement generally does not require a financial statement or a Notice of Federal Tax Lien.

The OPA page uses "$50,000 or less in combined tax, penalties and interest" for online long-term eligibility. Use the combined balance when deciding whether OPA is available.

### Partial payment installment agreement (PPIA) — i9465 "Partial payment installment agreement (PPIA)"

An agreement that will not pay the entire balance before the CSED. Requires a financial statement and supporting financial information, and is subject to future reviews; a review can raise, lower, or leave the payment unchanged. If line 11a does not pay in full by the CSED, the request may be considered for a PPIA (i9465 "Line 11a").

When a PPIA looks likely, also tell the user that an offer in compromise exists and route the comparison to `../../form-656/SKILL.md`. Do not recommend one over the other; that is a CPA decision.

### Over $50,000 (non-streamlined)

Form 9465 line 9 greater than $50,000: complete and attach **Form 433-F**, Collection Information Statement (Rev. 7-2024 is current: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f433f.pdf). The agent must collect 433-F data from the user; it never estimates income, assets, or expenses. Form 433-F's own instructions point living-expense entries to the IRS Collection Financial Standards: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/collection-financial-standards.

The OPA page notes that when a revised online payment does not meet the minimum, the IRS directs the user to Form 9465 with Form 433-F, or to Form 433-H (Installment Agreement Request and Collection Information Statement). If the IRS sends the user Form 433-H, use the form the IRS asked for.

## 3. Who may not use Form 9465

From i9465 "Who should not use this form?" and "Bankruptcy or offer in compromise":
- Can pay in full within 180 days → call 800-829-1040 or use OPA short-term.
- Wants to apply online → OPA (IRS.gov/OPA).
- Business still operating that owes employment or unemployment taxes → call the number on the most recent notice.
- In bankruptcy, or has a pending or accepted offer in compromise → do not file; call 800-829-1040 for the local Insolvency function or the office handling the offer.

Who should use it (i9465 "Who should use this form?"): an individual who owes income tax on Form 1040 or 1040-SR; who is or may be responsible for a trust fund recovery penalty; who owes employment taxes (Forms 941, 943, 940) of a sole proprietor business no longer operating; or who owes an individual shared responsibility payment.

Business entities: the OPA page states business accounts cannot apply online and must call the number on the notice or 800-829-4933. Sole proprietors and independent contractors apply as individuals.

Unfiled returns: the request is denied if any required tax return has not been filed (i9465, "How the Installment Agreement Works").

## 4. User fees (current)

**Use the irs.gov figures below, not the table in the July 2024 instructions.** The i9465 table (fees effective July 1, 2024) shows $22 for an online direct-debit agreement and $10 for an online reinstatement or restructure; irs.gov now lists $29 and $6. Source: irs.gov payment plans page, section "What are payment plan costs and fees? (updated March 3, 2026)"; matching figures on the OPA page.

| Plan | Apply online | Apply by phone, mail, or in person | Low-income |
|------|--------------|-------------------------------------|------------|
| Short-term (180 days or less) | $0 (individuals only) | $0 | — |
| Long-term, direct debit (DDIA) | **$29** | **$107** | Fee waived |
| Long-term, not direct debit | **$69** | **$178** | $43, may be reimbursed if conditions are met |

Payroll deduction (Form 2159): i9465 states the fee is $178 beginning July 1, 2024. The irs.gov page does not list payroll deduction separately. Tell the user the IRS will state the fee in its approval notice.

Form 9465 is a mail/paper channel, so a Form 9465 request pays the "phone, mail, or in person" column: $107 with direct debit, $178 without. The online column requires OPA.

The regulation at 26 CFR 300.1 still prints older amounts ($225 general, $107 direct debit, $149 online, $31 online direct debit, $43 low-income) last amended by T.D. 9798 (2016). The IRS currently charges the irs.gov amounts. Cite irs.gov for the fee and note the regulation only if the user asks why the numbers differ.

### Low-income taxpayers

Definition (i9465; irs.gov; Form 13844): an individual whose adjusted gross income for the most recent tax year available is **at or below 250% of the federal poverty guidelines**.

- Direct debit agreement → the IRS **waives** the user fee (lines 13a/13b).
- Unable to pay by direct debit → the IRS charges the reduced **$43** fee and **reimburses** it when the agreement is completed, but only if line 13c is checked.
- Neither 13a/13b nor 13c → treated as able but choosing not to pay electronically; no reimbursement.
- The IRS system usually identifies low-income status automatically. If it does not, the user files **Form 13844** within **30 days** of the date on the installment agreement acceptance letter; later applications are not considered (Form 13844, item 6).

## 5. What an agreement does not stop

- **Interest** continues on unpaid tax and penalties until paid in full (i9465 "Other costs"). Rates change quarterly: https://www.irs.gov/payments/quarterly-interest-rates.
- **Failure-to-pay penalty** continues. Under IRC §6651(h), for an individual who filed the return on or before its due date (including extensions), the rate is 0.25% instead of 0.5% per month for each month an installment agreement is in effect. A late-filed return does not get the reduced rate.
- **Refund offset**: any refund is applied to the balance, and the regular monthly payment is still due (i9465 "How the Installment Agreement Works").
- **Notice of Federal Tax Lien**: may be filed to protect the government's interest; generally not filed with guaranteed or streamlined agreements, but can be in certain situations; never filed for the individual shared responsibility payment (i9465).

## 6. Collection protections and the statute

- While a request is pending and while an agreement is in effect, the IRS generally is prohibited from levying, and the time to collect is suspended or prolonged (i9465 note under "Guaranteed installment agreement"; irs.gov payment plans page).
- If a requested agreement is rejected, the collection period is suspended for 30 days; same on a proposed termination after default; and during an appeal of either (irs.gov payment plans page).
- Appeals of a rejection or proposed termination go through the Collection Appeals Program (CAP); see Publication 1660 (linked from the About Form 9465 page).

## 7. Decision table for the agent

| Facts (all from notices/transcripts and the user) | Route |
|---|---|
| Can pay within 180 days | OPA short-term or call; no Form 9465 |
| Individual, all returns filed, combined balance ≤ $50,000, can use IRS Online Account | OPA long-term ($29 DDIA / $69 otherwise); Form 9465 only if the user prefers paper or is attaching to a paper return |

| Line 9 > $50,000 | Form 9465 + Form 433-F |
| Payment cannot reach line 10 | Form 9465 + 11b box + Form 433-F (possible PPIA) |
| Operating business with employment taxes, or corporation/partnership | Call; not Form 9465 |
| Bankruptcy, or OIC pending/accepted | Call 800-829-1040; not Form 9465 |
| Any required return unfiled | Stop; returns first |
