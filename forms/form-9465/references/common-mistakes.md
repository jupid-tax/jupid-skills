# Form 9465 — Common Mistakes

Errors that get a Form 9465 request delayed, denied, or more expensive than necessary. Each entry: the mistake, why it happens, the fix, and the source. Sources are the Form 9465 (Rev. September 2020), Instructions for Form 9465 (Rev. July 2024) at https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i9465.pdf, and the IRS payment plans page at https://www.irs.gov/payments/payment-plans-installment-agreements (fees updated March 3, 2026).

## 1. Filing paper when the online application would work

**Mistake:** The user mails Form 9465 for a $14,000 balance with all returns filed.

**Why it happens:** The user does not know the Online Payment Agreement (OPA) exists or assumes paper is required.

**Fix:** If the user is an individual, all required returns are filed, and the combined balance is $50,000 or less, offer OPA first. Same agreement type, lower fee: $29 online with direct debit vs $107 by mail with direct debit; $69 online without direct debit vs $178 by mail. If the user still wants paper (no online account, attaching to a paper return), proceed.

**Source:** Form 9465 tip at top of page 1; i9465 "Applying online"; irs.gov payment plans page fee table.

## 2. Using the fee table printed in the July 2024 instructions

**Mistake:** The draft tells the user the online direct-debit fee is $22 and an online reinstatement is $10.

**Why it happens:** Those are the i9465 (Rev. 7-2024) figures effective July 1, 2024.

**Fix:** Quote the irs.gov payment plans page (updated March 3, 2026): $29 online DDIA, $107 phone/mail DDIA, $69 online non-DDIA, $178 phone/mail non-DDIA, $6 online revise/reinstate, $89 phone/mail revise. Re-check the page on the day of the request.

**Source:** i9465 "Installment agreement user fees" table vs irs.gov payment plans page.

## 3. Estimating the balance

**Mistake:** Line 5 is "about $18,000" from the user's memory, or the agent adds its own interest estimate.

**Why it happens:** The user does not have the notice in hand.

**Fix:** Line 5 is the amount shown on the return(s) or notice(s). Ask for the notice or have the user pull an account transcript or check the IRS Online Account. Other charges not on a return or notice go on line 6. Never add projected interest; the form does not ask for it.

**Source:** Form 9465 lines 5–6; i9465 "Line 5" and "Line 6".

## 4. Leaving out balances already in an existing agreement

**Mistake:** The user has a 2023 agreement and requests a new one for 2025 only, listing only the 2025 balance.

**Fix:** Line 6 includes additional balances "even if the amounts are included in an existing installment agreement", and line 11a is the total proposed monthly payment for all liabilities.

**Source:** Form 9465 lines 6 and 11a.

## 5. Balance between $25,001 and $50,000 with no line 13, no line 14, no Form 433-F

**Mistake:** Line 9 is $38,412, line 11a beats line 10, and the user wants to pay by check. Nothing on line 13 or 14 and no Form 433-F.

**Fix:** For this tier the user must either complete lines 13a/13b (direct debit), check line 14 and attach Form 2159 (payroll deduction), or complete Form 433-F. Ask which the user chooses.

**Source:** Form 9465 line 11b second bullet; i9465 "Line 9" caution; i9465 "Streamlined installment agreement".

## 6. Line 9 over $50,000 with no Form 433-F

**Fix:** Attach a completed Form 433-F (Rev. 7-2024). Collect every figure from the user's statements; never estimate.

**Source:** Form 9465 line 11b third bullet; i9465 "Line 11b".

## 7. Proposed payment below line 10 with the box unchecked

**Mistake:** Line 11a is $210 against a line 10 of $284.17, line 11b blank, box unchecked.

**Fix:** Ask whether the user can raise the payment to at least line 10. If yes, enter it on 11b. If no, check the box under 11b and attach Form 433-F. Tell the user a payment that will not pay in full before the CSED may be treated as a partial payment installment agreement, which requires a financial statement and periodic reviews.

**Source:** Form 9465 line 11b; i9465 "Line 11a" and "Partial payment installment agreement (PPIA)".

## 8. Treating "line 9 ÷ 72" as the payment that clears the debt in 72 months

**Mistake:** The user picks exactly the line 10 amount believing the balance will be gone in six years.

**Why it happens:** Line 10 divides the current balance by 72 and ignores the interest and failure-to-pay penalty that keep accruing.

**Fix:** State in the draft that interest and penalties continue until the balance is paid in full, so a line-10 payment leaves a balance after 72 months. Suggest the user pay as much as possible, as the form itself says. Do not compute a projection without the current quarterly interest rate from https://www.irs.gov/payments/quarterly-interest-rates.

**Source:** Form 9465 line 11a text; i9465 "Other costs".

## 9. Payment day after the 28th

**Fix:** Line 12 accepts the 1st through the 28th only.

**Source:** Form 9465 line 12; i9465 "Line 12".

## 10. Routing number taken from a "payable through" check

**Fix:** If the check is payable through a different institution, get the routing number from the bank. Validate: 9 digits, first two 01–12 or 21–32. Account number: up to 17 characters, hyphens kept, spaces and symbols dropped, no check number.

**Source:** i9465 "Line 13a" and "Line 13b".

## 11. Joint return signed by one spouse

**Mistake:** Only one spouse signs a request for a joint-return balance with direct debit.

**Fix:** Both spouses sign. The direct debit is not approved unless both sign on a joint return.

**Source:** Form 9465 signature line ("If a joint return, both must sign"); i9465 caution under "Line 13b".

## 12. Filing with a return still unfiled

**Mistake:** The user requests an agreement for 2025 while the 2023 return was never filed.

**Fix:** Stop. The request will be denied if any required tax return has not been filed. Get the missing return filed first.

**Source:** i9465 "How the Installment Agreement Works".

## 13. Using Form 9465 for an operating business's payroll taxes

**Mistake:** A sole proprietor still in business with employees lists Form 941 balances.

**Fix:** Not eligible for Form 9465. The business calls the telephone number on its most recent notice. Line 2 is only for a business that is no longer operating.

**Source:** i9465 "Who should not use this form?"; Form 9465 line 2.

## 14. Filing while in bankruptcy or with an offer in compromise pending

**Fix:** Do not file Form 9465. Call 800-829-1040 for the local Insolvency function or the office handling the offer.

**Source:** i9465 "Bankruptcy or offer in compromise".

## 15. Sending a standalone request to the return-filing address

**Mistake:** The user already filed the return and mails Form 9465 alone to the address in the 1040 booklet.

**Fix:** Attach to the front of the return only when filing them together. A standalone request goes to the address in the i9465 table that matches the user's state and whether any year in the request includes Schedule C, E, or F. See `../filing.md`.

**Source:** i9465 "Where To File".

## 16. Expecting the 0.25% failure-to-pay rate on a late-filed return

**Fix:** The reduced 0.25% monthly rate during an installment agreement applies only to an individual who filed the return on or before its due date, including extensions.

**Source:** IRC §6651(h).

## 17. Missing the Form 13844 window

**Mistake:** A low-income user charged the full fee sends Form 13844 two months after the acceptance letter.

**Fix:** Form 13844 must be submitted within 30 days from the date on the installment agreement acceptance letter. Calendar the date when the letter arrives.

**Source:** Form 13844 (Rev. 2-2026), item 6.

## 18. Counting on refunds as payments

**Fix:** Refunds are applied to the balance, and the regular monthly payment is still due that month. Budget without them.

**Source:** i9465 "How the Installment Agreement Works".

## 19. Ignoring next year's taxes

**Mistake:** The user is on a plan and underpays estimated tax again; the new balance defaults the agreement.

**Fix:** Tell the user the agreement requires enough withholding or estimated tax so future returns are paid in full. Route to `../../form-1040-es/SKILL.md` or a Form W-4 update.

**Source:** i9465 "How the Installment Agreement Works" and "What happens if the taxpayer doesn't comply".
