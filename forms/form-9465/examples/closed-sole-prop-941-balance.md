# Example: Closed Sole Proprietorship With Form 941 and Form 1040 Balances ($25,001–$50,000)

A former café owner closed her sole proprietorship in 2025 and owes income tax plus two quarters of employment tax. The balance falls between $25,001 and $50,000, so to stay streamlined without Form 433-F she must pay by direct debit or payroll deduction. She is answering IRS notices, so the form goes standalone to the address in the Schedule C table.

Sources used: Form 9465 (Rev. 9-2020); Instructions for Form 9465 (Rev. 7-2024), sections "Who should use this form?", "Streamlined installment agreement", "Line 2", "Line 8", "Line 11b", "Where To File"; irs.gov payment plans and OPA pages.

## The user

- **Name**: Dana Kowalczyk (fictional)
- **Filing status**: Single
- **State**: Texas (San Antonio)
- **Former business**: Kowalczyk Coffee, sole proprietorship with two employees, closed March 31, 2025; filed final Form 941 for the quarter ending 03/31/2025
- **Now**: W-2 employee at a hospital cafeteria
- **Notices in hand**: a balance-due notice for 2024 Form 1040 and balance-due notices for Form 941, quarters ending 12/31/2024 and 03/31/2025

OPA: the irs.gov OPA page says sole proprietors apply as individuals and the combined balance is under $50,000, so the agent offered OPA first ($29 with direct debit vs $107 on paper). The page does not say whether the online tool covers Form 941 periods of a closed sole proprietorship, so the agent did not promise it would. She declined OPA because she wanted one written request covering the 1040 year and both 941 quarters, and she does not have an IRS Online Account. The agent logged her choice and proceeded with Form 9465.

## Line math

```
Line 5  = 17,642.18 + 9,315.40 + 6,088.55 = 33,046.13
Line 6  = 0.00
Line 7  = 33,046.13
Line 8  = 2,000.00   (check enclosed)
Line 9  = 33,046.13 − 2,000.00 = 31,046.13
Line 10 = 31,046.13 ÷ 72.0     = 431.20
Line 11a = 475.00 ≥ 431.20

Line 9 is over $25,000 and not over $50,000, and line 11a ≥ line 10:
→ Form 433-F not required, but line 13 (direct debit) or line 14 (payroll deduction) is required.
```

She chose direct debit over payroll deduction because she did not want her new employer involved (line 14 needs the employer to sign Form 2159).

## The check sent with the request (line 8)

Per i9465 "Line 8": a $2,000.00 check payable to "United States Treasury", with her name, address, SSN, daytime phone, and "2024 Form 1040" on it. She asked to apply it to the 2024 Form 1040 balance; the agent notes the IRS applies agreement payments "in the best interests of the United States" (i9465 "Other costs"), so the designation is a request, not a guarantee.

## Where it goes

She already filed every return and is responding to notices, so the form goes **by itself** to the i9465 address table. Her 2024 Form 1040 included **Schedule C**, so she uses the table "For taxpayers filing Form 1040 or 1040-SR with Schedule(s) C, E, or F for any tax year for which this installment agreement is being requested". Texas is in the Memphis row:

```

```

(Had no year in the request involved Schedule C, E, or F, Texas would have used the Doraville address in the other table.)

## Fee

- Form 9465 with direct debit: **$107** (irs.gov payment plans page, "Apply by phone, mail, or in-person").
- Low-income check: 2025 AGI $44,310, family unit size 1 → above the $39,900 Form 13844 (Rev. 2-2026) figure for one person in the 48 contiguous states. Not low-income.

## Notes in the deliverable

- Streamlined tier: $25,001–$50,000 for an individual or out-of-business sole proprietorship that agrees to direct debit or payroll deduction; payment must clear the assessed liability within 72 months or by the CSED, whichever is less (i9465). At $475 a month, $31,046.13 would take about 65.4 months before interest and penalties; with them it takes longer. Her 2025 failure-to-pay penalty rate depends on whether each return was filed on time (IRC §6651(h) applies the 0.25% rate only to timely filed individual returns). The agent does not project interest and tells her to pay above $475 when she can.
- Form 941 balances of a closed business can include trust fund amounts. As a sole proprietor she is personally liable for them already; nothing extra goes on Form 9465.
- She must keep her W-4 withholding high enough that 2026 does not create a new balance, or the agreement can default (i9465).
