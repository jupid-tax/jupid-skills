# Example: W-2 Employee With a Side Gig, Form 9465 Attached to a Paper Return

A single filer with wages and a small photography business files his 2025 Form 1040 on paper on extension and cannot pay the full balance. The balance is under $25,000, so lines 13 and 14 are optional and no Form 433-F is needed. The agent points out that the online application would cost less, and the user still chooses paper because he is mailing the return anyway.

Sources used: Form 9465 (Rev. 9-2020); Instructions for Form 9465 (Rev. 7-2024); irs.gov payment plans page (fees updated March 3, 2026); IRC §6159(c); IRC §6651(h).

## The user

- **Name**: Tomás Herrera (fictional)
- **Filing status**: Single, no dependents
- **State**: Ohio
- **Return**: 2025 Form 1040 with Schedule C (wedding photography side business), filed on paper under a valid extension on October 9, 2026 (extended due date October 15, 2026)
- **Prior history**: filed and paid on time for 2020–2024; never had an installment agreement
- **IRS Online Account**: none; he does not want to create one

## What the agent asked, and the answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is the "amount you owe" on the 2025 return? | $11,318 (Form 1040 amount-you-owe line, which includes a $214 estimated tax penalty from the penalty line) |
| Any other IRS balances, from notices or an existing plan? | None. He checked his last notice from 2023; it was paid. |
| Are all required returns filed? | Yes, 2025 is the latest; 2020–2024 were filed on time |
| Bankruptcy, or an offer in compromise pending? | No |
| Paying anything with the return? | $1,500 |
| How much can you pay each month? | $425 |
| Which day of the month? | The 15th (rent is due on the 1st) |
| How will you pay each month? | Direct debit from his checking account |
| Address new since the last return? | No |

## Routing decision

1. Can he pay $9,818 within 180 days? He says no. Short-term plan is out.
2. Individual, all returns filed, combined balance $9,818 ≤ $50,000 → he qualifies to apply online (irs.gov OPA page). The agent tells him the fees: online direct debit **$29** vs Form 9465 with direct debit **$107**, a **$78** difference (irs.gov payment plans page, updated March 3, 2026). He chooses paper anyway because he is mailing the return and does not want an online account. The agent proceeds.
3. Line 9 is under $25,000, so lines 13 and 14 are optional and Form 433-F is not required.
4. Guaranteed-agreement facts (IRC §6159(c)): tax-only balance after his payment is $11,318 − $214 penalty − $1,500 = $9,604, which is not more than $10,000; no failure to file or pay and no installment agreement in the prior 5 years; $425 a month clears $9,818 within 3 years (the 3-year minimum would be $9,818 ÷ 36 = $272.72). The IRS makes the "financially unable to pay in full" determination. The agent notes in the draft that the facts appear to meet the statutory test but does not label the agreement.

## Fee and cost notes in the deliverable

- User fee with Form 9465 and direct debit: **$107** (irs.gov payment plans page). Online with direct debit would have been **$29**.
- Low-income check: the agent asked for his 2025 AGI ($71,940) and family unit size (1). The Form 13844 (Rev. 2-2026) threshold for one person in the 48 contiguous states is $39,900. Not low-income; full fee applies.
- He filed by the extended due date, so once the agreement is in effect the failure-to-pay penalty runs at 0.25% a month instead of 0.5% (IRC §6651(h)). Interest continues on the unpaid balance (i9465 "Other costs"). The agent does not project interest.
- Without interest, $9,818 at $425 a month would take about 23.1 months; with interest and the penalty it takes longer. The draft says this in words, not as a schedule.

## Where it goes

Attach Form 9465 to the **front** of the 2025 Form 1040 and mail the return to the address in the Form 1040 instructions for an Ohio filer (i9465 "Where To File": "Attach Form 9465 to the front of your return and send it to the address shown in your tax return booklet"). The $1,500 goes with the return per the return instructions. The i9465 standalone tables do not apply because the form travels with the return.

Expect a reply in more than 30 days: the instructions say a request for tax due on a return filed after March 31 may take longer than the usual 30 days.

## Validation summary

- Math: line 7, line 9, line 10 recomputed; all match.
- Thresholds: line 9 $9,818 ≤ $25,000 → no line 13/14 requirement, no Form 433-F.
- Line 11a ≥ line 10 → 11b and the 433-F box left blank.
- Line 12 = 15 (within 1–28).
- Bank numbers: routing 9 digits with prefix 02 (within 01–12); account 11 characters. Masked in the draft.
- Signature: single filer; one signature required.
- Sanity: OPA was available and cheaper; user declined in writing in the chat. Logged.
