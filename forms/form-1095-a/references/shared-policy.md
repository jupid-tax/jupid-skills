# Shared Policy Allocation (Form 8962 Part IV)

When a single Form 1095-A covers individuals across two or more tax households, the policy amounts must be allocated. Form 8962 Part IV (Lines 30–34) handles this. Up to four allocations can appear.

## When does shared policy allocation apply?

Allocation is required when (1) the policy covered at least one individual in the user's tax family and at least one in another tax family, and (2) the user's Form 1095-A lists someone not in their tax family or omits a member of it, or the other family's 1095-A includes a member of the user's tax family (2025 Form 8962 instructions, Line 9). Common scenarios:

### Scenario 1 — Divorced or separated parents

A child is covered under one parent's Marketplace policy, but the OTHER parent claims the child as dependent on the tax return. The covering parent has a 1095-A that includes the child, but the child isn't on the covering parent's tax return.

### Scenario 2 — Unmarried parents

Two unmarried parents each have their own tax return. Their child is covered on one parent's Marketplace policy. The parent who claims the child as dependent on their tax return does NOT have the child on their own 1095-A. Allocation is required.

### Scenario 3 — Adult child filing own return

A 23-year-old child is covered under the parent's Marketplace policy but files their own tax return (parent does NOT claim as dependent). The child's coverage on the parent's 1095-A must be allocated.

### Scenario 4 — Multiple unrelated taxpayers on one policy

Two unrelated adults share a household and were enrolled together. They file separate returns. If the Form 1095-A issued does not match each one's tax family, they must allocate.

Three or more tax families on one policy in the same month: use the worksheets in Pub 974, "Allocation of Policy Amounts Among Three or More Taxpayers".

## How to allocate

The rule depends on the allocation situation (2025 Form 8962 instructions, Table 3 and Allocation Situations 1–4; Treas. Reg. §1.36B-4):

- **Situation 1 — divorced or legally separated during the year**: any agreed percentage (0%–100%), but the **same percentage for premium, SLCSP, and APTC**. No agreement → 50% each.
- **Situation 2 — married at year end, filing separately**: 50% to each spouse under the Situation 2 rules (column (g) only for a spouse who is not an applicable taxpayer).
- **Situation 3 — no APTC paid**: premiums allocated in proportion to each family's SLCSP premium; column (e) only.
- **Situation 4 — any other shared policy** (parents divorced in an earlier year, unmarried parents, an adult child filing their own return): any agreed percentage, which may differ by month, but the **same percentage for all three amounts in a month**. No agreement → the number of individuals enrolled by one taxpayer who are in the other taxpayer's tax family ÷ the total number enrolled.

Each column's percentages **must total 100%** across all sharing taxpayers. Enter them as decimals (e.g., "0.50"); 100% is entered as "100" and 0% as "-0-" (2025 instructions, Allocation Situation 4).

### Common allocations

**Default without agreement**: 50/50 only in Situations 1 and 2; otherwise the enrolled-individuals ratio above.

**100/0 to one party**: If both sharing taxpayers agree (Situations 1 and 4), one takes 100% and the other takes 0%.

**Asymmetric**: e.g., 60% to one and 40% to the other. Allowed in Situations 1 and 4, but never different percentages for different columns in the same month.

### Documenting the agreement

Best practice: get a written agreement signed by both parties before filing, and **both parties should use the same allocation on their respective Form 8962s**. Different allocations on different returns can lead to IRS correspondence.

## Filling Form 8962 Part IV

### Lines 30–33 — Up to four allocations

Each allocation row has six fields:
- (a) Policy number from Form 1095-A
- (b) SSN of other taxpayer
- (c) Allocation start month
- (d) Allocation stop month
- (e) Premium percentage allocation
- (f) SLCSP percentage allocation
- (g) APTC percentage allocation

If the same allocation applies all 12 months, enter "01" / "12" for start / stop.

### Line 34 — Have you completed all policy amount allocations?

Yes/No. "Yes" → multiply the 1095-A amounts by the allocation percentages, add any non-allocated policy amounts, and enter the combined monthly totals on Lines 12–23, columns (a), (b), and (f). "No" → see the Line 34 instructions for more than four allocations.

### After allocation, return to Part II Lines 12–23

Each monthly column on Lines 12–23 (the (a), (b), (f) columns) is multiplied by your allocation percentage before entering. So if your allocation is 0.60, and 1095-A January Column A = $1,200, you enter $720 (= $1,200 × 0.60) in Form 8962 Line 12 column (a). Part IV forces the monthly calculation (Line 10 = No).

Repeat for SLCSP (Column B → column (b)) and APTC (Column C → column (f)).

## Year-of-divorce special case

If you divorced during the year and were married at enrollment but file singly at year-end:
- Coverage months while married → both spouses' tax families share the policy → allocation needed
- Coverage months after divorce → each former spouse on separate return → may need partial-year allocation

For the months married, use Allocation Situation 1 (same percentage for all three amounts; 50% each without agreement). See the Form 8962 instructions, Part IV, Allocation Situation 1.

---

## What happens if allocations don't match between returns?

The IRS receives both returns and can compare Form 8962 Part IV with the Form 1095-A the Marketplace filed. If allocations differ, expect processing delays or IRS correspondence proposing an adjustment.

The way to avoid this: communicate with the other taxpayer before filing, agree on allocation, document the agreement, and both file using the same numbers.

## Allocation worksheet example

**Setup**: Two unmarried parents (David and Sarah) share a Marketplace policy that covers them and their daughter. Sarah claims daughter as dependent. David has 1095-A in his name; his Form 1095-A Part II shows David, Sarah, and daughter.

**They agree** (Allocation Situation 4): David takes 50% of premium, SLCSP, and APTC; Sarah takes 50%. APTC paid was $400/month total. Without an agreement, the default would be 2/3 to Sarah (David enrolled two people in Sarah's tax family, Sarah and the daughter, out of three enrolled).

**David's Form 8962 Part IV Line 30**:
- (a) Policy number: 1234567890
- (b) Sarah's SSN: ###-##-####
- (c) Start month: 01
- (d) Stop month: 12
- (e) Premium %: 0.50
- (f) SLCSP %: 0.50
- (g) APTC %: 0.50

**Sarah's Form 8962 Part IV Line 30**:
- (a) Policy number: 1234567890
- (b) David's SSN: ###-##-####
- (c) Start month: 01
- (d) Stop month: 12
- (e) Premium %: 0.50
- (f) SLCSP %: 0.50
- (g) APTC %: 0.50

Both use the same numbers but identify each other as the "other taxpayer." Each then computes Lines 12–23 monthly with the allocated amounts.
