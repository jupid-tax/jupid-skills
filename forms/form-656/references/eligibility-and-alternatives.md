# Eligibility, Offer Grounds, and Alternatives

Run this screen before any Form 433-A (OIC) arithmetic. A failed item means the offer will be returned, so the agent's output is a to-do list, not a draft. Sources: Form 656-B booklet (Rev. 4-2026), https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f656b.pdf; Form 656-L (Rev. 7-2026), https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f656l.pdf; IRS offer in compromise page, https://www.irs.gov/payments/offer-in-compromise.

---

## 1. Pre-checks (Form 656-B, page 1 "Are You Eligible?")

Before an offer can be considered, the taxpayer must:

1. File all tax returns they are legally required to file.
2. Have received a bill for at least one tax debt included in the offer.
3. Make all required estimated tax payments for the current year.
4. If a business owner with employees, make all required federal tax deposits for the current quarter and the two preceding quarters.

Ask about each, one question at a time. Typical tight questions:

- "Have you filed every federal return you were required to file, including this year's if it is past due? Any year you did not file because you thought you didn't have to?"
- "Have you received an IRS bill or balance-due notice for at least one of the years you want to include?"
- "Are your 2026 estimated tax payments up to date, or are you covered by withholding?" (route to [`../../form-1040-es/SKILL.md`](../../form-1040-es/SKILL.md) if not)
- "Do you have employees? Are this quarter's and the last two quarters' payroll tax deposits made?" (route to [`../../form-941/SKILL.md`](../../form-941/SKILL.md) if not)

Consequences printed in the booklet:

- Unfiled return found: the IRS applies any initial payment to the debt and returns only the offer and the application fee; no appeal (page 1).
- Returns filed but no bill yet: the offer and fee may be returned and the initial payment applied. Wait for a bill, then include a copy of any return filed within 10 weeks of the offer (page 2; Form 656 Section 6).
- The IRS offer page adds: a valid extension is required for a current-year return if applying for the current year.

## 2. Disqualifiers and items to resolve first (Form 656-B, pages 1 to 2)

| Situation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Taxpayer or business in an open bankruptcy proceeding | Not eligible; resolution generally happens inside the bankruptcy |
| Open audit or outstanding innocent spouse claim | Resolve before submitting |
| Deactivated ITIN | Offer not considered; reactivate with Form W-7 first |
| Business owes trust fund taxes | Not eligible unless the trust fund portion is paid or the IRS has made Trust Fund Recovery Penalty determinations on all potentially responsible parties; exception for victims of payroll service provider fraud or failure |
| All periods referred to the Department of Justice | Cannot be processed |
| Court-ordered restitution (restitution-based assessments), liabilities reduced to judgment, IRC §965(i) election liabilities | Cannot be compromised |
| Taxpayer can pay in full through an installment agreement and/or equity | Generally not accepted; see section 6 |

Section 9 of Form 433-A (OIC) asks directly about current bankruptcy; answer it truthfully.

## 3. The three Form 656 grounds (Form 656, Section 3)

Check exactly one:

1. **Doubt as to collectibility (DATC):** the taxpayer lacks the assets and income to pay the full liability and offers at least the Form 433-A (OIC) / 433-B (OIC) minimum. With special circumstances that prevent paying even the minimum, attach a detailed explanation.
2. **Effective tax administration — economic hardship:** the taxpayer owes the tax and could pay in full, but full payment would cause economic hardship. Individuals only. Detailed explanation required.
3. **Effective tax administration — public policy or equity:** the taxpayer owes and could pay, but exceptional circumstances (the form's example: a payroll service provider misappropriated withheld taxes) make collection inequitable. Detailed explanation required.

The booklet (page 1, "Can You Pay in Full?") says an ETA offer may be considered when full payment would create an economic hardship or full payment may be unjust. Whether facts support ETA is a judgment call; refer to a CPA, enrolled agent, attorney, or Low Income Taxpayer Clinic, and still complete the financial statement.

## 4. Doubt as to liability — Form 656-L (Rev. 7-2026)

Use when the taxpayer genuinely disputes the existence or amount of the tax (Form 656-L, page 2). Route here, then stop the 433-A (OIC) workflow: the financial-statement math does not apply.

- No application fee, no deposit, no payment with Form 656-L (page 2 and the checklist).
- Offer amount: what the taxpayer believes is correct; "You must submit an offer for $1.00 or more" (page 2 and checklist), while Section 3 of the form says "greater than $1.00". Offer more than $1.00 to satisfy both wordings.
- Required: a written explanation of why the tax is wrong, with supporting documents; otherwise the offer is returned (page 2).
- Not available if the liability was established by a final court decision or judgment, or the tax is based on current law (page 2); if the taxpayer is in an open bankruptcy, owes DOJ restitution, already has an accepted DATC or DATL offer for the same year and liability, or made a §965(i) election (page 2); or with a deactivated ITIN (page 3).
- Resolve pending matters first (notice responses, audit reconsideration, Form 1040-X) (pages 2 to 3).
- **Never submit a DATL offer and a DATC offer at the same time.** If both are sent, the Form 656 offer is returned and any initial payment is kept and applied (Form 656-L, page 4; Form 656-B, page 2).
- Mail: Brookhaven Internal Revenue Service, DATL Unit, P.O. Box 9008, Stop 681-D, Holtsville, NY 11742-9008 (Form 656-L checklist).
- Statutory support: IRC §7122(d)(3)(B) — a liability-only offer is not rejected solely because the IRS cannot locate the return, and no financial statement is required.

## 5. Low-Income Certification (Form 656, Section 1; Form 656-B, page 3)

Individuals and sole proprietors qualify if AGI from the most recently filed Form 1040, or household gross monthly income from Form 433-A (OIC) × 12, is at or below the table amount for family size and location (table in `line-by-line.md`; one person in the 48 states/DC/territories: $39,900; Alaska $49,875; Hawaii $45,900 under Rev. 4-2026).

Effects: no $205 fee, no initial payment, no monthly payments while the offer is considered; the first payment is due 30 calendar days after acceptance unless an amended offer sets another date. Not available to entities other than sole proprietors or to an offer for a deceased individual. The IRS verifies the certification. Statute: IRC §7122(c)(3) (250% of the applicable poverty level).

Ask for the AGI line of the latest filed return and the household size; compute both tests and show them.

## 6. When to route to an installment agreement instead

Route to [`../../form-9465/SKILL.md`](../../form-9465/SKILL.md) when:

- The full-pay screen in `rcp-calculation.md` shows equity (without allowances) plus remaining monthly income through the collection statute covers the balance.
- The minimum offer approaches or exceeds the balance.
- The user wants to keep paying and avoid the five-year compliance term and public inspection that come with an accepted offer (Form 656, Section 7(l), 7(v)).

Note for users already in an installment agreement: payments are not required while the offer is considered, and the agreement is reinstated if the offer is not accepted and no new debt arose (Form 656-B, page 3). But submitting an offer withdraws any **pending** installment agreement request, which is not automatically reinstated (Form 656, Section 7(p)).

## 7. How many Forms 656

| Situation | Forms 656 | Fee and payment |
|---|---|---|
| Joint debt only, both spouses offering | One joint Form 656 | One $205 fee and payment |
| Joint debt plus separate debt of either spouse (including TFRP) | Two: each spouse lists joint and their own separate debts | Each requires $205 and initial payment unless low-income |
| Joint debt, one spouse does not join | That spouse may file alone for their responsibility | Own fee and payment |
| Legally separated and living apart, or divorced | Separate offers only; no joint offer | Each pays |
| Individual debts plus business entity debts | Two packages (individual and business), separate financial statements and documents | Each requires $205 and initial payment |
| Partner wanting to settle a share of partnership debt | Not allowed; the partnership submits its own offer | Partnership pays |

Source: Form 656-B, page 4. A "business" here is a corporation, partnership, or any business operated as other than a sole proprietorship.

## 8. Facts the user should hear before deciding

- Penalties and interest keep accruing while the offer is considered (Form 656-B, page 2).
- The IRS may levy until an IRS official signs the offer as pending, and may keep levy proceeds (page 3; Form 656, Section 7(g)).
- Refunds for periods assessed before acceptance are kept and do not count toward the offer (page 1; Section 7(e)).
- The offer amount cannot come from an expected refund, money already paid, funds seized by collection, or anticipated capital or net operating loss benefits; using IRA or 401(k) money can create new tax (page 3).
- The IRS may file a Notice of Federal Tax Lien; liens are released generally within 45 days after the final payment is received and verified (Section 7(q)).
- Five years of filing and payment compliance after acceptance, with no installment agreement or new offer during that time (page 6; Section 7(l)).
- Accepted-offer details are open to public inspection for one year (Section 7(v)).
- Returned offers (for missing returns, missed periodic payments, unanswered information requests) have no appeal; rejected offers can be appealed (pages 2, 6).
