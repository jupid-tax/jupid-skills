# Who and What Counts on Schedule H

Use this file to decide whether a worker is a household employee, which tests the worker's pay counts toward, and which Forms W-2 and W-3 are owed. Sources: Publication 926 (2026) (P926); 2025 Instructions for Schedule H (ISH25) and 2026 draft (ISH26D); Publication 15 (2026) section 3; IRC §3121 and §3306 (text at law.cornell.edu).

---

## 1. Is the worker a household employee?

A household employee is someone you hire to do household work whose work you can control: not only what is done but how it is done. Full time or part time, hired directly or from an agency's list, paid hourly, daily, weekly, or by the job: none of that changes the answer (P926, "Do You Have a Household Employee?"; ISH25).

Household work is work done in or around your private home. A separate dwelling unit you maintain in an apartment house or hotel counts as a private home. Examples: babysitters, butlers, caretakers, cooks, domestic workers, drivers, health aides, housecleaning workers, housekeepers, maids, nannies, private nurses, yard workers. Services not of a household nature (private secretary, tutor, librarian) are not household work even when done in your home (P926).

Not your employee (P926; ISH25):

- A self-employed worker who controls how the work is done, usually brings their own tools, and offers services to the public (IRS example: a lawn care business with its own helpers and tools).
- A worker an agency provides when the agency controls what work is done and how.
- A worker who performs childcare for you in the worker's own home (generally).

Ask the user, for each worker: "Who decides how and when the work is done? Where is it done? Does the worker have other clients and their own tools? Did an agency place the worker, and does the agency direct the work?"

Government program boundary: a home care service recipient whose caregiver is paid through a federal, state, or local program can have a section 3504 agent (Form 2678) report and pay the taxes; if a government agency or third-party agent does so, the recipient does not file Schedule H for those wages (ISH25).

## 2. The two tests and what each one counts

| Test | Threshold | Who is counted | Where used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social Security and Medicare (line A) | Cash wages to any **one** employee of $2,800 (2025) / $3,000 (2026) or more in the year | Each employee separately; excludes spouse, child under 21, parent (unless the parent exception applies), and anyone under 18 at any time in the year (unless household work is their principal occupation; never a student) | Lines A, 1, 3, 5 |
| FUTA (line C and line 9) | Total cash wages to **all** household employees of $1,000 or more in any calendar quarter of the current or prior year | All household employees combined; excludes only spouse, child under 21, and parent (no parent exception) | Lines C, 9, 15, 20 |

Sources: line A, line C, line 9, line 15 text (SH25, SH26D); P926 Table 1 and "Wages not counted"; IRC §3121(a)(7)(B), §3121(b)(3), §3121(b)(21), §3306(a)(3), §3306(c)(2), §3306(c)(5).

Once an employee meets the line A test, Social Security and Medicare apply to all cash wages paid to that employee in the year, including the first $2,800 or $3,000 (ISH25, "$2,800 test"; P926). Below the test, none of that employee's wages are Social Security or Medicare wages (P926). The test uses wages paid in the year regardless of when earned.

### Family and age table

| Worker | Line A / Part I (Social Security, Medicare) | Line C / line 15 (FUTA) | Income tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your spouse | Excluded | Excluded | Wages are income; withholding only by agreement |
| Your child under 21 | Excluded | Excluded | Same |
| Your parent | Excluded unless the parent exception applies | Excluded (no exception) | Same |
| Anyone under 18 at any time in the year | Excluded unless household work is their principal occupation (never for a student) | Counted | Same |
| Any other household employee | Counted | Counted | Same |

Parent exception (both conditions): (1) your child or stepchild living with you is under 18 or needs an adult's personal care for at least 4 continuous weeks in the quarter, and (2) you are divorced and not remarried, widowed, or living with a spouse whose physical or mental condition prevents caring for the child for at least 4 continuous weeks in the quarter (ISH25 line A; P926; IRC §3121(b)(3)(B)).

Wages paid to a child, spouse, or parent are still subject to federal income tax even when they are excluded from Social Security, Medicare, and FUTA; withholding on household workers stays voluntary (P926, "Do You Need To Withhold Federal Income Tax?", "Wages").

## 3. Cash wages versus noncash wages

Cash wages: check, money order, cash, direct deposit, and similar. Not cash wages: the value of food, lodging, clothing, transit passes, and other noncash items. Cash given in place of those items is cash wages (P926; ISH25).

- Noncash wages are not Social Security, Medicare, or FUTA wages, but are income-tax wages unless excluded: report in W-2 box 1, not boxes 3 or 5.
- Meals provided at your home for your convenience, and lodging provided at your home for your convenience and as a condition of employment, are not wages for income tax (P926).
- Qualified parking and transit or commuter vehicle reimbursements up to $325 a month each (2025) / $340 (2026) can be excluded from Social Security and Medicare cash wages; excess is wages (ISH25; ISH26D; P926). Bicycle commuting reimbursements are wages after 2025 (P926; P.L. 119-21).
- State disability plan payments can be Social Security and Medicare wages; follow the state's notice and the line 8 adjustment (ISH25, "State Disability Payments").
- Income tax the employer pays for the employee without withholding it is wages for income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and FUTA (P926, "Paying tax without withholding").

## 4. Wage base and Additional Medicare

- Social Security: stop at $176,100 (2025) / $184,500 (2026) of an employee's cash wages; Medicare continues on all wages (ISH25; P926).
- Additional Medicare: withhold 0.9% on wages paid to an employee above $200,000 in the year, starting in the pay period that crosses $200,000; no employer share (ISH25; P926).

## 5. Withholding the employee's share

- Withhold 7.65% (6.2% + 1.45%) from each payment if you expect the employee to reach the test; you may withhold even if unsure. If the employee ends the year below the test, repay the withheld amounts and do not report them on Form W-2 (P926, "Withholding the employee's share").
- If you choose to pay the employee's share yourself, you owe the same Schedule H tax, and the amount you paid is added to the employee's W-2 box 1 (P926, "Not withholding the employee's share"; ISH25). P926 example: $100 weekly wage → $15.30 tax paid by the employer each payday; income-tax wages $107.65.
- Federal income tax: withhold only if the employee asks and gives a Form W-4 and you agree; either may end the agreement in writing. Use Pub. 15-T (P926).

## 6. EIN

You need an EIN to file Schedule H. Apply at IRS.gov/EIN, or by fax or mail with Form SS-4; international applicants may call 267-941-1099. Do not use an SSN in place of an EIN. A business owner who already has an EIN uses it (ISH25, "Do You Have an Employer Identification Number (EIN)?"; P926). Route the application to [`../../form-ss-4/SKILL.md`](../../form-ss-4/SKILL.md).

## 7. Forms W-2 and W-3

File a separate Form W-2 for each household employee to whom you paid (P926, "Form W-2"):

- Social Security and Medicare wages of $2,800 (2025) / $3,000 (2026) or more, or
- Wages from which you withheld federal income tax.

## 8. Earned income credit notice

You must notify the employee about the EIC if you agreed to withhold income tax but the tables showed no withholding. You are encouraged to notify employees whose wages were under $61,555 ($68,675 if married filing jointly) for 2025 (ISH25) or under $62,974 ($70,244) for 2026 (ISH26D). Notification methods: the official W-2 (EIC notice on the back of Copy B), a substitute W-2 with the same statement, Notice 797, or your own written statement with the same wording. If no W-2 is required, notify by February 9, 2026 (ISH25) / February 8, 2027 (ISH26D).

## 9. Legal work status

Form I-9 for each household employee hired to work regularly; keep it, do not file it (ISH25; P926). Out of scope beyond the reminder.

## 10. Questions to ask, verbatim

- "For each person you paid, what is their relationship to you, and how old were they at any time this year? Were they a student?"
- "Did the person work in your home following your instructions, or through an agency or their own business, or in their own home?"
- "How much cash did you pay each person in January to March, April to June, July to September, and October to December, this year and last year?"
- "Did anyone give you a Form W-4 and ask you to withhold income tax?"
- "Did you take 7.65% out of the pay, or pay that share yourself?"
- "Did you provide meals, lodging, or transit passes, or give cash in place of them?"
