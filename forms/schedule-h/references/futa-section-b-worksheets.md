# Schedule H FUTA: Section A, Section B, Worksheet 1, Worksheet 2

How to compute household FUTA on Schedule H Part II when the 0.6% shortcut does not hold. Built from the 2025 Instructions for Schedule H (ISH25), Part II and Worksheets 1 and 2 (pages 6–9), the 2026 draft instructions (ISH26D), and Publication 926 (2026) (P926). Statutory basis: IRC §3301 (6.0%), §3302(a)(3) (contributions paid after the return due date earn at most 90% of the credit), §3302(c) (credit cap and credit reduction), §3306(a)(3) (household $1,000 quarter test).

---

## 1. When Part II applies

Only if total cash wages to all household employees (excluding spouse, child under 21, and parent) were $1,000 or more in any calendar quarter of the current or prior year (line C or line 9). The FUTA wage base is the first $7,000 of each employee's cash wages in the year (ISH25 Part II; P926, "FUTA wages").

## 2. Contributions: what counts

Contributions are payments a state requires the employer to make to its unemployment fund. Not contributions (ISH25 Part II):

- Payments deducted or deductible from the employee's pay
- Penalties, interest, or special administrative taxes
- Voluntary contributions paid to get a lower experience rate

Ask for the state account statement and strip those items.

## 3. Section A or Section B

```
Line 10: Contributions paid to only one state, and that state is not a credit reduction state?
Line 11: All contributions for the year paid by April 15 of the next year
         (fiscal-year filers: by the return due date, not including extensions)?
Line 12: All FUTA-taxable wages also taxable for the state's unemployment tax?

All three Yes → Section A: line 15 × 0.006 = line 16.
Any No        → Section B: lines 17–24, plus Worksheet 1 and/or Worksheet 2.
```

Credit reduction states, 2025: California (0.012) and U.S. Virgin Islands (0.045) per ISH25 Worksheet 2. For 2026 the draft Worksheet 2 shows "0.0XX" for both (ISH26D); the U.S. Department of Labor potential list (Jan 15, 2026) shows California at 0.015 plus an estimated 0.038 add-on and the U.S. Virgin Islands at 0.048, but final rates come only after the November 10, 2026 deadline. Never use potential rates in a filed return; wait for the final 2026 instructions.

## 5. Section B line 17 columns

Required columns: (a), (b), (h) always; (c) and (d) if the rate is 5.4% or higher; all columns if the rate is below 5.4%. One row per state and rate period. If you skip columns that apply, you lose the credit (ISH25 line 17).

| Col | Entry |
|---|---|
| (a) | State |
| (b) | Taxable wages as defined in the state act (with a 0% rate: wages that would have been taxed) |
| (c) | Experience rate period From / To |
| (d) | Experience rate |
| (e) | (b) × 0.054 |
| (f) | (b) × (d) |
| (g) | (e) − (f); -0- if zero or less |
| (h) | Contributions paid to the state for the year **by April 15 of the next year** (fiscal-year filers: by the return due date without extensions); attach the state letter if claiming excess credits as payments |

Then: 18 = totals of (g) and (h); 19 = 18(g) + 18(h); 20 = FUTA wages (line 15 rules); 21 = 20 × 0.06; 22 = 20 × 0.054; 23 = smaller of 19 or 22 unless a worksheet applies; 24 = 21 − 23.

Column (g) is the additional credit for an experience rate below 5.4%; column (h) is the credit for contributions actually paid on time. Contributions paid after April 15 do not go in (h); they enter through Worksheet 1.

## 7. Worksheet 2 — Household Employers in a Credit Reduction State (ISH25, page 9)

| Line | Computation |
|---|---|
| 1 | Smaller of Schedule H line 19 or line 22; if Worksheet 1 was completed, its line 8 |
| 2 | Total taxable FUTA wages, Schedule H line 20 |
| 3 | Mark an "X" for every state in which you had to pay state unemployment tax. If every checked state has a zero rate, Worksheet 2 is not needed. | 4 | Total credit reduction |
| 5 | Line 1 − line 4; if zero or less, 0 → Schedule H line 23 (check the box) |

2025 rates printed on Worksheet 2: CA x 0.012; VI x 0.045; every other state, DC, and PR x 0.000.

## 9. Worked pattern: late contributions in California

Facts: one caregiver; California taxable wages $7,000 (California's 2025 taxable wage base per DOL Significant Provisions, July 2025); assigned rate 3.4%; the $238.00 owed was paid after April 15, 2026.

Line 17: (b) 7,000; (d) .034; (e) 378.00; (f) 238.00; (g) 140.00; (h) 0.00 → line 19 = 140.00. Line 21 = 420.00; line 22 = 378.00.
Worksheet 1: 378.00; 140.00; 238.00; 238.00; 238.00; 214.20; 354.20; 354.20 → Worksheet 2 line 1.
Worksheet 2: 354.20 − (7,000 × 0.012 = 84.00) = 270.20 → line 23.
Line 24 = 420.00 − 270.20 = 149.80, versus 126.00 had the state been paid by April 15, 2026.

## 10. Questions to ask, verbatim

- "Which state or states did you pay unemployment contributions to for your household employee, and on what dates?"
- "What experience rate did the state assign, for what period, and what were the state taxable wages?"
- "Does the state's statement include any penalty, interest, administrative charge, or amount taken from the employee's pay? I need to leave those out."
- "Did the state tax all of the wages that are FUTA wages? If the state does not cover a worker, tell me which one."
