# Paying Household Employment Taxes, Business-Owner Option, Stand-Alone Filing, and Corrections

Sources: Publication 926 (2026) (P926), "How Do You Make Tax Payments?", "What Forms Must You File?", "How Can You Correct Schedule H?", "What Records Must You Keep?"; 2025 Instructions for Schedule H (ISH25), "When and Where To File", "You Should Also Know", "Rules for Business Employers", "How To Correct Schedule H"; 2026 draft instructions (ISH26D); IRC §3510 and §6654 (text at law.cornell.edu).

---

## 1. No deposits; taxes ride on the income tax return

Household employment taxes reported on Schedule H are reported on a calendar-year basis, filed with the income tax return by the 15th day of the fourth month after the year ends, and no deposit requirement applies (IRC §3510(a)). For calendar-year filers: April 15, 2026 for 2025 wages; April 15, 2027 for 2026 wages. An extension of time to file the return also extends Schedule H; fiscal-year filers file with their fiscal-year return (ISH25; P926).

Pub. 15 (2026) section 14 confirms that FUTA for household employees is not deposited unless the wages are reported on Form 941, 943, or 944.

## 2. Estimated tax penalty exposure

For the estimated tax penalty, household employment taxes are treated as income tax of the employer for the year (IRC §3510(b)(1)). So line 26 counts in the "tax shown on the return" that estimated payments and withholding must cover.

Exception: the penalty does not apply to the household taxes if both (1) the employer has no federal income tax withheld from wages, pensions, or other payments, and (2) the employer's income taxes, excluding household employment taxes, would not have required estimated payments (ISH25, "Estimated Tax Penalty"; P926 caution; IRC §3510(b)(2)).

Safe harbors (IRC §6654(d)(1)(B)–(C)): required annual payment is the lesser of 90% of the current year's tax (including Schedule H) or 100% of the prior year's tax; 110% if prior-year AGI exceeded $150,000 ($75,000 married filing separately). No penalty if the tax after withholding credits is under $1,000 (IRC §6654(e)(1)). Penalty computation: [`../../form-2210/SKILL.md`](../../form-2210/SKILL.md).

## 3. Ways to pay during the year (P926, "How Do You Make Tax Payments?")

| Method | How | Timing effect |
|---|---|---|
| More withholding from the user's wages | New Form W-4 to the user's employer with an extra amount per paycheck. See [`../../form-w4/SKILL.md`](../../form-w4/SKILL.md) | Income tax withheld is deemed paid in equal parts on each installment due date unless the taxpayer shows actual dates (IRC §6654(g)(1)), so a late-year increase still counts as paid evenly |
| More withholding from a pension or annuity | New Form W-4P (or the payer's form) with an extra amount per payment | Same rule (withholding credit under §31) |
| Estimated tax payments | Form 1040-ES vouchers or electronic payment (IRS.gov/Pay). See [`../../form-1040-es/SKILL.md`](../../form-1040-es/SKILL.md) | 2026 due dates: April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027 (P926). 2025 due dates: April 15, June 16, September 15, 2025, and January 15, 2026 (ISH25) |
| Increase existing estimated payments | Raise remaining installments | Payments count when made |

ISH26D: if the 2026 Form 1040 or 1040-SR is filed by February 1, 2027 and the rest of the tax is paid, the January 15, 2027 payment is not needed. ISH25 gives the same rule for 2025 with January 31, 2026.

Pub. 505 and the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator (IRS.gov/W4app) help size the extra withholding (P926).

## 4. Business-owner option (boundary)

If the user owns a business or farm operated for profit, the household employment taxes may be paid either with the income tax (Schedule H) or with the business's employment tax deposits, reporting the household employee's Social Security, Medicare, and withheld income tax on Form 941, 944, or 943 and the FUTA on Form 940 (P926, "Payment option for business employers"). If the user chooses the business route:

- Do not file Schedule H for that employee (ISH25, "Rules for Business Employers"; P926, "Business employment tax returns").
- Deposits follow Pub. 15 section 11.
- File that employee's W-2 with the business W-2s and W-3.
- Route to [`../../form-941/SKILL.md`](../../form-941/SKILL.md) and [`../../form-940/SKILL.md`](../../form-940/SKILL.md).

Either way, wages and employment taxes for household employees are not deductible on Schedule C or Schedule F (P926 caution).

Statutory note: IRC §3510(d) says the Schedule H regime does not apply, to the extent provided in regulations, to an employer liable for employment taxes on non-domestic wages. Pub. 926 (2026) and the Schedule H instructions present the business route as the employer's choice. Follow the publications, ask the user which route they use, and refer a user who already files Form 941 and is unsure to a CPA.

Ask: "Do you want this employee on your business payroll with quarterly returns and deposits, or on Schedule H with your personal return?" Do not choose for the user.

## 5. Filing when no Form 1040 is required

File Schedule H by itself by April 15 (2026 for 2025; 2027 for 2026): complete Part IV, mail with a check or money order payable to "United States Treasury" for the total household employment taxes, and write the name, address, SSN, daytime phone, and "2025 Schedule H" (or "2026 Schedule H") on the payment. Do not send cash. Addresses are in [`../filing.md`](../filing.md). Tax-exempt household employers with no return (for example a church paying a worker who keeps a minister's home) may also file Schedule H by itself (ISH25, "When and Where To File"; P926).

Alternative when no return is required: an employer who files Form 941, 944, or 943 and Form 940 for other employees can include the household employee there (P926, "Filing options when no return is required").

## 6. Correcting a filed Schedule H

| Original filed with | How to correct | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR | Form 1040-X with a corrected Schedule H. See [`../../form-1040-x/SKILL.md`](../../form-1040-x/SKILL.md) | ISH25; P926 |
| Form 1040-SS | A new (corrected) Form 1040-SS with a corrected Schedule H | ISH25; P926 |
| Form 1041 | An "Amended" Form 1041 with a corrected Schedule H | ISH25; P926 |
| Stand-alone | Another stand-alone Schedule H with "CORRECTED" and the discovery date in bold in the top margin, plus a statement explaining the correction; for an overpayment also write "ADJUSTED" or "REFUND" | P926 |

Timing and money (P926, "How Can You Correct Schedule H?"):

- Underpayment: file the corrected Schedule H no later than the due date of the next return after discovery (generally April 15 of the following year) and pay the underpayment by then; filed and paid on time, no interest or failure-to-pay or estimated tax penalty on the Social Security and Medicare correction; underreported FUTA is subject to interest.
- Overpayment: within the refund limitation period (generally 3 years from filing or 2 years from payment, whichever is later). Adjust (no interest) or claim a refund; within 90 days of the limitation period, only a refund. FUTA overpayments cannot be adjusted.
- Repayment or consent: before adjusting overreported Social Security and Medicare, repay or reimburse the employee's overcollected share; before a refund claim, repay or obtain the employee's written consent, and include a statement saying so.
- Additional Medicare Tax: prior-year errors can be corrected only for administrative errors; under-withholding must be recovered from the employee by the end of the calendar year in which it occurred.
- File Form W-2 or W-2c to match the correction.

## 7. Records

Keep Schedule H and related Forms W-2, W-3, and W-4 for at least 4 years after the due date of Schedule H or the date the taxes were paid, whichever is later. Each payday, record the dates and amounts of cash and noncash wages, employee Social Security and Medicare withheld or paid by you, federal income tax withheld, and state employment taxes withheld (ISH25, "What Records To Keep"; P926).

## 8. Related credit (pointer only)

If the household employee cares for a dependent under 13 or a spouse or dependent not able to care for themselves so the user can work, the user's share of federal and state employment taxes counts with the wages as qualifying expenses for the child and dependent care credit (P926, "Can You Claim a Credit for Child and Dependent Care Expenses?"; Pub. 503). Out of scope for this skill.
