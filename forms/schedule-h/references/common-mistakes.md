# Common Schedule H Mistakes

Errors to check before declaring a Schedule H draft ready. Each entry: symptom, rule with authority, fix. Sources: 2025 Schedule H and Instructions (SH25, ISH25), 2026 drafts (SH26D, ISH26D), Publication 926 (2026) (P926), IRC sections as cited.

---

## 1. Taxing only the wages above the threshold

**Symptom.** Line 1 = $44,720 − $2,800.

**Rule.** Once an employee meets the test, Social Security and Medicare apply to all cash wages paid that year, including the first $2,800 (2025) / $3,000 (2026) (ISH25, "$2,800 test"; P926).

**Fix.** Put every cash dollar paid to that employee on lines 1 and 3 (line 1 capped at the wage base).

## 2. Under-18 student on line 1 but not on line 15

**Symptom.** A 16-year-old sitter's pay appears in Part I; line 15 is blank.

**Rule.** Wages of anyone under 18 at any time in the year are left out of line A unless household work is their principal occupation, which is never the case for a student (ISH25 line A; P926; IRC §3121(b)(21)). FUTA has no age exclusion; line 15 includes them (ISH25 line 15).

**Fix.** Remove from Part I; include in line C, line 9, and line 15.

## 3. Parent exception applied to FUTA

**Symptom.** A widowed parent pays their mother for childcare; her wages are on line 15.

**Rule.** The parent exception counts a parent's wages for Social Security and Medicare only (ISH25 line A; IRC §3121(b)(3)(B)). Line C, line 9, and line 15 always exclude a parent (SH25 lines C and 9; ISH25 line 15; IRC §3306(c)(5)).

**Fix.** Keep the parent on line 1/3 if the exception applies; remove from line 15 and from the quarter test.

## 4. Forgetting the prior-year quarter

**Symptom.** Line C answered No because no 2026 quarter reached $1,000.

**Rule.** The test looks at any calendar quarter of the current or prior year (SH26D line C; IRC §3306(a)(3)).

**Fix.** Ask for the prior year's quarterly totals; if any quarter reached $1,000, FUTA applies to the current year's wages.

## 5. Counting noncash pay as cash wages

**Symptom.** The value of a live-in nanny's room and board is on lines 1, 3, and 15.

**Rule.** Cash wages exclude food, lodging, clothing, transit passes, and other noncash items; noncash wages are reported in W-2 box 1 only (ISH25, "Cash wages"; P926).

**Fix.** Remove noncash items from Schedule H; keep taxable noncash wages in W-2 box 1. Ask whether any cash was paid in place of those items.

## 6. Section A used after paying the state late

**Symptom.** Line 11 answered Yes although a state payment was made after April 15.

**Rule.** Line 11 asks whether all contributions for the year were paid by April 15 of the next year (fiscal-year filers: by the return due date without extensions). Late contributions earn only 90% of the credit (ISH25 Section B caution; IRC §3302(a)(3)).

**Fix.** Answer No, complete Section B and Worksheet 1.

## 7. Line 10 answered Yes in a credit reduction state

**Symptom.** A California household employer uses Section A and reports $42.

**Rule.** "If you paid contributions to a credit reduction state, see instructions and check 'No'" (SH25 line 10). For 2025, California's reduction rate is 0.012 and the U.S. Virgin Islands' is 0.045 (ISH25 Worksheet 2).

**Fix.** Section B with Worksheet 2. One employee at $7,000 in California, paid on time, owes $126, not $42.

## 8. W-2 boxes 3–6 filled for wages under the test

**Symptom.** An employee paid $2,100 had income tax withheld; the W-2 shows Social Security and Medicare wages.

**Rule.** When wages are not subject to Social Security and Medicare, complete only boxes 1 and 2; the SSA rejects a W-2 with boxes 3–6 filled below the threshold (ISH25, "Who Needs To File Form W-2 and Form W-3?").

**Fix.** Clear boxes 3–6; refund any Social Security and Medicare withheld (P926).

## 9. Employer paid the employee's share but box 1 was not grossed up

**Symptom.** Box 1 equals box 3 although the user never withheld the 7.65%.

**Rule.** The employee share paid by the employer is added to box 1 (boxes 3 + 4 + 6, or 4 + 5 + 6 if box 5 > box 3), but is not Social Security, Medicare, or FUTA wages (ISH25, "Employee's portion of taxes paid by employer"; P926).

**Fix.** Recompute box 1; leave boxes 3 and 5 and Schedule H lines 1, 3, and 15 unchanged.

## 10. Wrong Schedule 2 line for the year

**Symptom.** A 2026 Schedule H total is carried to Schedule 2 line 9.

**Rule.** Line 9 on the 2025 Schedule 2; line 17a on the 2026 draft Schedule 2 (SH25 line 27; SH26D line 27; 2026 draft Schedule 2).

**Fix.** Use the line printed on the final Schedule H line 27 for the year.

## 11. SSN used in the EIN box

**Symptom.** EIN field holds the employer's SSN.

**Rule.** An EIN is required to file Schedule H; do not use an SSN in place of an EIN; if applied for, write "Applied For" and the date (ISH25, EIN sections).

**Fix.** Get an EIN ([`../../form-ss-4/SKILL.md`](../../form-ss-4/SKILL.md)).

## 12. Agency or self-employed workers on Schedule H

**Symptom.** A cleaning company's crew member or an independent lawn service is listed as an employee.

**Rule.** Workers the agency controls, and self-employed workers who control how the work is done, are not your employees; a worker caring for your child in the worker's own home generally is not your employee (ISH25; P926).

**Fix.** Remove them. No Schedule H, no W-2.

## 13. Household wages deducted on Schedule C

**Symptom.** A home-based business owner deducts the nanny's pay and employer taxes as business wages.

**Rule.** The Schedule C and F deduction for wages and employment taxes applies only to business and farm employees, not household employees (P926 caution).

**Fix.** Remove the deduction.

## 14. Reporting the same employee on Schedule H and Form 941

**Symptom.** A business owner adds the nanny to the business payroll and also files Schedule H.

**Rule.** Don't use Schedule H if you chose to report household employment taxes with your other employees on Form 941, 943, or 944 (ISH25, "Rules for Business Employers"; P926).

**Fix.** Keep one route.

## 15. Surprise balance due in April

**Symptom.** The user's own withholding covered only income tax; Schedule H adds thousands at filing.

**Rule.** Household employment taxes are treated as income tax for the estimated tax penalty (IRC §3510(b)), so they must be covered by withholding or estimated payments unless the §3510(b)(2) exception applies (ISH25, "Estimated Tax Penalty").

**Fix.** Increase W-4 or W-4P withholding (deemed paid evenly under IRC §6654(g)) or make Form 1040-ES payments; check Form 2210.
