# Example: Grandmother Caregiver, Teenage Sitter, and an Agency Cleaner (Tax Year 2026, Planning Draft)

A widowed parent pays her own mother for childcare, hires a 17-year-old student for the summer, and uses a cleaning company. The example shows how the parent exception, the under-18 rule, and the agency boundary send each worker to a different part of Schedule H. It was prepared in October 2026 as a planning draft on the **2026 draft Schedule H** (Created 5/8/26) and **2026 draft instructions** (dated Aug 19, 2026). Re-check every line on the final 2026 form; fourth-quarter amounts are projections the agent must confirm in January 2027.

## The household

- **Employer:** Mei Tan, Tucson, Arizona. Widowed in 2023; not remarried. Files Form 1040. Daughter Lily, age 4, lives with her. Mei is employed and has income tax withheld from her salary.
- **Grace Tan (Mei's mother), 66.** Cares for Lily in Mei's home four days a week, $260 a week, 40 paid weeks in 2026 (projected) = $10,400. Mei withholds Grace's 7.65% ($16.12 Social Security + $3.77 Medicare per week). Grace did not ask for income tax withholding.
- **Ava Brooks, 17,** high-school student, babysits in Mei's home in the summer: $585 in June (Q2) and $1,755 in July and August (Q3) = $2,340. No withholding.
- **Cleaner from a cleaning company.** The company assigns the worker, sets the tasks and methods, and bills Mei. Not Mei's employee (Pub. 926 (2026): an agency that controls what work is done and how).
- **2025:** no household employees; Grace started in January 2026. (Parent wages would not count toward the $1,000 quarter test in any case.)
- **Arizona:** Mei confirmed with the state agency that Ava's wages are covered and will pay the assessed contributions by April 15, 2027. Estimate used: $2,340 × 2.00% = $46.80, using Arizona's 2.00% new-employer base rate (DOL, Significant Provisions of State UI Laws, July 2026). Replace with the state's actual assessment. Arizona is not on the DOL potential 2026 credit reduction list (California and U.S. Virgin Islands only); confirm on the final 2026 Worksheet 2.
- **EIN:** Mei has none. She must apply before filing ([`../../form-ss-4/SKILL.md`](../../form-ss-4/SKILL.md)); until it arrives, the EIN box reads "Applied For" and the date.

## Classification

| Worker | Household employee? | Line A / Part I | Line C / line 15 | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grace (parent) | Yes | Counted: $10,400 | Excluded | Parent exception: Lily is under 18 and lives with Mei, and Mei is a widow (2026 draft instructions, line A; IRC §3121(b)(3)(B)). FUTA excludes a parent with no exception (line C and line 15 text; IRC §3306(c)(5)) |
| Ava (17, student) | Yes | Excluded | Counted: $2,340 | Under 18 at some time in the year and a student, so household work is not her principal occupation (Pub. 926 (2026); IRC §3121(b)(21)). FUTA has no age exclusion |
| Agency cleaner | No | — | — | Agency controls the work |

Quarter test (excluding Grace): Q2 $585; Q3 $1,755 ≥ $1,000 → line C and line 9 are Yes.

## Paying during the year

Mei has wage withholding, so the IRC §3510(b)(2) exception does not apply and the $1,605.24 counts toward her 2026 estimated tax requirement (IRC §3510(b)(1)). In October 2026 the agent proposed a new Form W-4 adding $270 to each of her six remaining biweekly paychecks: 6 × $270 = $1,620 ≥ $1,605.24. Withholding is treated as paid evenly through the year (IRC §6654(g)(1)), so a late-year change still counts for every installment. Alternatively, a Form 1040-ES payment by January 15, 2027 (Pub. 926 (2026)). See [`../../form-w4/SKILL.md`](../../form-w4/SKILL.md) and [`../../form-1040-es/SKILL.md`](../../form-1040-es/SKILL.md).

Mei's own cost beyond wages: employer share 7.65% × $10,400 = $795.60, FUTA $14.04, Arizona about $46.80.

## Why each non-obvious choice

**Grace counts on line A only because Mei is widowed.** If Mei were married to a spouse able to care for Lily, Grace's wages would be excluded from line A too, and with Ava under 18 line A would be No; line C would still be Yes because of Ava, so Schedule H would carry only the FUTA on Ava's wages.

**Ava is FUTA-only.** The under-18 student rule removes her from Social Security and Medicare but not from FUTA; her $1,755 quarter alone triggers line C.

**Line 15 is $2,340, not $7,000.** Ava earned less than $7,000, and Grace is excluded from FUTA wages.

**The agency cleaner is ignored.** The agency, not Mei, controls the work.
