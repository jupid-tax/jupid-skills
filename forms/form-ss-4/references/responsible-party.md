# Responsible Party (Form SS-4, Lines 7a and 7b)

Who goes on lines 7a and 7b, what number goes on 7b, and what happens later when the person changes. Sources: Instructions for Form SS-4 (Rev. December 2025), "Lines 7a–7b" and "Reminders"; IRS page [Responsible parties and nominees](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/responsible-parties-and-nominees) (reviewed 22-Aug-2026); IRS page [Employer identification number](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/employer-identification-number) (reviewed 17-Jul-2026); Regulations section 301.6109-1(d)(2)(ii).

## Definition

The instructions define the responsible party as "the person who ultimately owns or controls the entity or who exercises ultimate effective control over the entity." That person "should have a level of control over, or entitlement to, the funds or assets in the entity that, as a practical matter, enables the person, directly or indirectly, to control, manage, or direct the entity and the disposition of its funds and assets."

Unless the applicant is a government entity, the responsible party must be an individual (a natural person), not an entity.

## Who it usually is, by entity type

| Entity | Responsible party | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Corporation | Principal officer | Responsible parties page |
| Partnership | General partner | Responsible parties page |
| Publicly traded or SEC-registered corporation | Principal officer | Instructions, Lines 7a–7b |
| Publicly traded or SEC-registered partnership | General partner; if the general partner is a corporation, that corporation's principal officer | Instructions, Lines 7a–7b |
| Tax-exempt organization | Generally the "principal officer" as defined in the Form 990 instructions | Instructions, Lines 7a–7b |
| Government entity | Agency or agency representative who can legally bind the entity (may enter an EIN on 7b) | Instructions, Lines 7a–7b |
| Trust | Grantor, owner, or trustor | Instructions, Lines 7a–7b |
| Decedent estate | Executor, administrator, personal representative, or other fiduciary | Instructions, Lines 7a–7b |
| Single-member LLC | The individual who owns and controls it (ask; if an entity owns the LLC, trace to the individual who controls that owner) | Definition above |
| Multi-member LLC | The member or manager the members designate who controls the entity and its funds; ask which person the IRS should recognize | Responsible parties page ("If you have more than 1 responsible party, list the person you want the IRS to recognize") |

## Who is not a responsible party

- **A nominee.** A nominee is someone given limited authority to act for the entity during formation, with little or no control over its assets. Nominees can't apply for an EIN and shouldn't be listed on Form SS-4. If the entity used a nominee for its state formation, identify the real responsible party before applying (Responsible parties page; Employer identification number page: "Nominees are not authorized to apply for an EIN").
- **A person who funds the entity or is entitled to its property but is not authorized to control, manage, or direct it**, for example a minor child beneficiary (Responsible parties page).
- **Another company**, a registered agent, or a formation service (follows from the "must be an individual" rule and the nominee rule).

If a nominee was already listed on an earlier application, correct it on Form 8822-B (Responsible parties page).

## What goes on line 7b

| Situation | Line 7b entry | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Responsible party has an SSN | SSN | Instructions, Lines 7a–7b |
| Responsible party has an ITIN | ITIN | Instructions, Lines 7a–7b; Line 9a ("enter the ITIN in place of a requested SSN") |
| Applicant is a government entity | EIN may be entered | Instructions, Lines 7a–7b |
| Responsible party has no SSN or ITIN and is ineligible to obtain one | "foreign" or "N/A" | Instructions, Lines 7a–7b TIP |

An entry on 7b is required. Never type a number the user has not provided; never reuse a number from another person.

### Consequence of "foreign" or "N/A" on 7b

The IRS online application requires the responsible party's SSN or ITIN (IRS [Get an EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number) page, "Who can use this tool"). An applicant whose responsible party has neither applies by telephone (only if the applicant has no legal residence, principal place of business, or principal office or agency in the U.S. or U.S. territories), by fax, or by mail. See `../filing.md`.

Whether the person should get an ITIN is a separate question about the person's own filing obligations, not an SS-4 requirement. If the person will need to file a U.S. return (for example Form 1040-NR for effectively connected income), route to `../../form-w7/SKILL.md`.

## One EIN per responsible party per day

"EIN issuances are limited to one per responsible party, per day." For trusts, the limit applies to the grantor, owner, or trustor; for estates, to the decedent (decedent estate) or debtor (bankruptcy estate). It applies to every channel: online, telephone, fax, and mail (Instructions, General Instructions caution; Get an EIN page, "Daily limit").

Agent rule: if the same person is the responsible party for two new entities, schedule the second application for a later day. If an online session fails, do not retry repeatedly the same day without first confirming whether an EIN was issued (call 800-829-4933 to verify a number, Instructions, "Apply by mail").

## Questions to ask the user

1. "Who ultimately owns or controls the entity and decides how its money is used?"
2. "Is that person an individual? If an entity owns this one, who controls that owner?"
3. "Did a formation service or attorney act only as an organizer or nominee? If so, they are not the responsible party."
4. "Does the person have a U.S. Social Security number or an ITIN?" (Do not ask for the number itself until submission.)
5. For multi-owner entities: "Which person do you want the IRS to recognize as the responsible party?"

## Later changes: Form 8822-B within 60 days

Any entity with an EIN must report a change in its responsible party within 60 days of the change on Form 8822-B (Instructions, Lines 7a–7b caution and "Reminders"; Regulations section 301.6109-1(d)(2)(ii); About Form SS-4 page). The same form reports mailing address and location changes. If no confirmation letter arrives within 60 days, mail a copy of the Form 8822-B marked "Second Request" (Responsible parties page).

Worked date: a responsible-party change effective March 12, 2027 must be reported by May 11, 2027 (60 days later).

A change of responsible party does not require a new EIN (Employer identification number page: "You don't need a new EIN to change your business name, address or responsible party").
